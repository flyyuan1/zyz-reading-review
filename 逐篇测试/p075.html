Jellyfish – The Dominant Species

水母：占据主导的物种

AJellyfish have become the curse of beach holidays, permeating every ocean on the globe, thriving in the Arctic and the tropics. In an ever-changing world where other species struggle to endure, jellyfish populations are on the rise. To the untrained eye, these creatures drift aimlessly on the oceans’ currents and appear benign. In addition, they lack sharp claws, piercing teeth or even a brain. Despite this, they are armed with an amazing arsenal of weapons, especially the stinging power of their tentacles. As a result, jellyfish are among the most-feared, least-understood creatures in the seas.

水母已经成为海滨度假的祸患。它们遍布全球每一片海洋，从北极到热带都能繁盛生长。在这个不断变化、其他物种艰难求生的世界里，水母数量却在上升。 在非专业人士看来，这些生物随洋流漫无目的地漂浮，似乎并无危害。况且，它们既没有锋利的爪子、尖锐的牙齿，甚至连大脑都没有。尽管如此，它们却配备了一整套惊人的武器，尤其是触手的蜇刺能力。因此，水母既是海洋中最令人畏惧的生物之一，也是最不为人了解的生物之一。

BAccording to Dr Monty Graham, a jellyfish scientist at the University of South Alabama, US, ‘Jellyfish are a pretty good group of animals to track coastal ecosystems. When you start to see jellyfish numbers grow, that usually indicates a stressed system.’ While populations appear to be down this year, Dr Graham sees ‘a statistically solid increase’ over the longer term. This increase first gained attention in the 1980s when a huge number of jellyfish, Atlantic Ocean natives named Mnemiopsis leidyi, devastated the Black Sea, an ecosystem already weakened by overfishing of anchovies. Scientists believe that this species of jellyfish came in on the bottom of a ship and then rapidly multiplied, feeding on anchovy eggs and the plankton that young fish rely on.

美国南阿拉巴马大学（University of South Alabama）水母科学家 Monty Graham 博士说：“水母是追踪沿海生态系统状况的一类很好的动物。当你开始看到水母数量增加时，通常说明这个系统正承受压力。”虽然今年水母数量似乎有所下降，但从更长时期来看，Graham 博士看到了“统计学上可靠的增长”。 这种增长最早在20世纪80年代引起关注。当时，大量名为 Mnemiopsis leidyi、原产大西洋的栉水母重创了黑海，而过度捕捞鳀鱼（anchovies）早已削弱了当地生态系统。科学家认为，这种栉水母随船底进入黑海，随后迅速繁殖，以鳀鱼卵和幼鱼赖以为生的浮游生物为食。

CDense jellyfish aggregations can be a natural feature of healthy ocean ecosystems, but a clear picture is now emerging of more severe and frequent jellyfish outbreaks worldwide. Dr Anthony Richardson, from the University of Queensland, Australia, explains that once jellyfish gain a foothold, if conditions are right, they can establish a massive population at the expense of other ocean life. The problem is that parts of the ocean might switch from being dominated by fish to being dominated by jellyfish.

水母密集聚集本来可以是健康海洋生态系统的自然特征，但如今一幅清晰的图景正在显现：世界各地水母暴发的严重程度和频率都在上升。澳大利亚昆士兰大学（University of Queensland）的 Anthony Richardson 博士解释说，一旦水母站稳脚跟，只要条件适宜，它们就能以牺牲其他海洋生物为代价，建立起庞大种群。问题在于，部分海域可能从由鱼类占主导，转变为由水母占主导。

DA study done by Richardson and his colleagues explores the causes behind jellyfish infestation, and the need for swift, decisive action to stem the jellyfish takeover. Jellyfish outbreaks are linked directly to human actions, including overfishing, the input of fertilizer and sewage into the ocean, and climate change. Overfishing has removed fish from marine ecosystems at astounding rates. According to Richardson, this has made it possible for jellyfish to take their place. ‘This is because small fish appear to keep jellyfish in check by predation (on jellyfish when they are very small) and competition (when feeding). So, once we remove fish, jellyfish can proliferate.’ Eutrophication is another human-caused change in the ocean that has likely contributed to jellyfish explosions. Eutrophication is an increase in nitrogen and phosphorus in the ocean, largely caused by fertilizer and waste run-off. This leads to algae blooms, which lower oxygen in the marine ecosystem, creating so-called ‘dead zones’, which have been increasing dramatically around the world. According to Richardson, these low-oxygen waters give jellyfish the advantage. ‘Fish avoid low-oxygen waters but jellyfish, having lower oxygen demands, not only survive but can thrive in these conditions as there is less predation and competition from fish.’

Richardson 及其同事开展的一项研究，探讨了水母泛滥背后的原因，以及迅速采取果断行动遏制水母接管海洋的必要性。水母暴发与人类活动直接相关，包括过度捕捞、化肥和污水排入海洋，以及气候变化。 过度捕捞正以惊人的速度把鱼类从海洋生态系统中清除。Richardson 说，这使水母有可能取代鱼类的位置。“这是因为小鱼似乎会通过捕食和竞争来抑制水母：水母很小时，小鱼会捕食它们；进食时，两者会相互竞争。所以，一旦我们把鱼类清除，水母就会大量繁殖。” 富营养化（Eutrophication）是人类造成的另一种海洋变化，很可能也助长了水母数量激增。富营养化是指海洋中氮和磷增加，主要由化肥和废弃物随径流入海所致。它会造成藻华，降低海洋生态系统中的含氧量，形成所谓的“死亡区”；世界各地的这类区域正在急剧增加。Richardson 说，这些低氧水域让水母占据优势。“鱼类会避开低氧水域，水母所需氧气较少，却不仅能够存活，还能在这种环境中繁盛，因为来自鱼类的捕食和竞争都减少了。”

EFurthermore, Richardson and his colleagues speculate that climate change may expand the traditional geographical range of jellyfish. ‘As water warms, tropical species are moving towards the poles. Many venomous jellyfish species are tropical and could move into more densely populated subtropical and temperate regions.’

此外，Richardson 及其同事推测，气候变化可能扩大水母传统的地理分布范围。“随着海水升温，热带物种正在向两极迁移。许多有毒水母是热带物种，可能会进入人口更为稠密的亚热带和温带地区。”

FOnce jellyfish appear en masse in an ecosystem, they can make it very difficult for fish to stage a comeback. By feeding on fish, the jellyfish successfully prevent fish from returning to their normal population numbers, says Richardson. ‘One can thus think of two alternate states with each being stable: one dominated by fish and the other by jellyfish. Unfortunately, where there is a jellyfish-dominated state then this does not support the nutritional needs of other fish, marine mammals, and seabirds.’ In other words, an ecosystem that loses fish also loses the species that depend on fish for survival. This state has been defined as a ‘monoculture of jellyfish’, an apt analogy since the situation shares similarities with other monocultures. When the rich biodiversity of tropical forests is replaced by plantations growing a single species of tree, an area of rich variety becomes a desert in terms of biodiversity, as do ocean ecosystems when jellyfish become the dominant species. One result of large jellyfish populations is the economic effect it has had on the fishing industry. In the Gulf of Mexico, shrimp fishermen are struggling with a jellyfish boom that fills nets, causing them to break and resulting in millions of dollars in losses.

水母一旦在生态系统中大量出现，就会让鱼类很难恢复。Richardson 说，水母以鱼为食，从而有效阻止鱼类数量恢复到正常水平。“因此，我们可以设想两种彼此替代、各自稳定的状态：一种由鱼类主导，另一种由水母主导。遗憾的是，水母主导的状态无法满足其他鱼类、海洋哺乳动物和海鸟的营养需求。”换言之，一个失去鱼类的生态系统，也会失去依赖鱼类生存的物种。 这种状态被定义为“水母单一群落”（monoculture of jellyfish）。这个类比十分贴切，因为它与其他单一群落有相似之处。当生物多样性丰富的热带森林被只种植单一树种的种植园取代时，一个物种丰富的地区便会沦为生物多样性意义上的荒漠；水母成为主导物种时，海洋生态系统也会如此。 水母种群庞大造成的一个后果，是给渔业带来经济影响。在墨西哥湾，捕虾的渔民正为水母激增而苦恼：水母塞满渔网，导致渔网破裂，造成数百万 dollars 的损失。

GExperts say that a greater understanding of jellyfish, including their ideal water temperature and feeding habits, is necessary to determine with certainty what is causing the recent massive invasion and to come up with ways to combat it. Due to the difficulty of turning ecosystems around once jellyfish have become dominant, Richardson and his colleagues propose focusing on ‘prevention rather than cure’. They recommend a halt to overfishing small fish that are vital to keeping jellyfish in check, reducing the amount of fertilizer and sewage running off into the oceans, and finally, if possible, confronting climate change.

专家表示，要确定近期水母大规模入侵的确切原因并想出应对方法，就必须更加了解水母，包括它们最适宜的水温和进食习性。 水母一旦占据主导地位，生态系统就很难扭转，因此 Richardson 及其同事主张把重点放在“预防而非治疗”上。他们建议停止过度捕捞那些对抑制水母至关重要的小鱼，减少流入海洋的化肥和污水，最后还要在可能的情况下应对气候变化。
