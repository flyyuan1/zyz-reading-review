Herbal Medicines

草药

The popularity of herbs and plants as medicines could be good for New Zealand

草药和植物作为药物受到欢迎，这可能给新西兰带来好处

AThere is an age-old practice of harvesting plants and herbs in their natural environment for use as medicines. And today there is huge potential for New Zealand to develop a herb industry based on the excellent growing conditions and expertise there, according to Phil Rasmussen, a pharmacist and medical herbalist. Take arnica, for example, a popular pharmaceutical herb used to treat bruises and joint problems. Traditionally collected by Romany communities in Europe, it is now in high demand worldwide as interest develops in the capabilities of the small alpine plant. A 2008 report for the Plant and Food Research organisation concluded that New Zealand has a good opportunity to cultivate arnica flowers and roots for the international markets. However, the initiative was stopped in its tracks when the government abruptly halted funding for the research programme. It was an unjustified move, according to agronomist Malcolm Douglas, based on a number of inaccurate views about weed invasion.

在自然环境中采集植物和草药用作药物，是一种由来已久的做法。药剂师兼医学草药师 Phil Rasmussen 表示，凭借优越的种植条件和当地专业知识，如今新西兰开发草药产业的潜力巨大。以常用药用植物山金车（arnica）为例，它可用于治疗瘀伤和关节问题。传统上，这种植物由欧洲的罗姆人（Romany）群体采集；随着人们越来越关注这种小型高山植物的功效，如今全球需求旺盛。2008年一份为植物与食品研究机构（Plant and Food Research）撰写的报告认为，新西兰很有机会种植山金车，采收其花和根以供应国际市场。然而，政府突然停止为研究计划提供资金，这项行动随即夭折。农学家 Malcolm Douglas 认为，这一决定缺乏依据，是建立在对杂草入侵的一些错误看法之上。

BThe history of herbal medicines in New Zealand has long featured disagreements of this sort. For the early Maori people – the original inhabitants of New Zealand – the forest was well stocked with edible plants that were an obvious source of nutrition, but that were taken for the relief of pain as well. While the earliest European doctors in New Zealand relied on imported dried herbs, many were keen to include native plants in their practices: in his book published in 1891, herbalist James Neil described manuka and koromiko as among New Zealand’s most valuable herbs. Despite its popularity among some, however, herbalism continued to have its detractors. In 1907 Neil, president of the then very young New Zealand Association of Medical Herbalists (NZAMH), petitioned parliament for legal status for herbalists, but he was unsuccessful. The Evening Post newspaper perhaps summed up the opposing position by claiming that herbalism ‘is obsolete’.

新西兰草药史长期伴随着这类分歧。对早期的毛利人（Maori）——新西兰原住民——来说，森林里有丰富的可食用植物，显然是营养来源，同时也被用来缓解疼痛。新西兰最早的欧洲医生依靠进口干燥草药，但许多人很愿意把本土植物纳入医疗实践。草药师 James Neil 在1891年出版的著作中，把 manuka 和 koromiko 列为新西兰最有价值的草药之一。不过，尽管草药疗法受到一些人欢迎，反对者仍一直存在。1907年，当时成立不久的新西兰医学草药师协会（New Zealand Association of Medical Herbalists，NZAMH）主席 Neil 向议会请愿，要求给予草药师合法地位，但没有成功。《晚邮报》（The Evening Post）或许概括了反对方立场，声称草药疗法“已经过时”。

CIn fact, traditional Maori medicine faced a more determined challenge. In 1908 the government passed a controversial law that had the effect of restricting traditional herbal practice, or pushing it underground. Then for a brief period in the mid-20th century, herbal medicine was largely ignored, shunned by the majority of the medical profession, and absent from medical school curricula. But a centuries-old tradition was not going to disappear so easily, and in the 1980s the NZAMH was revived, and it became possible to complete a course in herbal medicine at several polytechnics around the country. However, the problem remained of how to connect the culture of plant-based medicine with conventional scientific thinking in order to promote herbal use within the general population. For many years, the limited evidence there was that herbal medicines worked was undermined by lack of interest among general practitioners and within the scientific community.

事实上，毛利传统医学遭到了更为强硬的挑战。1908年，政府通过一项有争议的法律，实际效果是限制传统草药实践，或迫使其转入地下。随后在20世纪中期的一段短暂时期，草药医学基本无人问津，受到大多数医学界人士排斥，也没有进入医学院课程。然而，延续数百年的传统不会轻易消失。20世纪80年代，NZAMH 恢复活动，人们也可以在全国数所理工学院完成草药医学课程。不过，如何把植物药文化与主流科学思维联系起来，从而推动草药在普通人群中的使用，仍是一个问题。多年来，能够证明草药有效的证据本就有限，又因全科医生和科学界缺乏兴趣而进一步受到削弱。

DToday, the ideas of herbalists in the 1980s – eat a varied diet, include lots of fresh fruit and vegetables, recognise stress – are completely accepted by the medical establishment and widely practised in society as a whole. Natural health products are big sellers in pharmacies, and global pharmaceutical companies are buying up natural supplement brands or developing their own. What’s more, according to Rasmussen, robust clinical trials show that herbal medicine is generally safe – safer than most drugs. ‘It doesn’t do everything,’ he says, ‘but there’s a lot it does do, particularly in terms of preventative health care.’ One significant issue for today’s export-oriented herbal medicine producers is the question of how to guarantee standards for consumers in different countries. In other words, there is the need for some form of globally recognised system of documentation. Rasmussen’s range of extracts are produced under the Good Manufacturing Practice (GMP) scheme, which is respected around the world. So too is the range produced by Sandra Clair, who says that such assurance is expensive but necessary if you want to export herbal remedies to the rest of the world. Moves to launch a joint Australia–New Zealand agency to regulate herbal remedies under a single, streamlined licensing process have recently been revived, after being abandoned in 2007 through lack of support in parliament.

如今，20世纪80年代草药师提出的观念——饮食多样化、多吃新鲜水果和蔬菜、认识压力的影响——已经被医学界完全接受，也在整个社会广泛实践。天然健康产品在药店十分畅销，全球制药公司正在收购天然补充剂品牌，或开发自己的品牌。此外，Rasmussen 表示，严格的临床试验显示，草药通常是安全的，甚至比大多数药物更安全。他说：“它并非无所不能，但确实有许多作用，尤其是在预防性保健方面。”对如今面向出口的草药生产商来说，一个重要问题是如何确保产品达到标准，让不同国家的消费者放心。换言之，需要某种获得全球认可的文件记录体系。Rasmussen 的系列提取物依照良好生产规范（Good Manufacturing Practice，GMP）体系生产，该体系在全球受到认可。Sandra Clair 的系列产品也是如此。她表示，这类质量保证费用高昂，却是向世界其他地区出口草药所必需的。此前因议会支持不足而于2007年放弃的一项计划，近来再次启动；该计划拟成立澳大利亚与新西兰联合机构，通过一套统一、精简的许可流程监管草药。

EThere are those in the industry who support further regulation still. According to Rasmussen, some herbs should require a prescription from a suitably qualified medical herbalist or doctor. For example, consumers shouldn’t be able to go to their local supermarket and buy St John’s Wort, according to Rasmussen, because it can interact in harmful ways with at least ten pharmaceutical drugs. Isla Burgess, who is part of the International Research Group for the Conservation of Medicinal Plants, agrees that tighter regulation is necessary, but for a different reason. ‘More than 400,000 tonnes of medicinal and aromatic plants are traded in the world each year,’ says Burgess. ‘The great majority of these are harvested from the wild, so they each have an impact on their local ecosystem.’ She gives the example of elm trees in the USA, ringbarked and stripped for the growing market for a product called ‘slippery elm’: this endangered tree should be protected. But others argue that more regulation would be prohibitively expensive for all but the largest manufacturers – a change, cautions Clair, that would probably put some local companies out of business. So, for the time being at least, it seems that the topic of herbal medicines will continue to provoke debate.

业内还有人支持进一步加强监管。Rasmussen 认为，一些草药应当凭具备相应资质的医学草药师或医生所开处方购买。例如，Rasmussen 认为消费者不应能在当地超市买到圣约翰草（St John’s Wort），因为它会与至少十种药物发生有害相互作用。国际药用植物保护研究小组（International Research Group for the Conservation of Medicinal Plants）成员 Isla Burgess 也认为有必要加强监管，但理由不同。Burgess 说：“全球每年交易的药用和芳香植物超过40万吨。其中绝大多数采自野外，因此每一种都会影响当地生态系统。”她以美国境内的榆树为例：为满足一种名为“滑榆”（slippery elm）的产品不断增长的市场需求，人们对榆树进行环状剥皮，剥取树皮；这种濒危树木应当受到保护。但另一些人指出，更严格的监管费用会高得令人无法承受，只有最大的制造商例外；Clair 警告说，这项变化很可能让一些本地企业倒闭。因此，至少在目前看来，草药这一话题仍将继续引发争论。
