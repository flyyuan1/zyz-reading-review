Seeing in the Sea

海中视觉

Beneath the waves, ocean animals with bizarre and exotic ways of seeing create new understandings of vision

海浪之下，海洋动物奇特而陌生的视觉方式，正让人们对视觉产生新的认识。

AOf all the human senses, the visual system – the network that turns light into neural signals that create the perception of sight – is the most studied and best understood. Our intense curiosity about sight is no coincidence: we are animals who judge the world mostly by appearances rather than, say, by aromas. But our eyes allow us only one view out of a multitude of possibilities. In the depths of the ocean – a world we are beginning to learn about in detail – marine species have developed visual systems drastically different from our own. The majority of eyes, whether on land or in the sea, share two basic components: something to focus light as it hits the eye (for humans, a cornea and lens), and something to absorb and detect the light (a retina). Then the divergences begin.

在人类所有感觉系统中，视觉系统受到的研究最多，也最为人所了解；这个系统将光转化为神经信号，从而产生视觉。我们对视觉如此好奇，并非偶然：我们这种动物主要凭外观，而非比方说气味，来判断世界。但我们的眼睛所呈现的，只是众多可能视角中的一种。在海洋深处——这个我们才开始详细了解的世界里——海洋物种已经演化出与我们截然不同的视觉系统。无论是在陆地上还是海洋中，大多数眼睛都有两个基本组成部分：一部分在光进入眼睛时将其聚焦（在人类身上，是角膜和晶状体），另一部分则吸收并感知光（即视网膜）。从这里开始，各种眼睛便出现了差异。

BOcean creatures’ eyes have evolved a bewildering array of structures suited to each animal’s place in the natural order and the depth at which it lives. Some amphipods (deep-sea crustaceans) rely on biological fiber-optic cables to send light to their retina. Sight-dependent, nomadic predators have excellent vision, whereas many creatures that live at great depths can make only simple distinctions between light and dark. Light is abundant in the sunny shallows, but 50 feet below the surface, only the shorter wavelengths are able to penetrate, eliminating all discernable colors except blue. By about one mile down, the last traces of sunlight disappear. In this realm of perpetual night, the only illumination is manufactured, provided by the many creatures that generate their own light in a process known as bioluminescence.

海洋生物的眼睛演化出了形形色色的结构，以适应各自的生态位置和生活水深。一些端足类动物（amphipods，深海甲壳类动物）依靠生物光纤将光传送到视网膜。依赖视觉、四处游动的捕食者视力极佳，而许多生活在极深水域的生物只能简单地区分明暗。阳光照耀的浅水区光线充足，但到了水面下50英尺，只有波长较短的光能够穿透，除蓝色外，其他可辨别的颜色都消失了。到了约一英里深的地方，最后一丝阳光也消失了。在这片永夜世界里，唯一的照明来自生物自己制造的光：许多生物通过一种名为“生物发光”的过程，产生自身的光。

CAs they search for prey, swordfish swim through cold, deep water, occasionally diving as far as 3,000 feet below the surface. But even in the chilly depths, the swordfish keeps its eyes and brain 10 to 15 degrees Celsius warmer than the surrounding water by means of an unusual heat-generating muscle near the fish’s eye. Vision researcher Kerstin Fritsches, of the University of Queensland in Australia, along with colleagues there and in the United States and Sweden, found that keeping warm improves the swordfish’s vision. At 20°C, the scientists found, a swordfish retina can detect objects moving five times as quickly as the objects it can spot at 10°C. Since swordfish hunt speedy prey like squid, the ability to see fast-moving swimmers is crucial for finding dinner.

剑鱼（swordfish）在寻找猎物时会游过寒冷的深水区，偶尔下潜至水面以下3,000英尺。但即使在寒冷的深处，剑鱼也能借助眼睛附近一块不同寻常的产热肌肉，使眼睛和大脑的温度比周围水温高出10至15摄氏度。澳大利亚昆士兰大学（University of Queensland）的视觉研究者 Kerstin Fritsches 与该校以及美国、瑞典的同事发现，保持温暖能改善剑鱼的视觉。科学家发现，在20摄氏度时，剑鱼视网膜能察觉到的物体，其移动速度可达10摄氏度时所能察觉物体速度的五倍。由于剑鱼捕食鱿鱼等行动迅速的猎物，看清快速游动者的能力对于觅食至关重要。

DThe box jellyfish has surprisingly sophisticated eyes. It possesses a total of 24, divided among four eyestalks at the corners of its body. Four of the eyes on each stalk are simply groups of light-sensing cells that help it distinguish between bright and dark. The other two are complex, with lenses that produce high-quality images – but those lenses focus light well below the retina, meaning that the images are out of focus when they hit the light receptors. ‘They’re not really interested in fine details,’ says biologist Dan Nilsson, of the University of Lund in Sweden. Once the jellyfish can see the outlines of its environment, signals from its four simple brains (one in each eyestalk) steer it home to the shallow coastal waters of Australia, all the way from Puerto Rico on the other side of the world.

箱形水母（box jellyfish）的眼睛复杂得出人意料。它共有24只眼睛，分布在身体四角的四个眼柄上。每个眼柄上的四只眼睛只是一些感光细胞群，帮助它分辨明暗。另两只眼睛则很复杂，晶状体能够形成高质量的图像；但这些晶状体把光聚焦在视网膜下方较远的位置，这意味着图像到达感光细胞时已经失焦。瑞典隆德大学（University of Lund）的生物学家 Dan Nilsson 说：“它们其实并不关心细节。”一旦水母看清周围环境的轮廓，它的四个简单大脑（每个眼柄中有一个）就会发出信号，引导它一路从地球另一端的波多黎各（Puerto Rico），回到澳大利亚沿岸的浅水区。

EThe mantis shrimp, an inhabitant of tropical waters, leads a particularly vibrant life. University of Queensland neurobiologist Justin Marshall found that this crustacean has 12 different color receptors – cells in the retina that are sensitive to different wavelengths of light – whereas humans have only three. These shrimp behold a world we cannot begin to imagine: in addition to seeing a much richer light spectrum, they perceive linear and circular polarized light. Marshall suspects the ability to see polarized light ‘probably has something to do with reproduction’. A flash of polarized light reflected off a female shrimp, for example, could catch the eye of a potential mate without drawing unwanted attention from predators.

生活在热带水域的螳螂虾（mantis shrimp），过着格外色彩缤纷的生活。昆士兰大学的神经生物学家 Justin Marshall 发现，这种甲壳类动物有12种不同的颜色受体——即视网膜中对不同波长的光敏感的细胞——而人类只有三种。这些虾看到的世界，是我们根本无法想象的：除了能看见丰富得多的光谱，它们还能感知线偏振光和圆偏振光。Marshall 推测，看见偏振光的能力“很可能与繁殖有关”。例如，雌虾身上反射出的一闪而过的偏振光，可能吸引潜在配偶的注意，同时又不引起捕食者的注意。

FIn the deep-sea darkness, beyond the reach of the sun’s rays, some creatures make their own light. Organically created light, or bioluminescence, is so useful that it has evolved independently about 40 times across a range of creatures during the course of life on Earth, says bioluminescence expert Edith Widder, founder and CEO of the Ocean Research and Conservation Association in Fort Pierce, Florida, in the United States. Female anglerfish let someone else do the work, relying on colonies of light-emitting bacteria to give their lure its distinctive glow. The visual systems of male anglerfish are attuned to this light source; they can tell whether a female belongs to their species just by the silhouette of her lure, thus making it easy to find a mate in the dark.

在阳光无法到达的漆黑深海中，一些生物会自己发光。生物发光专家 Edith Widder 说，生物自身产生的光，也就是生物发光，用途如此之大，以至于在地球生命演化的过程中，它在多种生物中独立演化了大约40次；Widder 是海洋研究与保护协会（Ocean Research and Conservation Association）的创始人兼首席执行官，该协会位于美国佛罗里达州皮尔斯堡（Fort Pierce）。雌性鮟鱇（anglerfish）让别的生物代劳，依靠一群群发光细菌，使自己的诱饵发出独特的光。雄性鮟鱇的视觉系统与这种光源相适应；仅凭雌性诱饵的轮廓，它们就能判断对方是否属于同一物种，因此很容易在黑暗中找到配偶。

GThe atolla jellyfish, found in oceans across the globe, is high on beauty but low on brains. Looking beyond the scarlet tentacles will reveal that the creature has no central nervous system. Since it lacks the neural architecture to interpret complex visual information, the jellyfish must rely on its simple but numerous pigment cups. When attacked, as part of its defence the atolla launches a stunning blue bioluminescent display visible for hundreds of feet through the dusky mid-depth waters where it lives. Blue hues are the only colors of light able to penetrate that far below the surface, so the atolla’s red pigmentation normally makes it largely invisible.

遍布全球海洋的 atolla 水母，外形美丽，却没有多少“脑力”。不只看它鲜红的触手，就会发现这种生物没有中枢神经系统。由于缺乏解读复杂视觉信息所需的神经结构，这种水母只能依靠简单但数量众多的色素杯。遭到攻击时，atolla 水母会发出一片令人惊叹的蓝色生物光，作为防御手段的一部分；在它生活的昏暗中层水域中，数百英尺之外也能看到。只有蓝色光能穿透到水面下这么深的地方，因此 atolla 水母的红色色素通常使它几乎不可见。
