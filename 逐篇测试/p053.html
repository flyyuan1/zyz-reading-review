A mechanical friend for children

孩子们的机械朋友

AThe development of robots that interact socially with people and assist them in everyday life has been an elusive goal of modern science. Despite impressive advances in the mechanical aspects of this problem, producing robots that bond and socialize with people for sustained periods of time has proven difficult. The most successful robots so far have been storytellers, but they have only been able to maintain human interest for a limited time and typically rely on the robot telling stories that change over time. In practice, commercially available robots seldom cross the 10-hour barrier (i.e., individual users tend to spend less than a combined total of 10 hours with the robots before losing interest). This observation is in sharp contrast to the long-term interactions and bonding that commonly develop between humans and their pets.

开发既能与人进行社会互动、又能在日常生活中帮助人的机器人，一直是现代科学难以实现的目标。尽管这一问题的机械技术方面取得了显著进步，但事实证明，要造出能与人长期建立联系并进行社会交往的机器人仍十分困难。迄今最成功的机器人是会讲故事的机器人，但它们只能在有限时间内维持人的兴趣，而且通常依靠不断更换所讲的故事。实际中，市售机器人很少能跨过10小时这道门槛，也就是说，每位用户与机器人相处的累计时间通常不到10小时便失去兴趣。这与人和宠物之间普遍形成的长期互动与感情纽带形成鲜明对比。

BIn a recent study, researchers from the Institute for Neural Computation in California introduced a state-of-the-art social robot into a classroom of 18-to-24-month-olds for five months as a way of studying human/robot interactions. The researchers, including Fumihide Tanaka and Javier R. Movellan, introduced a toddler-sized humanoid robot into a classroom at a childhood education center. One of the QRIO series of robots, the 58 cm machine, was originally developed by Sony. ‘Children of toddler age were chosen because they have no preconceived notions of robots,’ according to Tanaka. One of the goals of the study was to establish whether it was possible for social robots to maintain the interest of children beyond the 10-hour barrier.

在最近一项研究中，加州神经计算研究所（Institute for Neural Computation）的研究人员把一个最先进的社交机器人放进由18至24个月大幼儿组成的班级，持续5个月，以此研究人与机器人的互动。包括 Fumihide Tanaka 和 Javier R. Movellan 在内的研究人员，把一个幼儿大小的人形机器人带进一所幼儿教育中心的教室。这个机器人属于 QRIO 系列，高58厘米，最初由索尼（Sony）开发。Tanaka 说：“之所以选择学步期幼儿，是因为他们对机器人没有先入之见。”研究目标之一，是确定社交机器人能否在超过10小时后仍保持孩子的兴趣。

CThe researchers sent instructions to the robot about every two minutes to do things like giggle, dance, sit down, fall down, or walk in a certain direction. The 45 sessions were videotaped, and interactions between toddlers and the robot were later analyzed. The results showed that the quality of those interactions improved steadily over 27 sessions. The interactions deteriorated quickly over the next 15 sessions, when the robot was ordered to behave in a more limited, predictable manner. Finally, the human/robot relations improved in the last three sessions, after the robot had been instructed to display its full range of behaviors.

研究人员大约每两分钟向机器人发送一次指令，让它咯咯笑、跳舞、坐下、摔倒，或朝某个方向行走。全部45次活动都被录像，随后研究人员分析了幼儿与机器人的互动。结果显示，在前27次活动中，互动质量稳步提高。接下来的15次活动里，机器人被要求以更加有限、可预测的方式行动，互动质量很快下降。最后，研究人员让机器人展现全部行为，在最后3次活动中，人与机器人的关系又有所改善。

D‘Initially, the children treated the robot very differently from the way they treated each other,’ Tanaka said. ‘Early in the study, some children cried when QRIO fell. But a month into the study, the toddlers helped QRIO stand up by pushing its back or pulling its hands.’ ‘The most important aspect of interaction was touch,’ Tanaka said. ‘At first, the toddlers would touch the robot on its face, but later on they would touch it only on its hands and arms, like they would with other humans. Another robot-like toy named Robby, which resembled QRIO but did not move, was used as a control in the study. While hugging of QRIO increased, hugging of Robby decreased throughout the study. Furthermore, when QRIO lay down on the floor, caretaking behaviors were frequently observed toward QRIO but seldom toward Robby.’

Tanaka 说：“起初，孩子们对待机器人的方式与彼此相待的方式大不相同。研究初期，QRIO 摔倒时有些孩子会哭。但研究进行一个月后，幼儿会推QRIO的背或拉它的手，帮助它站起来。”Tanaka 还说：“互动中最重要的是触摸。起初，幼儿会摸机器人的脸；后来，他们只摸它的手和手臂，就像对待其他人一样。研究还使用了另一个名为Robby的机器人玩具作为对照。它外形像QRIO，但不会移动。整个研究期间，孩子拥抱QRIO的次数增加，拥抱Robby的次数则减少。此外，QRIO 躺在地上时，人们经常观察到孩子照料它的行为；这样的行为在 Robby 身上却很少出现。”

EThe study concluded that after 45 days of immersion in a childcare center over a period of five months, long-term bonding and socialization occurred between toddlers and a state-of-the-art social robot. Overall, the interaction between children and the robot improved over time and the children progressively treated it more as a peer than a plaything. ‘To my knowledge, this is the first long-term study of this sort,’ said Ronald Arkin, a roboticist at the Georgia Institute of Technology in the US.

研究得出结论：在历时5个月的研究中，机器人在儿童照护中心与幼儿累计相处45天后，幼儿与这个最先进的社交机器人之间形成了长期联系，并进行了社会交往。总体而言，孩子与机器人的互动随时间推移不断改善，他们也越来越把它当作同伴，而不是玩具。美国佐治亚理工学院（Georgia Institute of Technology）机器人专家 Ronald Arkin 说：“据我所知，这是首项此类长期研究。”

FTanaka and Movellan are now developing autonomous robots for the toddler classroom. ‘It could have great potential in educational settings, assisting teachers and enriching the classroom environment,’ Tanaka said. The researchers hope that more advanced versions of robots like QRIO could become personalized tutors to assist teachers in classrooms. A robotic tutor could react on the spot to social cues and approximate social skills like facial expression and eye gaze, they said. ‘It is becoming clear that, to achieve this goal, we are going to endow machines with something similar to emotion, not just traditional forms of intelligence,’ said Movellan.

Tanaka 和 Movellan 目前正在为幼儿班开发自主机器人。Tanaka 说：“它在教育环境中可能大有潜力，可以协助教师并丰富课堂环境。”研究人员希望，QRIO这类机器人的更高级版本可以成为个性化辅导老师，在课堂上协助教师。他们说，机器人辅导老师可以对社交线索即时作出反应，并模拟面部表情和目光注视等社交技能。Movellan 说：“越来越明显的是，要实现这一目标，我们必须赋予机器某种类似情感的东西，而不只是传统形式的智能。”

GAssociate Professor David Powers, an expert in artificial intelligence and cognitive science at Flinders University in South Australia, commented, ‘In this study, it is clearly demonstrated that a limited range of robot behaviors, however impressive, is nowhere near as important to human/robot interaction as being able to make appropriate responses from a broad repertoire of behaviors.’ Ronald Arkin was not surprised by the affection demonstrated by the toddlers toward the robot. ‘Humans have a tremendous propensity to bond with artifacts, whether it be a car, a doll, or a robot,’ he said. But he also cautioned that researchers do not yet understand the consequences of increased human/robot interaction. ‘Studying how robots and humans work together can give us insight into whether this is a good thing or a bad thing for society,’ Arkin said. ‘We need to find out what the consequences are of introducing a robot into a cadre of children. How will that enhance, or potentially interfere with, their social development? Do we really understand the long-term impact of having a robot as a childhood friend?’

南澳大利亚弗林德斯大学（Flinders University）人工智能与认知科学专家、副教授 David Powers 评论道：“这项研究清楚表明，无论一套有限的机器人行为多么令人印象深刻，它对人机互动的重要性都远不及从丰富的行为库中作出恰当回应的能力。”幼儿对机器人表现出的喜爱并未令 Ronald Arkin 意外。他说：“人类非常容易与人造物建立感情，无论那是一辆汽车、一个玩偶，还是一个机器人。”但他也警告，研究人员尚不了解人机互动增加会带来什么后果。Arkin 说：“研究机器人与人如何共同活动，可以帮助我们判断这对社会究竟是好事还是坏事。我们必须弄清楚，把机器人带进一群孩子中会产生什么后果。它会如何促进、又可能如何妨碍孩子的社会性发展？让机器人成为童年朋友会产生什么长期影响，我们真的了解吗？”
