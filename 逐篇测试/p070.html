How to be Happy

如何获得幸福

Some recent developments in the science of happiness

幸福科学近期的一些进展

AA psychiatrist Tony Fernando was walking down the street when he saw a group of young homeless men sitting on the footpath. As Fernando handed out gifts of food, he tried to video the men’s reactions in his mind, recording how their gratitude lifted him in return. ‘I felt warm, content, meaningful,’ he says. Fernando believes the brain can be trained to make us happy and that his ‘video method’ is one way to achieve this. In fact, a growing body of scientific research internationally is showing how we can learn happiness in the same way we can learn to play the piano. In the soft tissue that forms the brain, there are approximately ten trillion synapses connecting roughly a hundred billion neurons. Active synapses become more sensitive; less active synapses die, but new ones grow quickly. This is relatively new thinking – just 30 years ago, neuroscientists believed that adult brain cells couldn’t regenerate. One of the pioneers in this field is University of Wisconsin professor Richard Davidson. He studied Buddhist monks using brain scans in his research facility, and found that those trained in meditation had higher levels of activity in the left prefrontal cortex – the area associated with happiness. Davidson believes that the trained brain is physically different from the untrained one.

精神科医生 Tony Fernando 有一次走在街上，看见一群无家可归的年轻男子坐在人行道上。Fernando 把食物送给这些人时，试着在脑海中把他们的反应录成“视频”，记下他们的感激如何反过来振奋自己。他说：“我感到温暖、满足，也觉得自己所做的事有意义。”Fernando 相信，大脑可以通过训练让我们感到幸福，而他的“视频法”就是一种实现方式。事实上，越来越多的国际科学研究表明，我们可以像学习弹钢琴一样学习如何获得幸福。构成大脑的柔软组织中，约有10万亿个突触，连接着大约1,000亿个神经元。活跃的突触会变得更敏感；不太活跃的突触会消失，但新的突触也会迅速生长。这是一种相对较新的认识——仅仅30年前，神经科学家还认为成年人的脑细胞无法再生。威斯康星大学（University of Wisconsin）教授 Richard Davidson 是这一领域的先驱之一。他在研究机构中利用脑部扫描研究佛教僧侣，发现受过冥想训练的人左前额叶皮层活动水平更高，而这一区域与幸福感相关。Davidson 认为，经过训练的大脑在生理上不同于未经训练的大脑。

BThe idea that we can train our brains to find more happiness has been called ‘mindfulness’ by neuropsychologist Rick Hanson, author of Hardwiring Happiness. It was in college that Hanson, quite unintentionally, first recognised the importance of something that would become his life’s work. As an unconfident teenager, he discovered he could turn a small event, ‘a young woman smiling at me’, into a good feeling he could hold on to. To do this, Hanson employs exactly the same imaginary ‘video method’ as psychiatrist Tony Fernando. ‘The brain takes its shape from what the mind rests upon,’ says Hanson. If you keep resting your mind on self-criticism and worries, he argues, your brain will be shaped by that. Hanson explains that resting it on pleasant feelings and the things you have accomplished means that over time your brain will take on a different shape, with strength and optimism hard-wired into it.

神经心理学家、著有《Hardwiring Happiness》的 Rick Hanson，把通过训练大脑来获得更多幸福的理念称为“正念”（mindfulness）。Hanson 在大学期间无意中首次认识到某件事的重要性，而它后来成了他毕生的研究主题。少年时，他缺乏自信，却发现自己可以把一件小事——“一位年轻女子对我微笑”——转化为一种能够留住的美好感受。为此，Hanson 采用了与精神科医生 Tony Fernando 完全相同的想象“视频法”。Hanson 说：“心念经常停留在哪里，大脑就会依此塑形。”他认为，如果心里总想着自我批评和忧虑，大脑就会受其塑造。Hanson 解释说，如果让心念停留在愉快感受和自己取得的成就上，久而久之，大脑便会形成不同的样貌，把坚韧与乐观深深刻入其中。

CIn New Zealand, the Mental Health Foundation runs mindfulness programmes in 14 schools. Foundation CEO Judi Clements says, ‘It’s a valuable approach because children are told to pay attention, but aren’t told how to pay attention.’ Preliminary results on the outcomes found that children had sustained increases in well-being. The Foundation recognises five pathways to happiness – being active, connecting with others, taking notice, learning and giving. And, says Clements, ‘We’re detecting a hunger for it from different organisations and professions as well, so the Foundation is soon going to pilot an online “well-being game” in several workplaces.’ The project leader is psychologist Carsten Grimm, who is interested in whether some pathways to happiness are more effective than others. In a study, Grimm recorded the activities and corresponding happiness scores of 173 people. What he found was that people who used several pathways to happiness achieved the best results, suggesting it’s better to have a balance than concentrate on the single pathway you most identify with.

在新西兰，心理健康基金会（Mental Health Foundation）在14所学校开展正念课程。基金会首席执行官 Judi Clements 说：“这种方法很有价值，因为孩子们会被要求集中注意力，却没人告诉他们该怎样集中。”初步结果显示，孩子们的幸福感得到了持续提升。基金会提出了通往幸福的五条路径：保持活跃、与人联结、用心观察、学习和给予。Clements 还说：“我们发现，不同组织和行业也都对此有强烈需求，因此基金会很快会在几个工作场所试行一款网络‘幸福游戏’。”项目负责人是心理学家 Carsten Grimm，他希望了解某些通往幸福的路径是否比其他路径更有效。在一项研究中，Grimm 记录了173人的活动及相应的幸福感评分。他发现，采用多条幸福路径的人效果最好。这提示，与其只专注于自己最认同的单一路径，不如保持多种路径之间的平衡。

DHowever, Associate Professor of Psychological Medicine Nathan Consedine greets our relentless search for the secrets to happiness with a tired sigh. ‘The more you chase it, the less you get it,’ he says, quoting studies showing those who value contentment and well-being are, in fact, less happy. The big question, he says, is whether there’s any benefit in actively seeking happiness. He cites a study, for example, which showed that people who were happier were less inclined to show sympathy. Consedine says that’s probably because happy people are strongly motivated to stay happy, but engaging sympathetically with others typically involves engaging with their distress, and that’s going to reduce their own happiness. Consedine’s not disagreeing with the theory of mindfulness, but believes there’s more work to be done.

然而，心理医学副教授 Nathan Consedine 面对人们孜孜不倦地寻找幸福秘诀，疲惫地叹了口气。他援引相关研究说：“越是追求幸福，得到的反而越少。”这些研究显示，重视满足感和幸福感的人，事实上没有那么幸福。他说，最大的问题在于，主动追求幸福是否有任何益处。例如，他提到一项研究，其中显示较幸福的人不太愿意表达同情。Consedine 认为，这可能是因为幸福的人非常希望维持自己的幸福状态，但同情他人通常意味着要接触对方的痛苦，而这会降低他们自己的幸福感。Consedine 并不反对正念理论，但认为仍需开展更多研究。

ESome of that work is being conducted at Auckland University’s Centre for Brain Research. Associate Professor Johanna Montgomery has been studying Hanson’s theory – that altering our brain activity at a synaptic level affects our behaviour. She accepts that it’s a logical explanation based on studies from animals, but we haven’t yet got the technology to prove it scientifically in humans. It’s true that MRI scans certainly do indicate an increased flow of blood to different areas of the brain which are experiencing positive stimuli. However, she warns that this is a long way from confirming that the brain itself is developing or strengthening.

其中一部分研究正在奥克兰大学脑研究中心（Auckland University’s Centre for Brain Research）开展。Johanna Montgomery 副教授一直在研究 Hanson 的理论，即改变大脑突触层面的活动会影响人的行为。她承认，这是根据动物研究得出的一种合乎逻辑的解释，但我们目前还没有技术能够在人类身上对其作出科学证明。磁共振成像（MRI）扫描确实显示，受到积极刺激的大脑不同区域血流量有所增加。然而，她提醒说，这远远不能证实大脑本身正在发育或增强。

FNonetheless, Montgomery believes she is making progress. In the laboratory, when scientists want to experiment with synaptic connections, they stimulate the brain with electricity, as this copies the high-frequency firings that happen normally in the brain every second. Using this technique, Montgomery has made a number of findings. For example, the hormones that are released routinely in response to good or bad situations – such as adrenaline – act to regulate the brain, in the same way that weather has a regulating effect on human activity more generally. But scientists understand some of these stimuli better than others. More is known about the impact on brain activity of prolonged periods of fear than the effects of ‘happiness’ hormones that are released by positive events in life. This is one potentially important area that she hopes to investigate further in future.

尽管如此，Montgomery 认为自己的研究正在取得进展。在实验室里，科学家若想研究突触连接，就会用电刺激大脑，因为这种做法可以模拟大脑中每秒都会自然发生的高频放电。Montgomery 利用这项技术取得了多项发现。例如，人体通常会针对好坏不同的情境释放激素，如肾上腺素；这些激素会调节大脑，就像天气会影响人的各种活动一样。不过，科学家对其中一些刺激的了解要多于另一些。对于长时间恐惧如何影响大脑活动，人们的认识要多于生活中的积极事件所释放的“幸福”激素会产生何种作用。她希望今后进一步研究这一可能十分重要的领域。
