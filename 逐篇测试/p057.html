Antarctic research

南极研究

Thirteen nations took part in the recent International Polar Year

13个国家参加了最近的国际极地年（International Polar Year）活动。

ANew Zealand’s contribution to International Polar Year was a 52-day voyage by the ship Tangaroa in order to conduct research in Antarctica’s Ross Sea. Lead scientist Mary Livingston emphasises just what a multi-faceted expedition this was, bringing together zoologists, oceanographers, meteorologists and other scientists from the Ministry of Fisheries, the National Institute for Water and Atmospheric Research, the National Museum and various universities, to study life beneath the seas of Antarctica. Competition for berths – the ship can carry 44 people, including 13 crew – was fierce. Voyage leader Stu Hanchet says, ‘We could have filled the science positions four times over … we had so many people requesting a berth … We ended up with a really strong team.’

新西兰对国际极地年的贡献，是由“坦加罗阿”号（Tangaroa）开展一次为期52天的航行，前往南极洲罗斯海（Ross Sea）进行研究。首席科学家 Mary Livingston 强调，这次考察涉及多个领域。渔业部（Ministry of Fisheries）、国家水与大气研究所（National Institute for Water and Atmospheric Research）、国家博物馆（National Museum）和多所大学的动物学家、海洋学家、气象学家及其他科学家汇聚在一起，研究南极海域水下的生命。船上可载44人，其中包括13名船员，因此争夺船位的竞争十分激烈。航行负责人 Stu Hanchet 说：“申请船位的人实在太多……科学岗位即使增加到4倍也能全部招满……最后我们组建了一支非常强大的团队。”

BIt would be hard to overstate how hostile the Antarctic environment is for scientists. The Tangaroa’s captain, Graham Leachman, explains that the Ross Sea is subject to katabatic winds* that sweep down off the Antarctic continent to create rough seas. Another danger is the possibility of the ship colliding with floating ice. ‘Even though the ship’s officers use all sorts of high-tech equipment … none of it can tell you how thick the ice is,’ says Leachman, ‘and you still can’t beat looking out of the window.’ In the dark, he says, you can’t tell how thick the ice is, but if you can see its colour, it’s possible to estimate. This is why he prefers sailing in summer, when the hours of daylight in these high latitudes are longest, even though this plays tricks with his passengers’ body clocks.

南极环境对科学家极其恶劣，怎么强调都不为过。“坦加罗阿”号船长 Graham Leachman 解释说，罗斯海会遭遇从南极大陆俯冲而下的下降风（katabatic winds）*，造成汹涌海况。另一项危险是船只可能与浮冰相撞。Leachman 说：“虽然船上的驾驶员会使用各种高科技设备……但没有一种能告诉你冰有多厚，往窗外看仍是最可靠的方法。”他说，黑暗中无法判断冰层厚度；但只要能看见冰的颜色，就可以作出估计。正因如此，他更喜欢夏季航行，此时这些高纬度地区的白昼最长，尽管长时间日照会扰乱乘客的生物钟。

CAnalysis of what the expedition found continues, but it’s likely that several new species have been discovered and numerous other secrets revealed about life 3,000 metres below the surface. ‘We have collected huge worms and strange crustaceans,’ says Livingston, ‘but most impressive of all were the starfish that measured more than half a metre across – that’s most abnormal for creatures belonging to this species.’ This phenomenon of Antarctic seas is called ‘gigantism’ – the fact that some species grow to unusually large sizes. Livingston suggests various possible causes for gigantism including the extreme cold, few predators and high levels of oxygen in the seawater, but as yet no final determination can be made. Another interesting discovery was that in some places every inch of the sea floor was covered with life, whereas elsewhere icebergs have scoured out deep scars and ravines in the sea floor as they go by.

对考察发现的分析仍在继续，但这次考察很可能发现了几个新物种，并揭示了水面下3000米处生命的许多其他奥秘。Livingston 说：“我们收集到了巨大的蠕虫和奇特的甲壳动物，但最令人惊叹的是直径超过半米的海星——对这一物种来说，这种尺寸极不寻常。”南极海域的这种现象称为“巨型化”（gigantism），即某些物种会长到异常巨大的体型。Livingston 提出了造成巨型化的多种可能原因，包括极端寒冷、捕食者很少和海水含氧量高，但目前还无法作出最终判断。另一个有趣发现是，有些地方海床的每一寸都覆盖着生命；而在另一些地方，冰山经过时会在海床上刮出深深的痕迹和沟壑。

* katabatic wind: a wind caused by the local downward motion of cool air

*下降风：由局部冷空气向下运动形成的风

DBy using deep-sea trawl nets, high-definition cameras and water samplers, the team has revealed that many of the creatures that live at extreme depths have a bizarre appearance. Among them were spotted tunicates, plankton-eating animals that are slender structures that appear to be fashioned from glass. The most fearsome-looking fish was the Southern Ocean daggertooth, a species that is ‘monocyclic’, meaning the fish die after the first spawning. They are also unusual for being capable of shedding their teeth and growing a new set. Another predatory fish brought to the surface was the stareater, an extraordinary species that has an appendage like a piece of cord hanging from its chin. In these deep, dark waters, this glows red and attracts prey to swim within striking distance. Just as impressive and much more beautiful were the basket stars, a species related to starfish that lives on the seabed. The basket stars’ five arms branch out numerous times to form a spectacular fan-like structure which they turn to face into the current so that food is brought into their embrace.

研究团队利用深海拖网、高清摄像机和水样采集器发现，许多生活在极深水域的生物外形怪异。其中包括斑点被囊动物，这是一种以浮游生物为食的动物，身体细长，看起来仿佛由玻璃制成。外表最可怕的鱼是南大洋匕首齿鱼（Southern Ocean daggertooth）。这个物种属于“单次繁殖型”（monocyclic），也就是第一次产卵后便会死亡。它们还有一个不同寻常之处：能让牙齿脱落，再长出一副新牙。另一种被带到水面的掠食鱼是 stareater。这个奇特物种的下巴垂着一个像绳子一样的附肢。在黑暗的深水中，这个附肢会发出红光，吸引猎物游到它可以出击的距离内。同样令人惊叹、而且美丽得多的是篮海星（basket stars）。它是海星的近亲，生活在海床上。篮海星的5条腕反复分支，形成壮观的扇形结构；它们让扇面朝向水流，使食物随水流落入它们的怀抱。

EBut whatever exciting discoveries were made, everyday life onboard ship in the Ross Sea is unrelenting. Most people share a cabin with one other person – they’re allocated so that as one person begins their 12 hours off duty, the other occupant is starting work on deck. The fresh food runs out in a few days and after that most provisions are frozen, canned or dried. Expedition general manager Fred Smits recognises that the cook has an essential role in ensuring the well-being of those onboard. ‘When you are at sea for so long,’ he says, ‘you think a lot about your next meal.’

不过，无论取得了多么令人兴奋的发现，在罗斯海的船上，日常生活都十分辛苦，毫无喘息。大多数人会与另一个人共用船舱；人员这样安排，是为了让一个人开始12小时休息时，另一位同舱者正好到甲板上开工。新鲜食物几天后就会吃完，此后的大部分食物都是冷冻、罐装或干制品。考察队总经理 Fred Smits 明白，厨师对保障船上人员的身心状态至关重要。他说：“长时间待在海上时，你会常常想着下一顿饭。”

FPerhaps most controversially, one of the precious berths on the Tangaroa was taken up by cameraman Max Quinn, who filmed a documentary about the voyage. Quinn’s inclusion wasn’t welcomed at first. ‘Scientists by nature,’ Livingston says, ‘tend to be camera-shy and quiet. They’re not into having cameras there, so it was difficult getting everyone’s permission and agreement.’ But in the end Quinn fitted in very well. ‘The trick,’ he says, ‘is to mostly film the scientists at work, in their professional roles … rather than eating their breakfast.’ He knows when not to intrude. The documentary is a window on a trip few people get to make and also a peek into what research really looks like. ‘Science,’ says Livingston, ‘is intrepid, more so than people realise. It’s not necessarily examining dry samples in the laboratory, but can also mean braving the elements on deck. That’s what the documentary captures, the sense of real people with real lives.’

或许最具争议的是，“坦加罗阿”号一个宝贵的船位给了摄影师 Max Quinn，他拍摄了一部有关此次航行的纪录片。起初，人们并不欢迎 Quinn 加入。Livingston 说：“科学家天性往往不愿面对镜头，而且性格安静。他们不喜欢旁边有摄像机，所以很难取得每个人的许可和同意。”但 Quinn 最终很好地融入了团队。他说：“诀窍是主要拍摄科学家工作时的专业状态……而不是他们吃早餐的样子。”他知道何时不应打扰别人。这部纪录片为人们打开了一扇窗，让他们看到一段很少有人能亲身经历的旅程，也一窥真实的研究是什么样子。Livingston 说：“科学工作勇于冒险，远超人们的想象。它未必是在实验室里检查干燥样本，也可能意味着在甲板上直面恶劣天气。纪录片捕捉到的正是这一点——真实的人过着真实生活的感觉。”
