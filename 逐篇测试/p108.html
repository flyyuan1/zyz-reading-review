Walking and shoes in eighteenth-century London

十八世纪伦敦的步行与鞋履

A look at changing fashions in footwear and leisure

鞋履与休闲时尚变迁一瞥

AIt was difficult to get about in eighteenth-century London. During the first half of that century, the physical mobility of the upper classes in the city had been considerably restricted: the poor condition of the streets did not allow ‘walking for pleasure’ outside private parks or pleasure gardens. Streets were dirty, rubbish was everywhere and the British climate did not help. Walking could be a risky business. The terrible state of roads, always flooded and muddy, created a metropolitan world of shoe cleaners and street sweepers.

十八世纪的伦敦出行很困难。在该世纪上半叶，城中上层阶级的出行受到了很大限制：街道状况恶劣，除私人公园或游乐花园外，人们无法“以散步为乐”。街道肮脏，垃圾遍地，英国的气候也不利于步行。步行可能是一件危险的事。道路状况糟糕，总是积水、泥泞，这也催生了城中擦鞋工和清道夫的生计。

BReferences to walking are not abundant in early eighteenth-century texts. This is truer for urban spaces than for places in the countryside, where country life had always been dominated by a lifestyle of outdoor activities. For instance, in eighteenth-century London, a sightseer’s experience was restricted to touristic places and involved journeys by a series of boats and coaches. Walking was considered to be an activity for the poorer members of society — or simply those who could not afford a coach.

十八世纪早期的文本很少提及步行。关于城市的记载比关于乡村的记载更少提到步行，因为乡村生活一向以户外活动为主。例如，在十八世纪的伦敦，观光客的体验仅限于旅游景点，往来则要接连乘船和马车。步行被视为社会中较贫困人群的活动，或者简单说，是那些乘不起马车的人所做的事。

CThis situation underwent a sudden improvement in the mid-eighteenth century, with measures implementing the provision of public spaces in which to walk. The first legislation concerning paving in London was passed in 1762, and during the following decades similar laws were enacted in many cities. By the end of the century, areas for walking were commonplace, and far more people were enjoying the pastime than before. Sophie von La Roche writes about metropolitan street life in the diary of her visit to London of 1786. She comments on how many more pedestrians there were on the broad, clean pavements, alongside the houses where, she says ‘many thousands of neatly clad people, eminent men and dressy women, pursue their way safe from the carriages, horses and dirt’. Although the pastime of walking was pursued only in specific spaces designed for the purpose, the number of these spaces increased as the nineteenth century approached. Travelling on foot became smoother and less dangerous.

十八世纪中叶，随着政府采取措施提供供人步行的公共空间，这种情况迅速改善。伦敦第一部有关铺设路面的法律于1762年通过，此后几十年里，许多城市也颁布了类似法律。到该世纪末，步行区域已十分常见，享受这项消遣的人远比以前多。Sophie von La Roche 在她1786年到访伦敦的日记中写到了大都市的街头生活。她提到，宽阔、洁净的人行道上行人比以往多了许多；这些人行道沿房屋而设，用她的话说，“成千上万衣着整洁的人、显要男士和穿着时髦的女士，在这里赶路，不受车辆、马匹和污泥之扰”。尽管步行这种消遣仅限于专为此目的设计的特定空间，但随着十九世纪临近，这类空间的数量不断增加。徒步出行变得更顺畅，也更安全。

DAttitudes toward walking through public spaces and their changes over time were visible in the practical designs of fashionable footwear. Protective overshoes and other devices designed to keep feet above the dirty and wet ground soon disappeared, and ease of mobility increased. In the early eighteenth century, the look of men’s and women’s shoes started to diverge in appearance as a distinction between male and female footwear filtered into production. Contemporary descriptions of the boot and shoe trade underlined the differences between ladies’ shoemakers and men’s shoemakers too.

人们对穿行公共空间的态度及其随时间发生的变化，都体现在时尚鞋履的实用设计上。用于让双脚远离湿脏地面的防护套鞋和其他装置很快消失，行动也更加方便。十八世纪早期，随着男女鞋有别的观念逐渐进入生产环节，男鞋和女鞋的外观开始分化。当时对靴鞋行业的描述也强调了制作女鞋与制作男鞋的鞋匠之间的差异。

EThe production of men’s and women’s footwear required different skills, as the products were essentially different. Men wore more sober footwear, not unlike modern shoes. Their shoes were entirely made of leather, which provided resistance to rain, mud and bad weather. Women’s shoes had uppers made of textiles — silk, satin or cloth — and their resistance to bad weather and dirty streets was limited. Only the lower ranks of women wore leather shoes of a form similar to men’s. The high-quality shoes for women were often embroidered and were normally worn with protective overshoes, as they were prone to being damaged or disfigured by the conditions of the time.

制作男女鞋履需要不同的技艺，因为两类产品有着根本差异。男性穿的鞋更为素净，与现代鞋并无太大不同。他们的鞋完全由皮革制成，能够抵御雨水、泥泞和恶劣天气。女鞋的鞋面则由纺织品制成，如丝绸、缎子或布料，因此对恶劣天气和肮脏街道的抵御能力有限。只有社会地位较低的女性才穿形制与男鞋相似的皮鞋。优质女鞋往往带有刺绣，通常还要搭配防护套鞋，因为在当时的环境下，这些鞋很容易损坏或变得不美观。

FThe shoes of men and women related to their more general clothing in different ways. Wealthy women’s footwear harmonised with their other garments, a harmony that could be effected through matching textiles, general colour, embroidered detail or buckles. In the eighteenth century, men’s shoes matched their clothing in different ways, for example with glossy black, red or pink heels. The shoe of an upper-class man also acted as a type of sign which emphasised the difference between the wearer and young boys and women. The shoe also distinguished him from working men who wore leather or cloth protective leggings, ragged shoes and clogs. The symbolic marking of the entry of a wealthy young man into manhood through his shoes, as well as his other dress, may have been of more importance than has previously been acknowledged in costume history.

男性和女性的鞋履与各自整体服装的搭配方式不同。富裕女性的鞋履与其他衣物协调一致，这种协调可以通过相配的面料、整体色彩、刺绣细节或鞋扣来实现。在十八世纪，男鞋以不同方式与服装相配，例如采用亮黑色、红色或粉色的鞋跟。上层阶级男性的鞋还是一种标志，用来突出穿鞋者与年幼男孩和女性之间的区别。这种鞋也让他有别于体力劳动者，后者穿着皮革或布制的防护绑腿、破旧的鞋和木底鞋。富裕青年男子的鞋和其他服装共同象征着他步入成年，这种标志的意义可能比以往服饰史研究所承认的更为重要。
