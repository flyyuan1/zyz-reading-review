The internal body clock

体内生物钟

Each of our cells has an internal 'clock' which dictates our daily rhythms. But why?

我们的每个细胞都有一个内部“时钟”，支配着每日节律。但为什么会这样？

AFrom buffalo to bacteria, oaks to algae, all life follows the same relentless 24-hour cycle, which is driven by the rising and setting of the Sun. Or is it? Over 250 years ago, a French scientist performed a simple experiment that blew apart the idea that all life on Earth does the bidding of the Sun. Intrigued by the way some flowers open and close their leaves each day, Jean-Jacques de Mairan put a heliotrope in a darkened room and observed the effect. He was expecting the plant, robbed of sunlight, to cease its daily routine. To his astonishment, its leaves continued to open and close as if in response to some invisible timekeeper.

从水牛到细菌，从橡树到藻类，所有生命都遵循同一个毫不间断的24小时周期，而这个周期由太阳升起和落下驱动。真是这样吗？250多年前，一位法国科学家进行了一项简单实验，彻底打破了地球上一切生命都听命于太阳的观念。Jean-Jacques de Mairan 对一些花卉每天开合叶片的现象很感兴趣，于是把一株天芥菜（heliotrope）放进黑暗房间，观察会有什么影响。他原以为，植物失去阳光后会停止每日活动。令他惊讶的是，叶片仍继续开合，仿佛在响应某个看不见的计时器。

BBut what – and where – is this internal timekeeper? And how does it achieve such astonishing regularity? These are the mysteries at the forefront of chronobiology, the study of the effect of time in living organisms. The search for answers is about more than just tying up some scientific loose ends. Internal ‘clocks’ clearly cause a lot of hardship. When an individual’s ‘clock’ is knocked out of synchronization through having to work at night or go on long-haul flights, that person can be left utterly unable to think or act. Yet even when these ‘clocks’ work correctly, the natural rhythms of alertness they generate prove dangerous: traffic accident statistics show two deadly peaks, at 4 a.m. and again twelve hours later when people are at their least alert.

但这个内部计时器是什么，又在哪里？它如何实现如此惊人的规律性？这些是时间生物学（chronobiology）前沿研究中的未解之谜；这门学科研究时间对生物体的影响。寻找答案不只是为了解决几个尚未弄清的科学问题。内部“时钟”显然会造成许多困扰。一个人因夜间工作或长途飞行而使自己的“时钟”失去同步后，可能会完全无法思考或行动。然而，即使这些“时钟”正常运转，它们产生的自然警觉度节律也可能十分危险：交通事故统计显示有两个致命高峰，一个在凌晨4点，另一个在12小时后，这两个时段都是人们警觉度最低的时候。

CDuring the 1970s, best-selling books began to emerge claiming such phenomena were manifestations of so-called biorhythms, a set of three cycles governing physical, emotional and intellectual traits. Said to start from the moment of birth and repeat every 23, 28 and 33 days respectively, they were supposed to result in ‘critical days’ when one or more cycles led to sub-optimal performance, with unfortunate consequences.

20世纪70年代，一些畅销书开始出现，声称这类现象是所谓“生物节律”（biorhythms）的表现。按照这种理论，身体、情绪和智力特征分别受3个周期支配。据说，这些周期从出生时开始，分别每23天、28天和33天重复一次，并会造成“关键日”；在这些日子里，一个或多个周期会使人的表现低于最佳水平，带来不幸后果。

DScientists have since dismissed biorhythms as pseudoscience, insisting the existence of the three cycles has no basis in fact. In 1998, Dr Terence Hines of Pace University, New York State published the most comprehensive study of the claims made for biorhythms, reviewing the results of over 130 investigations. He found that three-quarters of them failed to provide any support for biorhythms. Most of the remainder contained blunders ranging from faulty mathematics to basic errors in statistics, while the handful of positive studies were explicable as flukes.

此后，科学家否定了上述“生物节律”理论，认为它属于伪科学，并坚持指出，这3个周期的存在毫无事实依据。1998年，纽约州佩斯大学（Pace University）的 Terence Hines 博士发表了对该理论主张最全面的研究，审查了130多项调查的结果。他发现，其中四分之三没有提供任何支持该理论的证据。其余研究大多存在错误，从数学计算不当到基本统计错误都有；而少数得出正面结论的研究，也可以用偶然巧合来解释。

EBut there is no doubting the existence of 24-hour biological cycles – or, rather, ones roughly 24 hours long. Following de Mairan’s pioneering work, other researchers found that when deprived of the cues provided by sunlight, organisms settle down to ‘free-running’ cycles that are close to, but rarely exactly, 24 hours long. In the case of humans, the cycle is typically around 24.5 hours long. This is the so-called ‘circadian’ cycle (from the Latin meaning ‘about a day’), the length of which is generated by the mysterious internal ‘clock’.

不过，24小时生物周期的存在毋庸置疑；更准确地说，是长度约为24小时的周期。de Mairan 完成开创性研究后，其他研究人员发现，生物体失去阳光提供的提示时，会稳定地进入接近24小时、却很少恰好为24小时的“自由运行”（free-running）周期。人类的周期通常约为24.5小时。这就是所谓的“昼夜节律”（circadian）周期；这个词来自拉丁语，意为“大约一天”，而周期长度由神秘的内部“时钟”决定。

FDuring the late 1960s, chronobiologists believed they had found the ‘clock’, in the form of the suprachiasmatic nucleus (SCN), a collection of nerves in a region of the brain known as the hypothalamus. Linked to photosensitive cells in the eye, the SCN senses daylight and triggers the release of hormones like melatonin, which keep body functions in synchronization with the time of day. For a few years the SCN was regarded as the ultimate pacemaker – at least in higher organisms such as ourselves. But in 1971, scientists at the California Institute of Technology working with fruit flies found evidence for something truly amazing. Fruit flies appeared to have genes affecting the daily rhythm of their behavior – suggesting that there are ‘clocks’ inside each of their cells. Further evidence for this emerged in 1995, when researchers at Massachusetts General Hospital isolated nerve cells from the SCN, and found they kept up a circadian cycle without help from daylight. Finally in 1997, scientists at Northwestern University, Illinois, found a gene that regulates the daily rhythms of cellular activity in mammals.

20世纪60年代末，时间生物学家认为自己找到了这个“时钟”，即视交叉上核（suprachiasmatic nucleus，SCN）。它是位于大脑下丘脑（hypothalamus）区域的一组神经。视交叉上核与眼内感光细胞相连，能够感知日光，并触发褪黑素（melatonin）等激素释放，使身体功能与一天中的时间保持同步。有几年时间，视交叉上核被视为最终的节律起搏器，至少对人类这样的高等生物而言如此。但1971年，加州理工学院（California Institute of Technology）研究果蝇（fruit flies）的科学家发现了某种真正惊人的证据。果蝇似乎拥有影响其每日行为节律的基因，这提示每个细胞内部都有“时钟”。1995年又出现了进一步证据：马萨诸塞总医院（Massachusetts General Hospital）的研究人员从视交叉上核分离出神经细胞，发现即使没有日光帮助，这些细胞仍会保持昼夜节律。最后，1997年，伊利诺伊州（Illinois）西北大学（Northwestern University）的科学家发现了一个调节哺乳动物细胞活动每日节律的基因。

GThe details of how this gene, known as CLOCK, actually works are still being investigated, but they could culminate in better ways of coping with shift-work and jet-lag. Professor Takahashi, who led the research, says that now that we know that circadian clocks exist throughout our bodies, we will need new strategies and therapeutics to reset all of our cells. But how do all the biochemical ‘clocks’ inside their cells stay in synchronization and why did organisms bother to acquire a link with sunlight? So far, no-one knows, though there are several theories. For example, sunlight might be useful in keeping the myriad cellular ‘clocks’ in lockstep.

这个名为 CLOCK 的基因究竟如何运作，细节仍在研究中，但研究最终可能带来更好的办法，帮助人们应对轮班工作和时差反应。领导该研究的 Takahashi 教授说，既然我们已经知道昼夜节律时钟遍布全身，就需要新的策略和治疗方法来重置所有细胞。但生物体细胞中的所有生化“时钟”如何保持同步？生物体又为什么会形成与阳光的联系？到目前为止，无人知晓，不过已有几种理论。例如，阳光或许有助于让无数细胞“时钟”保持步调一致。

HIn the search for answers, researchers are studying the behavior of a microbe called cyanobacterium. In 2005, a team at Nagoya University, Japan, showed that chemical reactions between three proteins produced by this bacterium ebbed and flowed on a 24-hour cycle. Cyanobacterium is the oldest form of life on Earth, so this suggests that cellular ‘clocks’ have existed for over three billion years. All subsequent organisms have followed suit, says Professor Johnson of Vanderbilt University, Tennessee. ‘Bacteria, fungi, plants and animals all appear to have evolved clock systems independently from each other.’ Quite why is still unknown. But one thing is clear: the daily routine of life is certainly not a modern invention.

为寻找答案，研究人员正在研究一种名为蓝细菌（cyanobacterium）的微生物的行为。2005年，日本名古屋大学（Nagoya University）的一个团队发现，这种细菌产生的3种蛋白质之间的化学反应会按24小时周期起伏变化。蓝细菌是地球上最古老的生命形式，因此这提示，细胞“时钟”已经存在了30多亿年。田纳西州（Tennessee）范德比尔特大学（Vanderbilt University）的 Johnson 教授说，此后出现的所有生物都遵循了同样的道路。“细菌、真菌、植物和动物似乎各自独立演化出了时钟系统。”至于究竟为什么，仍然无人知晓。但有一点很清楚：生命的每日规律绝不是现代才出现的。
