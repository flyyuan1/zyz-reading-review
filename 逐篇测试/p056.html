A unique golden textile

独一无二的金色织物

A two-man project to use spider silk is achieved after 4 years

一项由两人开展的蜘蛛丝利用项目，历时4年终于完成。

AA rare textile made from the silk of more than a million wild spiders has been on display at the American Museum of Natural History in New York City. To produce this golden cloth, 70 people spent four years collecting golden-orb spiders from telephone poles in Madagascar, while another dozen workers carefully extracted about 80 feet of silk filament from each of the arachnids. The resulting 11-foot-by-4-foot textile is the only large piece of cloth made from natural spider silk in the world today.

一件用100多万只野生蜘蛛的蛛丝制成的稀有织物，曾在纽约市美国自然历史博物馆（American Museum of Natural History）展出。为了制作这块金色织物，70个人花了4年时间，从马达加斯加的电话线杆上收集金色圆网蛛（golden-orb spiders）；另有12名工人小心地从每只蜘蛛身上抽取约80英尺长的丝线。最终制成的织物长11英尺、宽4英尺，是当今世界上唯一一块用天然蛛丝制成的大型布料。

BSpider silk is very elastic and strong compared with steel or Kevlar, said textile expert Simon Peers, who co-led the project. Kevlar is a lightweight synthetic fabric, chemically related to nylon, that is used in bullet-proof vests. Kevlar is resistant to wear, tear and heat and has virtually no melting point. But the tensile strength of spider silk is even greater than Kevlar’s aramid filaments and higher than that of high-grade steel. Most importantly, spider silk is extremely lightweight: a strand long enough to circle the Earth would weigh less than 500 grams (18 oz). It is also especially ductile, able to stretch up to 140 percent of its length without breaking and to retain its strength below –40 °C, giving it toughness equal to that of leading commercial fibres.

共同负责该项目的纺织专家 Simon Peers 说，与钢或凯夫拉（Kevlar）相比，蛛丝弹性很大，也很坚韧。凯夫拉是一种轻质合成织物，在化学性质上与尼龙相关，用于制作防弹背心。凯夫拉耐磨、抗撕裂、耐热，而且几乎没有熔点。但蛛丝的抗拉强度甚至高于凯夫拉的芳纶丝，也高于优质钢。最重要的是，蛛丝极其轻盈：一根足以环绕地球的蛛丝重量不到500克（18盎司）。它的延展性也特别好，在不断裂的情况下可以拉伸至自身长度的140%，并能在低于零下40摄氏度时保持强度，因此其韧性可与领先的商用纤维相当。

CResearchers have long been intrigued by the unique properties of spider silk. Unfortunately, spider silk is extremely hard to mass-produce. Unlike silkworms—easy to raise in captivity—spiders have a habit of biting off each other’s heads when housed together. According to Peers, there is intensive research worldwide aimed at replicating spider-silk tensile properties for use in medicine and industry, but no-one has yet reproduced all the qualities of natural silk.

长期以来，研究人员一直对蛛丝的独特性质着迷。遗憾的是，蛛丝极难大规模生产。蚕很容易人工饲养，而蜘蛛养在一起时却有咬掉彼此脑袋的习性。Peers 说，世界各地正在开展大量研究，试图复制蛛丝的抗拉性能，用于医疗和工业领域；但目前还没有人能重现天然蛛丝的全部特性。

DPeers conceived the idea of weaving spider silk after reading about French missionary Jacob Paul Camboué, who worked with spiders in Madagascar during the 1880s and 1890s. Camboué built a small hand-driven machine to extract silk from up to 24 spiders at once, without harming them: the spiders were briefly restrained, their silk collected, then released. Peers built a replica of this 24-spider “silking” machine, said co-leader Nicholas Godley. As a test the pair collected about 20 spiders. “When we stuck them in the machine and started turning it, lo and behold, this beautiful gold-coloured silk started coming out,” Godley recalled.

Peers 读到法国传教士 Jacob Paul Camboué 的事迹后，萌生了用蛛丝织布的想法。19世纪80和90年代，Camboué 曾在马达加斯加研究蜘蛛。Camboué 制作了一台小型手摇机器，一次最多可以从24只蜘蛛身上抽丝，而且不会伤害它们：蜘蛛只被短暂固定，蛛丝收集完毕后便会被放生。项目另一位负责人 Nicholas Godley 说，Peers 仿制了这台可容纳24只蜘蛛的“抽丝”机器。两人收集了约20只蜘蛛进行测试。Godley 回忆道：“我们把它们放进机器，开始转动手柄；没想到，美丽的金色蛛丝真的出来了。”

ETo make a textile of any significant size, the scale had to increase dramatically. Fourteen thousand spiders yield about an ounce of silk, Godley said, and the finished textile weighs about 2.6 pounds. By the end, handlers had worked with more than one million female golden-orb spiders—abundant in Madagascar and famed for their golden thread. Because the spiders produce silk only in the rainy season, all were collected between October and June. An additional 12 workers used hand-powered machines to extract the silk and twist it into 96-filament yarn. After “silking”, the spiders were released; within a week they regenerate their silk, allowing the same individuals to be used again—“the gift that never stops giving,” said Godley.

要制成具有一定面积的织物，项目规模就必须大幅扩大。Godley 说，1.4万只蜘蛛只能产出约1盎司蛛丝，而成品织物重约2.6磅。项目结束时，工作人员已经接触过100多万只雌性金色圆网蛛。这种蜘蛛在马达加斯加数量很多，以产出金色丝线闻名。由于蜘蛛只在雨季产丝，所有蜘蛛都是在10月至次年6月期间收集的。另有12名工人使用手摇机器抽丝，并将蛛丝捻成由96根细丝组成的纱线。“抽丝”后，蜘蛛会被放生；它们可在一周内重新生成蛛丝，因此同一只蜘蛛还能再次使用。Godley 称其为“永不停歇的馈赠”。

FSpending four years to produce a single piece of cloth is hardly practical for scientists or companies hoping to exploit spider silk in biomedicine or as a Kevlar alternative. Several groups have inserted spider genes into bacteria and even goats to make silk, but results have been only partly successful. One reason is that spider silk begins as a liquid protein produced in a special gland in the abdomen. Using the spinneret, the spider applies force that rearranges the protein’s molecular structure, transforming it into solid fibre. “When we talk about a spider spinning silk, we’re talking about how it applies forces to convert liquid to solid,” explained spider-silk expert Todd Blackledge of the University of Akron, who was not involved in the project. “Every year we get closer to mass production, but we’re not there yet.” For now, we must be content with one extraordinarily beautiful cloth—courtesy of more than a million spiders.

对于希望把蛛丝用于生物医学或用作凯夫拉替代品的科学家和公司而言，花4年时间只制作一块布显然并不实用。几个研究团队已把蜘蛛基因植入细菌，甚至山羊体内来生产丝，但只取得了部分成功。原因之一在于，蛛丝最初是一种液态蛋白质，由蜘蛛腹部的特殊腺体产生。蜘蛛利用吐丝器施加力量，重新排列蛋白质的分子结构，使其转变为固体纤维。未参与该项目的阿克伦大学（University of Akron）蛛丝专家 Todd Blackledge 解释说：“我们谈论蜘蛛吐丝时，谈的就是它如何施加力量，把液体变成固体。我们每年都更接近大规模生产，但目前仍未实现。”眼下，我们只能满足于这一块美得非同寻常的织物——这是100多万只蜘蛛共同带来的成果。
