Some views on the use of headphones

对使用耳机的一些看法

Whether wearing headphones at work, or in other areas of everyday life, is a good thing or a bad thing has generated a lot of research and opinion

无论是在工作中还是在日常生活的其他场合，戴耳机究竟是好是坏，都引发了大量研究和看法。

To visit a typical modern office today is to walk into a room with possibly a dozen songs playing simultaneously but to hear none of them. Up to half of younger workers listen to music on their headphones, and nearly all of them think it makes them better at their jobs. In survey after survey, people report with confidence that music makes them happier, better at concentrating, and more productive.

如今走进一间典型的现代办公室，房间里可能同时播放着十来首歌，你却一首也听不见。年轻员工中，多达一半会戴耳机听音乐，而其中几乎所有人都认为，这能让自己工作得更好。一次又一次调查中，人们都自信地表示，音乐使他们更快乐、更能集中注意力，工作效率也更高。

Scientists do not share this belief; they maintain that listening to music hurts people’s ability to recall other things they should be doing, and any pop song, loud or soft, reduces overall performance for both extroverts and introverts. A Taiwanese study linked music that has lyrics to lower marks on concentration tests for college students, and other research has shown music with lyrics scrambles our brains’ verbal-processing skills. “As silence has the best overall performance, it would still be advisable that people work in silence,” another reporter dryly concluded.

科学家并不认同这种看法。他们认为，听音乐会损害人们记住其他待办事项的能力；任何流行歌曲，无论音量大小，都会降低外向者和内向者的整体表现。台湾一项研究发现，带歌词的音乐与大学生专注力测试分数较低有关；其他研究也表明，带歌词的音乐会扰乱大脑处理语言的能力。另一名报道者不动声色地总结道：“既然安静环境下整体表现最佳，仍然应该建议人们安静地工作。”

The question is therefore: if headphones are so bad for productivity, why do so many people at work have them? One factor to consider is that countries like the USA have moved from a farming and manufacturing economy to a service economy, with an emphasis on jobs in offices that require higher levels of concentration, reflection and creativity. As an estimated 70 percent of office workers work in open-plan office spaces, it is more important to create one’s own enclosing bubble of sound. Lending strength to the argument for headphones at work is evidence that music relaxes our muscles, improves our mood, and may even moderately reduce blood pressure, heart rate and anxiety.

因此问题是：如果耳机如此有损工作效率，为什么职场中还有那么多人佩戴？需要考虑的一个因素是，美国等国家已经从农业和制造业经济转向服务经济，工作重点也转向需要更高专注力、思考能力和创造力的办公室岗位。据估计，70%的办公室员工在开放式办公空间工作，因此营造一个包围自己的独立声音空间变得更加重要。支持工作中戴耳机这一主张的证据还包括：音乐能放松肌肉、改善情绪，甚至可能适度降低血压、心率和焦虑程度。

The story of headphones began in 1910, when the US Navy received an odd letter written in purple ink on blue-and-pink paper. The letter writer, an eccentric inventor and repairman named Nathaniel Baldwin, from the US state of Utah, made what at the time was an astonishing claim: he had built, in his kitchen, a new kind of headset that could amplify sound. This was an opportune invention for the Navy, which asked for a sound test and then enthusiastically adopted the headsets, later called headphones, and used them in World War I for naval radio communication.

耳机的故事始于1910年。当时，美国海军（US Navy）收到一封奇怪的信，信中文字用紫色墨水写在蓝粉相间的信纸上。写信人是来自美国犹他州（Utah）的古怪发明家兼修理工 Nathaniel Baldwin。他提出了一个在当时令人震惊的说法：自己在厨房里制造了一种能够放大声音的新型头戴设备。对海军来说，这项发明来得正是时候。海军要求进行声音测试，随后热情采用这种头戴设备；它后来被称为耳机，并在第一次世界大战期间用于海军无线电通信。

The purpose of headphones is to concentrate a quiet and private sound in the ear of the listener, which is a radical departure from music’s social purpose in history. “Music, together with dance, co-evolved biologically and culturally to serve as a technology of social bonding,” Nils L. Wallin and Björn Merker wrote in The Origins of Music. Songs don’t leave behind bones, but evidence of musical notation dates back to Sumeria, 3,500 years ago, and in 1995 archaeologists discovered a bone flute in southern Europe estimated to be 44,000 years old. If music evolved as a social glue for the species, as a way to make groups and keep them together, headphones have done what writing and literacy did for language – they made music private.

耳机的用途，是把轻柔而私密的声音集中送入听者耳中，这与音乐在历史上的社会功能截然不同。Nils L. Wallin 和 Björn Merker 在《音乐的起源》（The Origins of Music）中写道：“音乐与舞蹈在生物和文化层面共同进化，成为建立社会联结的技术。”歌曲不会留下骨骼，但音乐记谱法的证据可追溯到3,500年前的苏美尔（Sumeria）；1995年，考古学家还在南欧发现了一支估计有44,000年历史的骨笛。如果音乐进化成了把人类群体组织起来并维系在一起的社会黏合剂，那么耳机对音乐所做的，就像文字和读写能力对语言所做的一样——它们使音乐变得私密。

Author and columnist Stephen Marche wrote that separation from other people is one of the first things ordinary Americans spend their money achieving. It is “a by-product of a long-standing national appetite for independence,” he said. Americans are not alone in their desire for personal independence and privacy. Marche is right; wealth can buy – and modern technology can deliver – personal independence, and it is this that people have always sought.

作家兼专栏作者 Stephen Marche 写道，普通美国人花钱实现的首要目标之一，就是与他人分隔开来。他说，这是“一个国家长期渴求独立所产生的副产品”。希望拥有个人独立和隐私的并非只有美国人。Marche 说得没错：财富可以买到、现代技术也能带来个人独立，而人们一直追求的正是这一点。

Dr Michael Bull, an expert on personal music devices from the University of Sussex in the UK, has repeatedly made the larger point that personal music devices change how we relate to public spaces. Controlling our public spaces is more important now that more people are moving from the edges of cities to live in urban centres. “With the urban space, the more it’s inhabited, the safer you feel,” Bull says. “You feel safe if you can feel people there, but you don’t want to interact with them.” Headphones create shields for wearers, separating them from other people and their surroundings. Headphones have their own rules of good manners; they are like wearing a “Do not disturb” sign. We assume that people wearing them are busy and we should respect their privacy, so now people wear them to appear busy. In fact, it is now becoming quite common for people not to listen to anything at all, but just to wear headphones.

英国苏塞克斯大学（University of Sussex）的个人音乐设备专家 Michael Bull 博士一再提出一个更宏观的观点：个人音乐设备改变了我们与公共空间相处的方式。随着越来越多人从城市边缘迁往市中心生活，掌控自己的公共空间如今变得更加重要。Bull 说：“城市空间里，住的人越多，你就越觉得安全。能感觉到人们在那里，你就会安心，但又不想与他们互动。”耳机为佩戴者形成一道屏障，把他们与其他人和周围环境隔开。使用耳机有其自身的礼貌规则；它就像挂着一块“请勿打扰”的牌子。我们会假定戴耳机的人正忙，并应尊重他们的隐私，因此如今有人戴耳机只是为了显得忙碌。事实上，只戴着耳机却什么也不听，正变得相当普遍。

However, as pointed out at the beginning of this piece, although scientists have stated that headphones are bad for productivity, people still wear them at work. It is not just that headphones create privacy out of public areas, but also that music causes people to relax and reflect and pause. The outcome of relaxation, reflection and pausing at work won’t be captured in minute-to-minute productivity metrics. What must be considered is that in moments of extreme focus, our attention radiates outward, toward the problem, rather than inward, on how to solve the problem. However, with music “When our minds are at ease, we’re more likely to direct the spotlight of attention inward,” Jonah Lehrer wrote in his book Imagine: How Creativity Works. “The answers have been there all along. We just weren’t listening.” In a crowded world, real estate is the ultimate scarce resource, and a headphone is a small invisible fence around our minds – making space, creating separation, and helping us listen to ourselves.

不过，正如本文开头指出的，虽然科学家表示耳机会损害工作效率，人们在工作时仍会佩戴。原因不只是耳机能在公共区域中创造隐私，还在于音乐使人放松、思考和暂停片刻。工作中放松、思考和暂停所产生的结果，无法反映在逐分钟计算的生产率指标中。必须考虑的是，在极度专注的时刻，我们的注意力会向外投射，集中在问题本身，而不是向内思考如何解决问题。然而，听音乐时，正如 Jonah Lehrer 在《想象：创造力如何运作》（Imagine: How Creativity Works）一书中所写的那样：“当心绪放松时，我们更可能把注意力的聚光灯转向内心。答案一直都在那里。只是我们没有倾听。”在拥挤的世界里，空间是最稀缺的资源，而耳机就像围绕心灵的一道无形小栅栏——开辟空间、制造分隔，也帮助我们倾听自己。
