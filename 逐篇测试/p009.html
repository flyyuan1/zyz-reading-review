Building a castle

建造一座城堡

Guédelon is no ordinary building site. There is no rumble of diggers or shriek of circular saws. All is peaceful and quiet, save for the continual tapping of the small army of stonemasons, the quacking of the ducks or the occasional crow of the cockerel. Slowly but surely, a brand new medieval castle is rising from the woods of Guédelon, Yonne, home to the Burgundy vineyards in the heart of France. The year is 1229—that is, for the purposes of this project: to build a prince’s château fort using only the techniques and tools of the 13th century.

盖德隆（Guédelon）绝不是普通的建筑工地。这里听不到挖掘机的轰鸣，也没有圆锯刺耳的尖啸。四周宁静安详，只有一小队石匠持续不断的敲击声、鸭子的嘎嘎声，或偶尔响起的公鸡啼鸣。在法国腹地、勃艮第（Burgundy）葡萄园所在地约讷（Yonne）的盖德隆树林中，一座崭新的中世纪城堡正缓慢而稳步地拔地而起。这里的年份是1229年——至少对这个项目而言是如此：人们要只用13世纪的技术和工具，为一位亲王建造一座设防城堡。

‘The project has four main aims,’ explains Maryline Martin, managing director for Guédelon. ‘The first is experimental archaeology – to try and verify all the theories, based on documents of the time. There’s also a social aim, because this region is very poor, with high unemployment: so it provides jobs. Then there’s tourism: the project couldn’t work without visitors because a lot of money is needed to pay people working here and to run the site. And finally, we want to open experimental archaeology to everybody – because in France, history and architecture are still very much oriented to academics.’

“这个项目有四个主要目标，”盖德隆总经理 Maryline Martin 解释说，“第一是实验考古学——尝试验证依据当时文献提出的所有理论。项目也有社会目标，因为这个地区非常贫困，失业率很高，所以它能提供就业机会。接下来是旅游业：没有游客，项目就无法运转，因为支付这里工人的工资和维持工地运行需要大量资金。最后，我们希望让每个人都能接触实验考古学——因为在法国，历史和建筑仍然在很大程度上以学术界为中心。”

The idea for the project emerged about five years ago. It was the brainchild of Michel Guyot, who bought and renovated the nearby castle of Saint-Fargeau, which was built and rebuilt between the 10th and 18th centuries. Beneath his castle were the original foundations of the 13th-century building. Guyot thought it would be fun to construct a château to the same plans, but this proved too big to attempt. So Jacques Moulin, managing director for the local historical monuments, drew up plans for a smaller castle that would still be ambitious, but realistic. Five months later, having managed to get together 600,000 Euros in funding, they began looking for a site that would provide all the materials they needed: stone, water, sand, wood, iron and clay. They found it at Guédelon.

这个项目的构想大约在5年前出现。这个构想出自 Michel Guyot。他买下并翻修了附近的圣法尔若城堡（Saint-Fargeau）；这座城堡从10世纪到18世纪历经建造与重建。在他的城堡下面，还保留着13世纪原建筑的地基。Guyot 觉得，照同一套图纸再建一座城堡会很有趣，但这个计划规模太大，难以付诸实施。因此，当地历史古迹部门的负责人 Jacques Moulin 为一座较小的城堡绘制了图纸，使项目既雄心勃勃，又切实可行。5个月后，他们成功筹得60万欧元，开始寻找一处能够提供所需全部材料的地点，包括石头、水、沙、木材、铁和黏土。他们在盖德隆找到了这样的地方。

The only major cheat so far was early on when a mechanical earthmover was brought in to raise the castle floor. Archaeologists know how it was done in the 13th century: by backbreaking, tedious work. ‘To do it by hand would have added ten years to the project,’ says Franck, one of the site’s guides. It’s still a long, painstaking task. Next to the beginnings of the east wall is the quarry, where masons extract the ferruginous limestone to build the thick castle walls. They bore lines of holes into the rocks and then they hammer metal wedges into them until the rock splits along the line. It is then hewn into bricks, and cemented together with a mortar of chalk, sand and quicklime. Quicklime is made by heating limestone to 800°C – done off-site because of the poisonous gases that come from the process. Little wonder that 13th-century lime-burners had a life expectancy of only 25–30 years. ‘The bricks are then laid horizontally for the first metre of the wall, and vertically thereafter,’ says Franck, ‘because if there was a shock from, say, a catapult, and the stones were all laid in the same way, the shockwave would shatter the wall.’

到目前为止，项目唯一一次重大的“作弊”发生在早期：当时人们使用机械推土机来抬高城堡地面。考古学家知道13世纪如何完成这项工作——靠繁重而乏味的体力劳动。“如果用手工来做，项目会再延长10年，”工地导游 Franck 说。即便如此，这仍是一项漫长而艰苦的任务。东墙初具雏形的部分旁边就是采石场，石匠在那里开采含铁石灰岩，用来修建厚重的城墙。他们在岩石上钻出一排孔，再把金属楔子锤进孔中，直到岩石沿孔线裂开。随后，岩石被凿成砖块，用白垩、沙和生石灰制成的砂浆砌在一起。制作生石灰要把石灰岩加热到800摄氏度；由于过程中会产生有毒气体，这道工序在工地外进行。难怪13世纪烧制石灰的工人预期寿命只有25至30岁。“墙体最下面1米的石砖横向铺设，再往上则竖向铺设，”Franck 说，“因为如果受到投石机之类造成的冲击，而所有石头都以同一方向铺设，冲击波就会震碎城墙。”

The scientists and craftspeople alike are learning on the job, as they don’t know exactly why or how things were done as the historical evidence suggests. One such discovery concerns the significance of the marks that masons carved into each finished stone. It was thought that these probably had superstitious meaning, but experience and archaeological research reveals three very practical uses: to establish how much each mason should be paid; to keep track of who made what, in case of any problems; and to specify where the stone should be placed in the castle reconstruction.

科学家和工匠都在工作中边做边学，因为历史证据所反映的许多做法，具体为何采用、又如何实施，他们并不完全清楚。其中一项发现涉及石匠刻在每块成品石料上的记号有何意义。人们过去认为，这些记号可能带有迷信含义；但实际经验和考古研究揭示了3种非常实际的用途：确定每名石匠应得多少报酬；记录每件石料由谁制作，以便出现问题时追查；以及标明石料应当放在重建城堡的哪个位置。

Guédelon has a scientific committee of archaeologists that discusses ideas and problems as they emerge, and will approve works and practices only when its members are sure that they are true to the 13th century. Reaching unanimity is not always easy. ‘All the big plans for the castle itself are all done but the details aren’t settled,’ says Martin. ‘So, during the winter, the site manager and I visit other castles in the region. When we see something we want to do here, we draw a sketch, make plans and show this to the committee.’

盖德隆设有一个由考古学家组成的科学委员会，随着想法和问题出现，委员会会进行讨论；只有成员确信某项工程和做法符合13世纪的真实情况，才会予以批准。达成一致并不总是容易。“城堡本身的所有总体规划都已完成，但细节尚未确定，”Martin 说，“所以到了冬天，工地经理和我会参观本地区其他城堡。看到想在这里采用的做法时，我们就画草图、制订方案，再提交给委员会。”

The castle will take at least a decade longer to build than it would have in the 13th century. ‘With the same number of workers it would have taken 15 years then,’ says Franck. ‘But we could do this too if we had only professional workers, if the site wasn’t open to the public and if we worked harder – today it’s illegal to work more than 8 hours a day, as they did at the time.’

这座城堡的建造时间至少会比13世纪时多出10年。“如果工人人数相同，当时需要15年，”Franck 说，“不过，只要我们全部使用专业工人、不向公众开放工地，并且工作得更努力，我们也能达到这个速度——当年人们每天工作超过8小时，而今天这样做是违法的。”

Workers come and go but at any one time about 40 people are working on the site. Some are students, others have ‘downshifted’ from other careers, others are local unemployed people learning skills they might use elsewhere – say, in château restorations around France. They are trained on the site as the need arises, and in the winter they may be trained in modern techniques as part of Guédelon’s social remit.

工人来来去去，但任何时候工地上大约都有40人在工作。有些是学生，有些人从其他职业转来，选择较慢的工作节奏，还有些是当地失业者，正在学习日后可能在别处使用的技能，例如参与法国各地的城堡修复。需要哪种技能时，他们就在工地接受相应培训；到了冬季，作为盖德隆社会职责的一部分，他们还可能学习现代技术。

So, indeed, what will happen when Guédelon’s magical-looking castle is complete? They might build houses around it; perhaps villages may develop, testing out 13th-century agricultural techniques. If they can build this castle, anything seems possible.

那么，盖德隆这座如同出自魔法的城堡建成后，究竟会发生什么？人们可能会在它周围建造房屋；也许还会发展出村庄，用来检验13世纪的农业技术。如果他们能建成这座城堡，似乎就没有什么不可能。
