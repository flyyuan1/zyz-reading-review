Biomimicry

仿生学

AVelcro, now commonly used instead of buttons and zippers, is probably the most famous example of ‘biomimicry’, where technologists turn to nature for inspiration. In 1948, scientist Georges de Mestral was walking through long grass when he noticed that dozens of seeds had attached themselves to his trousers. Under a microscope, de Mestral noted the hook-and-loop system the seed cases used to stick so firmly, and was inspired.

如今常被用来替代纽扣和拉链的魔术贴（Velcro），可能是“仿生学”最著名的例子；在仿生学中，技术人员会向大自然寻求灵感。1948年，科学家 Georges de Mestral 走过一片高草地时，发现数十颗种子粘在了自己的裤子上。de Mestral 在显微镜下观察到，种子外壳利用钩与环组成的结构牢牢附着在物体上，并由此获得灵感。

BUS biologist Janine Benyus wrote a book on the subject called Biomimicry: Innovation Inspired by Nature. Published in 1997, the book set off the current wave of technology modelled on nature. According to Benyus, our ancestors were practised in the art of biomimicry. ‘I think it’s an old impulse for humans to take their cues from other organisms,’ she says, referring to African tribes that found edible plants by observing the dining habits of chimpanzees. But lately we’ve become more focused on what we can make ourselves. Benyus thinks our drift away from nature started with the advent of agriculture: ‘When we broke free from the challenges of hunting and gathering and learnt to stock our cupboards, we fooled ourselves into believing that we didn’t need other organisms at all,’ she says.

美国生物学家 Janine Benyus 写过一本有关这一主题的书，名为《仿生学：受自然启发的创新》（Biomimicry: Innovation Inspired by Nature）。该书于1997年出版，引发了当前这股模仿自然的技术浪潮。Benyus 认为，我们的祖先早已熟练掌握仿生之道。她说：“我认为，人类向其他生物寻求启示，是一种由来已久的本能。”她以非洲部落为例：他们通过观察黑猩猩的进食习惯来寻找可食用植物。然而近来，我们越来越专注于自己能够制造什么。Benyus 认为，人类逐渐疏离自然始于农业出现之时。她说：“当我们摆脱狩猎采集的种种挑战，学会储备食物后，我们便误以为自己根本不再需要其他生物。”

CThe scientific, industrial, petrochemical and genetic engineering revolutions have repeatedly reinforced the idea that we are liberated from biological constraints. In recent years, however, the illusion that we are independent of nature has been shattered by the spectre of global warming and the looming end to fossil fuel supplies. Since few of us would be willing to forgo the products and services we’ve grown accustomed to – food, water, shelter, the conveniences that modern technology brings – the challenge is to meet the complex demands of civilisation within the bounds of sustainability. ‘Nature has learnt to fly, live in the depths of the ocean and craft miracle materials,’ writes Benyus. ‘Living creatures have done everything we want to do, without guzzling fossil fuel or polluting the planet. What better models could there be?’

科学革命、工业革命、石油化工革命和基因工程革命，一再强化了这样一种观念：我们已经摆脱生物条件的限制。然而近年来，全球变暖的阴影和化石燃料供应即将枯竭的前景，打破了人类可以独立于自然的幻想。我们中很少有人愿意放弃已经习惯的产品和服务——食物、水、住所，以及现代技术带来的便利。因此，当前的挑战是在可持续发展的范围内满足文明社会的复杂需求。Benyus 写道：“大自然已经学会飞行、生活在海洋深处，并制造神奇的材料。生物已经做到了我们想做的一切，却不用大量消耗化石燃料，也不会污染地球。还有比它们更好的范本吗？”

DThere are no better models, according to Tim Finnigan, a marine engineer at the University of Sydney. In his quest to harness the world’s waves and tides for renewable energy more efficiently, Finnigan has taken his cues from aquatic life. With their streamlined bodies and stiff, high tail fins, sharks convert up to 90 per cent of their body energy into forward thrust. Inspired by such efficient hydrodynamics, but turning the theory on its head, Finnigan designed his tidal stream generator: an 18-metre-long biomimetic shark tail with a fin spanning 15 metres. ‘Rather than have a body moving through a stationary fluid, we have fluid that’s moving past a stationary body,’ says Finnigan.

悉尼大学（University of Sydney）海洋工程师 Tim Finnigan 认为，没有比自然更好的范本。为了更高效地利用全球海浪和潮汐产生可再生能源，Finnigan 从水生生物身上获得了启示。鲨鱼身体呈流线形，尾鳍高而坚挺，能够把多达90%的身体能量转化为向前的推力。Finnigan 受到这种高效水动力结构的启发，却把原理反过来运用，设计出一台潮汐流发电机：一条长18米的仿生鲨鱼尾，尾鳍宽达15米。Finnigan 说：“不是让一个物体在静止的流体中移动，而是让流体从一个静止物体旁流过。”

EThen there’s Finnigan’s biomimetic forest of giant seaweed. ‘As a diver I’ve looked at the way motions occur under water in the presence of waves,’ he says. ‘I see plants that move quite dramatically and yet they never seem to be pulled out, even in the most dramatic waves.’ The trouble with conventional designs, according to Finnigan, is that they’re made to stand rigidly against the power of the ocean. ‘The structures we try to build in the ocean just never end up being strong enough to survive out there,’ says Finnigan. In the manner of aquatic plants and animals, Finnigan’s designs respond to changing current or wave conditions by reorienting to maximise energy capture. And in severe weather, to avoid a battering, his wave energy generator will lie flat against the ocean floor.

此外，Finnigan 还仿照巨型海藻设计了一片仿生“森林”。他说：“作为潜水员，我观察过海浪作用下水中的运动方式。我看到植物摆动得非常剧烈，却似乎从来不会被拔起，即使海浪最猛烈时也是如此。”Finnigan 认为，传统设计的问题在于，它们被造得十分僵硬，试图正面抵抗海洋的力量。Finnigan 说：“我们设法建在海里的结构，到头来总是不够坚固，无法在那里完好地保留下来。”Finnigan 的设计模仿水生动植物，会根据水流或海浪条件的变化调整方向，以最大限度地获取能量。天气恶劣时，为避免遭受冲击，他的海浪能发电机会平卧在海床上。

FWhile conventional architects were designing buildings dependent on expensive air conditioning systems, when Mick Pearce tried to do the same, he struck a problem. ‘We were building office blocks for a client in Zimbabwe and we ran out of funds. So we looked for ways to make a building without traditional air conditioning.’ One day, driving through the grasslands, he saw a large mound created by termites, the ant-like insect common in Africa. He noticed that air entering at the base of the mound was mixed with water drawn from subterranean levels by the termites, causing evaporative cooling. Pearce wanted to reproduce this principle but he needed an alternative system, and in his building massive fans were employed at the base of the structure to lower the temperature of the circulating air. Pearce’s termite-inspired cooling system cut energy use to 10 per cent of a similar air-conditioned building.

传统建筑师通常会设计依赖昂贵空调系统的建筑；Mick Pearce 尝试采用同样做法时，却遇到了问题。他说：“我们当时正在津巴布韦（Zimbabwe）为一名客户建造办公楼，资金却用完了。因此，我们开始寻找不用传统空调也能建成大楼的方法。”一天，他驾车穿过草原时，看见一个由白蚁筑成的大土丘；白蚁是一种在非洲很常见、外形像蚂蚁的昆虫。他注意到，从土丘底部进入的空气会与白蚁从地下带上来的水分混合，从而通过蒸发降低温度。Pearce 想复制这一原理，但需要采用另一套系统，于是他在建筑底部安装了大型风扇，用来降低循环空气的温度。Pearce 这套受白蚁启发的冷却系统，把能源使用量降至同类空调建筑的10%。

GPhotosynthesis is the process by which green plants use energy from the sun to convert water and carbon dioxide into carbohydrates and oxygen. Plants can manage it with humbling ease. But, as the 40 researchers from 11 institutes who have collaborated to form the Australian Artificial Photosynthesis Network have realised, it is very complex. However, there is one aspect working in our favour. In nature, environmental variables like temperature, carbon dioxide and light availability limit the rate of photosynthesis. In a laboratory these variables can be optimized. ‘We don’t have to cope with drought or frost. We can work with a highly controlled, specified set of conditions,’ says Tom Collings, the group’s spokesman.

光合作用是绿色植物利用太阳能，把水和二氧化碳转化为碳水化合物和氧气的过程。植物完成这一过程轻而易举，令人自叹不如。然而，11家机构的40名研究人员合作组成了澳大利亚人工光合作用网络（Australian Artificial Photosynthesis Network），他们已经认识到，这一过程非常复杂。不过，有一个方面对我们有利。在自然环境中，温度、二氧化碳和光照条件等环境变量会限制光合作用的速度；在实验室里，这些变量可以得到优化。该团队的发言人 Tom Collings 说：“我们不必应对干旱或霜冻，可以在一套受到高度控制、明确规定的条件下开展工作。”
