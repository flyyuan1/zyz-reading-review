Surviving city life

城市生存之道

Although the colonisation of Australia profoundly affected the continent’s natural environment, many plants and animals have actually flourished since European settlement. Some even thrive in the concrete jungle of Australia’s biggest city, Sydney.

尽管澳大利亚的殖民过程深刻影响了这片大陆的自然环境，许多动植物实际上却在欧洲人定居后繁盛起来。有些甚至能在澳大利亚最大城市悉尼的混凝土丛林中繁衍生息。

AEcologists often prefer to study plants and animals in exotic locations, but a growing number have turned their attention to the complex interactions of the wildlife that inhabits concrete jungles. Inner-city Sydney is the laboratory of choice for a number of ecologists, and their research is timely. More than half the world’s human population resides in cities, and urban development continues to increase all over the world. By 2030, the United Nations projects five billion people will live in cities. Associate Professor Dieter Hochuli, a biologist at the University of Sydney, believes that ‘we need to understand how cities are changing the ecology of the systems they are built on, and how plants and animals are adapting to them’.

生态学家往往更喜欢在异域地点研究动植物，但越来越多的人开始关注生活在混凝土丛林中的野生生物之间复杂的相互作用。悉尼市中心是多位生态学家首选的实验场，而他们的研究正逢其时。全球一半以上的人口居住在城市，世界各地的城市开发仍在不断扩大。联合国（United Nations）预计，到2030年将有50亿人生活在城市。悉尼大学（University of Sydney）生物学家、副教授 Dieter Hochuli 认为：“我们需要了解城市如何改变其所在生态系统的生态状况，以及动植物如何适应城市。”

BIf any species has learnt to thrive in an urban environment, it’s the native white ibis. A strange long-legged bird with a bow-shaped beak, it is known as the ‘garbage turkey’. The bird’s reputation for digging through inner-city bins and scavenging street garbage has not endeared it to the public. The white ibis began its move to the city in the 1970s, when large parts of its natural habitat of inland wetlands became degraded due to years of low rainfall. ‘The species is a wetland forager,’ wildlife officer John Martin from Sydney’s Royal Botanic Gardens says. ‘Now it happily forages in city parks and landfill.’ During the peak of its spring breeding season, there are more than 9 000 of these birds in Sydney.

如果说有哪个物种学会了在城市环境中繁盛生存，那就是本地白鹮（white ibis）。这种长腿鸟外形奇特，长着弓形鸟喙，被称为“垃圾火鸡”。它经常翻找市中心的垃圾箱，在街头垃圾中觅食，因此并不讨公众喜欢。20世纪70年代，内陆湿地这一白鹮天然栖息地的大部分区域因多年降雨偏少而退化，白鹮便开始迁往城市。“这个物种在湿地觅食，”悉尼皇家植物园（Royal Botanic Gardens）的野生动物工作人员 John Martin 说；“如今，它很乐意在城市公园和填埋场里找食物。”春季繁殖高峰时，悉尼有9000多只这种鸟。

CSpecimens at Sydney’s Australian Museum show that the city’s overall bird life has changed dramatically over the two centuries since colonisation. Prior to urban development, Sydney’s native bushland was populated by large numbers of small insect-eating birds, such as the superb fairy-wren and the eastern yellow robin. Today, homeowners prefer to landscape their backyards with tall trees and manicured lawns—an environment that provides little protection for small birds. But one bird’s trash is another’s treasure. Gardens filled with flowering plants and fruit trees favour omnivorous birds such as currawongs, bowerbirds and the city’s most despised resident – the noisy miner. ‘They’re a real winner in cities,’ Australian Museum ornithologist Richard Major says. ‘The predominant driver in the decline of small birds is that we’ve made a suitable environment for native noisy miners.’

悉尼澳大利亚博物馆（Australian Museum）收藏的标本表明，殖民以来的两个世纪中，全市鸟类构成发生了巨大变化。城市开发以前，悉尼本地灌木林里生活着大量以昆虫为食的小型鸟类，如华丽细尾鹩莺（superb fairy-wren）和黄鸲鹟（eastern yellow robin）。如今，房主喜欢用高大树木和修剪整齐的草坪布置后院，而这种环境几乎不能保护小型鸟类。不过，对一种鸟不利的环境，对另一种鸟却可能是宝地。种满开花植物和果树的花园，更有利于钟鹊（currawongs）、园丁鸟（bowerbirds），以及全市最遭人厌恶的居民——黑额矿吸蜜鸟（noisy miner）——等杂食性鸟类。澳大利亚博物馆鸟类学家 Richard Major 说：“它们是城市里的真正赢家。小型鸟类减少的首要原因，是我们为本地黑额矿吸蜜鸟营造出了适宜环境。”

DMany invertebrates, such as the golden orb-weaver spider and the blue triangle butterfly, also relish living in the city. The golden orb spiders in Sydney are fatter and fitter than species found elsewhere, and Professor Hochuli and his team are trying to understand why. ‘We’re trying to determine whether it’s more food or the urban heat – as it’s up to four degrees warmer in the city.’ Hochuli has also found some varieties of ant more at home in the city. ‘The green ant, known for its painful bite, will build a nest where there is space and food, regardless of whether it’s a backyard or a sports oval.’ ‘It’s remarkable how many things persist in city environments,’ he says. The decline in birds that eat small invertebrates means these populations grow unchecked, allowing them to chew their way through the foliage of the city’s trees.

许多无脊椎动物也很喜欢城市生活，例如金圆蛛（golden orb-weaver spider）和蓝三角蝶（blue triangle butterfly）。悉尼的金圆蛛比其他地方的同类更肥壮、更健康，Hochuli 教授和团队正试图弄清原因。“我们想确定，这是因为食物更多，还是因为城市热效应——城市温度最高可高出4度。”Hochuli 还发现，一些蚂蚁种类在城市里生活得更自在。“绿蚁（green ant）以叮咬疼痛著称；只要有空间和食物，它就会筑巢，不管地点是后院还是运动场。”他说：“这么多生物能在城市环境中延续下来，令人惊叹。”捕食小型无脊椎动物的鸟类减少，意味着这些无脊椎动物的数量会不受控制地增长，任由它们啃食城市树木的叶片。

EWhile some species can survive in relatively small areas, mammals have been confined to patches of bushland scattered around Sydney and its nearby national parks. However, the rabbit-sized, long-nosed bandicoot has discovered the advantages of venturing out to grassy suburban backyards and gardens. ‘They forage for invertebrates in the grass and like the surrounding habitat to nest and escape from predators,’ Catherine Price, a research associate at the University of Sydney, says. Dr Price is trying to understand what encourages the little mammal into urban environments. ‘We don’t know if it’s an overflow from the park, or if they’ve got particular survival traits that allow them to evade dogs and cats, and use the urban habitat that benefits them,’ she says.

有些物种可以在相对狭小的区域生存，但哺乳动物一直局限在散布于悉尼各处及附近国家公园的灌木林斑块中。不过，兔子大小的长鼻袋狸（long-nosed bandicoot）发现，走出灌木林，前往郊区长草的后院和花园也有好处。悉尼大学研究员 Catherine Price 说：“它们在草地上寻找无脊椎动物，并喜欢利用周边栖息地筑巢和躲避捕食者。”Price 博士正试图了解，是什么吸引这种小型哺乳动物进入城市环境。她说：“我们不知道，它们只是从公园扩散出来，还是拥有某些特殊生存特征，可以避开狗和猫，并利用对自己有利的城市栖息地。”

FIt’s not just native wildlife that has sought comfort in city living. Non-native species such as black rodents, cockroaches and foxes have developed survival strategies too. But weeds are the pest that has gained the most advantage. ‘In residential Sydney there would not be a single area of remnant bushland not infested by introduced plant life,’ Michelle Leishman, a Macquarie University plant biologist, says. Over 20 years, Leishman and her colleagues have shown how Sydney’s huge stretches of impermeable concrete, together with the storm-water system, have helped weeds infiltrate the few remaining pockets of bushland. As rain washes over backyards and roadways, it collects chemicals which enter the storm-water system, where they are piped to the edges of bushland. The nutrient-rich water seeps into the earth, favouring the many exotic species that ‘live fast and die young,’ Leishman says. Indigenous plants prefer low-fertility soil and struggle to cope with one that is more fertile.

在城市生活中寻求安逸的，不只有本地野生动物。黑色啮齿动物、蟑螂和狐狸等外来物种也发展出了生存策略。不过，从中获益最多的有害生物是杂草。麦考瑞大学（Macquarie University）植物生物学家 Michelle Leishman 说：“在悉尼住宅区，残存的灌木林没有一处不受外来植物侵扰。”在20年的研究中，Leishman 和同事揭示了悉尼大片不透水的混凝土地面和雨水排放系统如何帮助杂草侵入仅存的少数灌木林。雨水流过后院和道路时会带走化学物质，进入雨水排放系统，再经管道流到灌木林边缘。Leishman 说，富含养分的水渗入土壤，有利于许多“长得快、死得早”的外来物种。本土植物偏好肥力低的土壤，很难适应肥力更高的环境。
