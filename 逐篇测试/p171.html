Marketing and Mind Control

营销与思维控制

How marketing and advertising appeal to the associative nature of the brain

营销与广告如何利用大脑的联想特性

While there had been a long tradition of giving rings as a commitment to marry, the custom of giving diamond engagement rings was in large part manufactured by one of the most effective marketing campaigns in history. In the early 1900s, diamond sales were declining, posing a serious problem for the company that essentially had control over the diamond market. In 1938, this company hired an advertising agency, which proposed reshaping social attitudes toward diamonds. As well as magazines showing film stars draped in diamonds, the agency arranged for movies to incorporate diamond engagement rings into their plots. The campaign culminated with the slogan: ‘A diamond is forever.’

虽然以戒指作为婚约信物的传统由来已久，但赠送钻石订婚戒指的习俗，在很大程度上是由历史上最成功的营销活动之一塑造出来的。20世纪初，钻石销量不断下滑，这给一家实际上掌控着钻石市场的公司带来了严重问题。1938年，这家公司聘请了一家广告代理机构，后者提出要重塑社会对钻石的看法。除了让杂志刊登影星佩戴大量钻石饰品的照片，这家机构还安排电影把钻石订婚戒指融入剧情。这场营销活动最终以“钻石恒久远”这句广告语达到高潮。

At the time, the approach was unique. Rather than pushing a brand, the objective was to promote diamonds as the symbol of everlasting love. This was achieved by exploiting the associative nature of the brain: associating neurons¹ activated by the concept of ‘love’ with neurons that encoded the concept of ‘diamonds’. By 1941, diamond sales had increased by 55%.

在当时，这种做法别具一格。它的目的并非推销某个品牌，而是把钻石宣传为永恒爱情的象征。为此，广告利用了大脑的联想特性：把被“爱情”这一概念激活的神经元¹，与编码“钻石”这一概念的神经元联系起来。到1941年，钻石销量已增长55%。

Advertising comes in many forms, from blatant neon signs to subtly embedded products in movies. In each case, the goal is to mould our habits, desires and opinions. Our visual system is targeted by an avalanche of information on the internet, street posters, billboards and in movie theatres. Our auditory system submits to catchy radio jingles and telemarketers. More surreptitiously, our olfactory system is targeted by variations of vanilla and citrus perfumes aimed at enticing us to linger in a retail outlet.

广告有许多形式，既有醒目的霓虹灯招牌，也有电影里不露痕迹的产品植入。无论采取哪种形式，其目的都是塑造我们的习惯、欲望和观念。互联网上、街头海报上、广告牌上以及电影院里的海量信息，都以我们的视觉系统为目标。我们的听觉系统则受到朗朗上口的广播广告歌和电话推销的影响。更隐蔽的做法是利用各种香草和柑橘香味来影响我们的嗅觉，吸引我们在商店里多停留一会儿。

It is difficult to measure how effective these campaigns are, but as in the ‘A diamond is forever’ campaign, they can be so successful that they change the fabric of our culture. In the case of bottled water, we are swayed by advertising into paying for something that we can obtain for free. Most people cannot distinguish bottled from tap water, much less between brands of bottled water, which is why you rarely hear of a bottled water company proposing a blind taste test.

这些营销活动究竟有多大效果，很难衡量；但就像“钻石恒久远”这场活动一样，它们可能成功到足以改变我们的文化面貌。以瓶装水为例，我们会受广告影响，花钱购买本来能够免费获得的东西。大多数人分不清瓶装水与自来水，更不用说不同品牌的瓶装水了；这就是为什么你很少听到瓶装水公司提议进行盲测。

So why is marketing such an effective mind-control technique? It is interesting to consider whether other animals exhibit anything analogous to humans’ susceptibility to advertising. If we provide a lab rat with two types of cereal, it will consume approximately the same amount of each. However, if we put that rat with another rat that spent its day eating just one type, when faced with a choice, our rat will now show a preference for the same type as the other rat was eating. Psychologists call this ‘socially transmitted food preference’.

那么，营销为什么是一种如此有效的思维控制手段呢？人类容易受广告影响；其他动物是否也会表现出与之类似的易受影响倾向，这是个值得思考的问题。如果给一只实验鼠提供两种谷物，它摄入这两种食物的量会大致相同。然而，如果把这只鼠与另一只当天只吃过一种谷物的鼠放在一起，那么再次面对选择时，前一只鼠就会偏爱另一只鼠所吃的那种谷物。心理学家把这种现象称为“经由同类传递的食物偏好”。

What many regard as the first documented examples of cultural learning in primates started with a clever monkey that lived in a colony of Japanese monkeys on the island of Koshima. She began taking her dirt-covered sweet potatoes to the river to wash them before eating them. Upon seeing this, a few other open-minded monkeys picked up on the idea. Potato washing then spread from monkey to monkey and, over the course of a few years, most monkeys were eating clean potatoes. Humans are clearly not the only animals to engage in imitation and social learning.

许多人认为，灵长类动物最早有记录的文化学习案例，始于一只聪明的猴子；它生活在 Koshima 岛的一个日本猴群中。它开始把沾满泥土的红薯拿到河里洗干净后再吃。看到这一做法后，另外几只愿意接受新事物的猴子也学会了。随后，洗红薯的做法在猴群中一只传给另一只；几年下来，大多数猴子都吃上了干净的红薯。显然，会进行模仿和社会学习的动物不只有人类。

Learning by observation can be an extraordinarily valuable brain feature. This is how we learn to communicate and perform motor skills as well as deal with many everyday problems. For example, a newcomer struggling to purchase tickets and navigate the subway system in a foreign city may step back to learn from the people nearby. Humans and other primates exhibit multiple forms of imitative learning and this is called cultural transmission.

通过观察来学习，是大脑一种能够发挥极大作用的能力。我们正是通过这种方式学会交流、掌握动作技能，以及应对许多日常问题。例如，一个初到异国城市、正为买票和搭乘地铁而犯难的人，可以先退到一旁，向周围的人学习。人类和其他灵长类动物会表现出多种形式的模仿学习，这被称为文化传递。

A component of advertising relies on the marketer’s ability to tap into the brain’s propensity for imitation. Anybody who has watched TV knows advertisements are disproportionately populated with attractive, successful-looking individuals. If we are going to imitate someone, we are more inclined to imitate those who appear to be popular and appealing.

广告之所以能发挥作用，部分原因在于营销人员能够利用大脑的模仿倾向。任何看过电视的人都知道，广告中有吸引力、看起来很成功的人物，所占比例异常高。如果我们要模仿某个人，就更倾向于模仿那些看起来受欢迎、有魅力的人。

Although not all researchers are convinced by the findings, a number of studies indicate that some animals also imitate dominant members of their group. Primatologist Frans de Waal provides anecdotal evidence of preferential imitation among chimpanzees. He noted that in one particular group the dominant male was hurt and was limping as a result. Soon juvenile males of the group started imitating his limp, a form of flattery that would have been unlikely if a non-dominant male had been injured.

虽然并非所有研究人员都信服这些发现，但多项研究表明，一些动物也会模仿群体中占支配地位的成员。灵长类动物学家 Frans de Waal 提供了黑猩猩有选择地模仿同伴的个案证据。他注意到，在某个群体中，占支配地位的雄性黑猩猩受了伤，因此走路一瘸一拐。不久，群体中的年幼雄性便开始模仿它跛行；这是一种讨好行为，如果受伤的是一只不占支配地位的雄性，就不太可能出现这种情况。

Imitation is undoubtedly an invaluable ability, but often our propensity to imitate generalises indiscriminately, leading to poor decisions. When athlete Dick Fosbury revolutionised the high jump by jumping over the bar backward in 1968, imitators obviously copied his jumping style, not his brand of sports shoes. However, today, sportspeople appear in advertisements asking us to buy the laptops or sports drinks that they promote. Rationally, we know these people’s success did not depend on these products, so it seems our propensity to purchase products relates more to neural programs that evolved to encourage imitation of those further up the social ladder.

模仿无疑是一种极其宝贵的能力，但我们往往会把这种倾向不加分辨地用到各种事情上，从而作出糟糕的决定。1968年，运动员 Dick Fosbury 以背向横杆的姿势越杆，革新了跳高技术；模仿者显然学的是他的跳高动作，而不是他穿的运动鞋品牌。然而，如今运动员会出现在广告中，劝我们购买他们推广的笔记本电脑或运动饮料。从理性上说，我们知道这些人的成功并不依赖这些产品；因此，我们购买产品的倾向，似乎更多地与在进化中形成的神经机制有关，这些机制会促使我们模仿社会地位比自己高的人。

Today, companies engage in stealth marketing campaigns in which people are paid to frequent bars or websites to covertly promote certain products. Companies also perform studies in which they track the eye movements of people viewing displays, and carefully craft names, packages and jingles associated with their products.

如今，公司会开展隐性营销活动，付钱雇人经常光顾酒吧或访问网站，暗中推广某些产品。公司还会开展研究，追踪人们观看展示内容时的眼球运动，并精心设计与产品相关的名称、包装和广告歌。

While we may like to believe that manipulation on a grand scale would not be possible, that’s not to say that advertising is innately harmful. To the contrary, the marketing of products or ideas is essential to human culture. The point is that we should ensure our choices reflect our actual goals and desires, and we must distinguish between the dissemination of information which is for our own good, and our manipulation for the benefit of companies.

虽然我们或许愿意相信，大规模操控人们是不可能的，但这并不意味着广告本身就有害。相反，产品或观念的营销对人类文化至关重要。关键在于，我们应确保自己的选择反映真实的目标和愿望，并且必须分清两种情况：一种是传播对我们有益的信息，另一种是为了公司的利益而操控我们。

¹ neurons: impulse-conducting cells that are the functional units of the nervous system

¹ 神经元：传导神经冲动的细胞，是神经系统的功能单位。
