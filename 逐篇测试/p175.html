Goya's portraits

Goya的肖像画

Francisco de Goya, 1746–1828, was a highly-skilled Spanish painter whose portraits can be found in many of the major galleries of the world

Francisco de Goya（1746–1828）是一位技艺高超的西班牙画家，世界许多主要美术馆都收藏了他的肖像画。

AThere are, according to current scholarship, 160 existing portraits by Francisco de Goya, about a third of his painted output. The real number, though, is much greater, since there are no pure landscapes in Goya's work. He was not interested in paintings of nature – everything he ever did deals with people. In the work of no other great portraitist are a nation's people, folk traditions and superstitions so comprehensively and relentlessly captured. Put his work together – the public and private paintings and the personal drawings and sketches – and what you have is a comprehensive portrait of Spain itself. His subject was a good one since Goya lived in fascinating times: his lifetime was a period that saw Spain pass through enormous changes. People were the constant during these unstable times and he recorded both the faces of those who mattered to Spain and to him – often they were one and the same.

根据目前的研究，Francisco de Goya有160幅肖像画存世，约占其绘画作品的三分之一。不过，若从更广的意义上看，数量远不止这些，因为Goya的作品中没有纯粹的风景画。他对描绘自然并不感兴趣；他创作的所有作品都关乎人。没有哪位其他肖像画大师，像他这样全面而执着地刻画一个国家的人民、民俗传统和迷信观念。把他的作品汇集起来，包括为公众和私人创作的绘画，以及个人素描和速写，你看到的就是一幅完整的西班牙社会肖像。他所选的主题很有价值，因为Goya生活在一个引人入胜的时代：在他的一生中，西班牙经历了巨大的变化。在这些动荡的岁月里，人始终是贯穿其中的主题；他记录了那些对西班牙、对他本人都很重要的人物的面容，而这两类人往往重合。

BHis work as a portraitist has its origins in the 45 tapestry cartoons he painted for the Royal Tapestry Factory in Madrid after he moved there in 1775. Although his designs don't show portraits, they do show types of people – the residents of the city who gave Madrid its street swagger: peasants and rich men, courting couples, singers, hunters, children and young men and women playing blind man's buff or tossing a mannequin into the air. The pictures use a limited range of colours, and the faces are individualised without being those of particular people. But in them Goya practised poses and groupings and a way of handling light on and around figures that was to be invaluable.

他的肖像画创作起源于1775年迁居马德里后，为当地皇家挂毯工厂绘制的45幅挂毯底稿。这些设计虽然不是肖像画，却展现了各类人物，也就是让马德里街头充满生气的市民：农民和富人、恋爱中的情侣、歌手、猎人、孩子，以及玩蒙眼捉人或把人偶抛向空中的年轻男女。画面使用的色彩种类有限，人物面容各具特点，却并不对应特定的真人。不过，Goya通过这些画练习了人物姿态、组合，以及处理人物身上和周围光线的方法；这些经验后来成为宝贵的财富。

CThe tapestry designs show social roles rather than the people inhabiting them, but when it came to painting portraits, Goya would turn this on its head. His greatest strength as a portraitist is that regardless of the status of the sitter, be they a king or a queen, a soldier, a priest or writer, it was the person he showed first and their position second. It was this trait, most apparent in his royal portraiture, which has led him to be seen as satirising the Bourbon monarchy rather than as a painter who depicted what he saw without using the usual flattering conventions. In the art critic Robert Hughes's phrase he did not 'pay reflexive homage to authority' but instead walked a fine line between respect and truth.

挂毯设计展现的是社会角色，而不是担当这些角色的具体个人；但在画肖像时，Goya将这个次序反了过来。作为肖像画家，他最大的长处是：无论模特身份如何，是国王、王后、士兵、牧师还是作家，他首先表现这个人本身，其次才是其地位。这一特点在王室肖像中最为明显，因此人们常认为他在讽刺波旁王朝，而没有把他视为一个只是描绘眼前所见、不采用惯常奉承手法的画家。借用艺术评论家Robert Hughes的说法，他不会“习惯性地向权势致敬”，而是在尊重与真实之间谨慎地把握分寸。

DGoya's success was rapid: in 1785 he was made deputy director of painting at the Royal Academy (his main message to his students was subversive – 'there are no rules in painting') and in 1786 he became the official court painter, which involved painting 'works required for royal service'. He immediately spent some of his generous salary on a two-wheeled horse carriage (one of only three in Madrid at the time) which he crashed on his first outing. His self-confidence untouched, he wrote to his childhood friend Martin Zapater: “I have now established an enviable way of living: I do not wait on anyone in antechambers, and if anyone wants anything from me they must come to me. I have made myself more in demand and unless it is a person of rank or at the request of a friend I would do nothing for anyone...”

Goya很快就取得了成功：1785年，他获任皇家学院绘画部副主任——他向学生传达的主要观念颇具颠覆性：“绘画没有规则”；1786年，他又成为正式的宫廷画师，负责绘制“王室所需的作品”。他立刻用丰厚薪水中的一部分买了一辆双轮马车，当时整个马德里只有三辆，但第一次出行就撞坏了。他的自信毫未受损，写信给童年好友Martin Zapater说：“我现在过上了令人羡慕的生活：我不用在前厅等候任何人，谁有事求我，就必须来找我。我让自己变得更受欢迎；除非对方有身份，或是朋友相托，否则我不会为任何人做什么……”

EPart of the reason for his success was that, unlike the 18th-century British artist Gainsborough, for example, he did not resent portraiture as an economically necessary chore that ate away at the time he could devote to higher art. Portraiture fascinated him; it was, as he put it, part of that 'sacred science which requires so much study', and his portraits reveal a fully engaged artist. There is hardly any repetition in his poses (he drew on prints of English portraits for inspiration). He worked hard to give his sitters in repose a sense of latent movement and he defined them by the careful depiction of the space around them.

他成功的部分原因在于，他不像18世纪英国画家Gainsborough等人那样，把画肖像视为迫于生计的苦差事，觉得它侵占了本可投入更高艺术追求的时间。肖像画令他着迷；用他的话说，这是那门“需要潜心研究的神圣学问”的一部分，而他的肖像画也展现出一位全心投入的艺术家。他笔下的人物姿态几乎不重复；他也从英国肖像画的版画复制品中汲取灵感。他努力让静坐的模特带有一种将动未动的感觉，并通过精心描绘人物周围的空间来塑造人物。

F“My work is very simple. My art reveals idealism and truth,” Goya claimed. The truth though is always more apparent than the idealism. With the portrait of Charles IV and the royal family of 1800, one of the most important commissions of his career, Goya rejected entirely the grandeur of majesty to portray the assembled Bourbons as they appeared – a motley grouping in fancy clothes. In fact, a French visitor to the Spanish court had previously likened Charles and his queen Maria Luisa to a butcher and his wife. Goya may have been a political liberal but he wouldn't have jeopardised his hard-won new position as first court painter in order to score political points at the expense of his patrons. That his sitters didn't regard the portrait as unflattering was evidenced by succeeding royal commissions, not least his 1815 portrait of Ferdinand VII. Ferdinand himself was physically unprepossessing, and had a reputation for being tyrannical, vicious and small-minded, so that is exactly how Goya shows him. The king, however, looking in Goya's particular mirror, saw no such characteristics staring back at him.

Goya曾说：“我的创作很简单。我的艺术表现理想与真实。”不过，其中的真实总是比理想更为鲜明。1800年为Charles IV及其王室家族绘制的肖像，是他职业生涯中最重要的委托之一；在这幅画里，Goya完全抛开了帝王应有的庄严气派，按眼前所见描绘聚在一起的波旁家族成员——一群穿着华服、各具模样的人。事实上，一位到访西班牙宫廷的法国人此前曾把Charles和王后Maria Luisa比作屠夫和他的妻子。Goya在政治上或许倾向自由主义，但他不会为了借讥讽赞助人来表达政治立场，就危及自己来之不易的新职位——首席宫廷画师。随后的王室委托表明，画中人并不认为这些肖像有损自己的形象，其中尤其值得一提的是他1815年为Ferdinand VII所画的肖像。Ferdinand本人外貌平平，又以专横、恶毒、心胸狭隘著称，Goya笔下呈现的正是这样一个人。然而，国王望着Goya这面独特“镜子”里的自己，却完全没有看到这些特征。

GThe private side of Goya's portraiture was a world away from the public. The contrast between the Ferdinand portrait and the remarkable 1820 double portrait showing Goya with Dr Arrieta represents a moral version of three aspects of the human condition. In this portrait, the 73-year-old painter is suffering from a debilitating illness and is being tended by his doctor. If Ferdinand represents human malignity and Goya its frailty then Arrieta stands for man's innate kindness. While not all his portraits are successful (some have a doll-like stiffness), this is what Goya at his best could do: in the guise of representing individuals he showed, without judging, man as a universal being.

Goya为私人创作的肖像，与那些面向公众的作品截然不同。将Ferdinand的肖像，与1820年那幅出色的Goya和Arrieta医生双人肖像相对照，就能从道德层面看到人类处境的三个侧面。在这幅双人肖像中，73岁的画家正患着使他衰弱的疾病，由医生照料。如果说Ferdinand代表人的恶意，Goya代表人的脆弱，那么Arrieta代表的就是人性中与生俱来的善良。虽然他的肖像画并非每幅都成功，有些像玩偶一样僵硬，但这幅画展示了Goya在最佳状态下所能达到的境界：以描绘具体个人的形式，不加评判地呈现普遍的人性。
