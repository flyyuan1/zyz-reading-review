Whale Culture

鲸类文化

AMost social scientists stubbornly resist the idea that animals have culture. Even such advanced cetacean mammals as whales and dolphins clearly don’t have art, literature, or architecture. But patient observation over many years has begun to reveal behaviours that can only be learnt from other whales. And that, say whale biologists, constitutes culture.

大多数社会科学家固执地不肯接受动物拥有文化这一观点。即便是鲸和海豚这些如此发达的鲸类哺乳动物，显然也没有艺术、文学或建筑。然而，多年来耐心的观察开始揭示出一些只能从其他鲸那里学会的行为。鲸类生物学家说，这就构成了文化。

BSo far, humpback and killer whales provide the best evidence of culture in cetaceans, and the song of the male humpback is among the most striking examples. Humpback populations in different oceans sing different songs, but within the same ocean they all stick to the same one. However, during the breeding season the sounds change, as it appears that females are drawn to novel songs. One male might add an extra set of groans; another might drop a series of grunts. Soon all the other males have altered their own rendition to incorporate the changes until they are once again singing the same song. Since this occurs among thousands of whales spread across a vast part of the planet, the change cannot be in response to any factor in the animal’s environment. The latest version of the song can be learnt only from other whales – almost certainly by imitation.

迄今为止，座头鲸和虎鲸提供了鲸类拥有文化的最佳证据，而雄性座头鲸的歌声就是最引人注目的例子之一。不同海域的座头鲸种群会唱不同的歌，但在同一海域内，它们全都唱同一首。不过，在繁殖季节，歌声会发生变化，因为雌鲸似乎会被新颖的歌声吸引。一头雄鲸可能会加上一组额外的低吟，另一头则可能删去一连串短促的咕哝声。很快，其他所有雄鲸都会改变自己的唱法，把这些变化融入其中，直到它们再次唱起同一首歌。由于这种变化发生在分布于地球广大区域的数千头鲸之间，因此不可能是对动物所处环境中某个因素的反应。最新版的歌只能从其他鲸那里学来——几乎可以肯定是通过模仿。

CCulture plays an even bigger part in the life of killer whales. Nowhere is this more obvious than along the north-west coast of America, where killer whales are split into two distinct populations – ‘residents’ and ‘transients’. They live in the same stretch of water, but they don’t mingle. In effect, they belong to two quite separate cultures. Residents live in stable groups, or ‘pods’, made up of two or three mothers and their offspring – perhaps 20 whales in all. Calves stay with their mothers throughout adulthood, and in many years of observation no one has ever seen a whale switch pods. Transients travel in smaller, more changeable groups of between three and six.

文化在虎鲸生活中所起的作用甚至更大。这一点在美洲西北海岸表现得最为明显，那里的虎鲸分为两个截然不同的种群——“定居型”和“过境型”。它们生活在同一片水域，却不相往来。实际上，它们属于两种完全不同的文化。定居型虎鲸生活在稳定的群体，即“鲸群”中；每个鲸群由两三头母鲸及其后代组成，总数或许约为20头。幼鲸成年后仍一直与母亲共同生活，而且经过多年的观察，从未有人见过哪头鲸转入另一个鲸群。过境型虎鲸则以更小、成员变化更频繁的群体活动，每群有3至6头。

DOne of the most obvious distinctions between the transient and resident societies is the way they impart information. Killer whales detect prey with a range of echo-locating clicks, but converse with a vocabulary of squeaks, whistles and whines. Transients have only a few such calls, and all transient societies share the same ones. Residents have a much more extensive repertoire, and each family group has its own unique and distinctive set of calls. Despite regular interaction between them, each resident pod sticks firmly to its own dialect. Research shows these dialects are maintained for at least 40 years.

过境型与定居型虎鲸社会之间最明显的区别之一，在于它们传递信息的方式。虎鲸利用一系列回声定位的咔嗒声探测猎物，却用由吱叫、哨音和呜咽声构成的“词汇”交流。过境型虎鲸只有少数几种这样的叫声，而且所有过境型群体使用的都相同。定居型虎鲸的叫声种类丰富得多，每个家族群体都有自己独特而鲜明的一套叫声。尽管不同鲸群之间经常互动，每个定居型鲸群仍会严格坚持自己的方言。研究表明，这些方言至少能保持40年。

ETo qualify as part of killer whale culture, dialects must be learnt from other members of the pod. Animals with different dialects share the same waters, so the variation can’t be a product of physical environment. ‘And we can throw out the notion that dialects are inherited,’ says Lance Barrett-Lennard of the University of British Columbia. He has spent the past seven years analysing DNA from 270 whales. His paternity tests reveal that female killer whales invariably attract mates from outside their own pod – males with a very different dialect. If dialects were programmed by genetics, call patterns from both father and mother would be passed on to the calf. ‘A calf uses the calls of its maternal pod very precisely. There’s no input from the father,’ says Barrett-Lennard.

若要算作虎鲸文化的一部分，方言就必须是从鲸群其他成员那里学来的。使用不同方言的虎鲸生活在同一片水域，因此这种差异不可能由自然环境造成。不列颠哥伦比亚大学的 Lance Barrett-Lennard 说：“我们也可以排除方言由遗传而来的说法。”过去7年里，他一直在分析270头鲸的DNA。他的亲子鉴定显示，雌性虎鲸总是会吸引本鲸群以外的配偶——也就是方言截然不同的雄鲸。如果方言由基因决定，父母双方的叫声模式都会传给幼鲸。Barrett-Lennard 说：“幼鲸会非常准确地使用母系鲸群的叫声，父亲对此没有任何影响。”

FThe question still remains – is this culture? It is, according to Frans de Waal of Emory University in Atlanta, who argues that culture is just another biological adaptation that has evolved in many creatures. One benefit of viewing culture in this way is that you can start to understand how and why it might have arisen in these creatures. Whales have several biological attributes that give them an advantage in social learning. Apart from their advanced mental abilities, they are adept at recognising sounds: ideal for communicating in the marine environment. Many species spend years rearing their offspring, and live in small, stable, multi-generational societies, a social system that provides ample opportunity for teaching and learning.

但问题依然存在——这算文化吗？亚特兰大埃默里大学的 Frans de Waal 认为算。他主张，文化只是另一种生物适应性特征，已经在许多生物身上演化出来。这样看待文化的一项好处是，我们可以开始理解它可能如何、又为何会在这些生物中出现。鲸具有若干有利于社会学习的生物属性。除了心智能力发达之外，它们还善于辨别声音，这非常适合在海洋环境中交流。许多鲸类会花数年时间抚育后代，并生活在规模小、关系稳定且由多代成员组成的社会中；这种社会体系为教与学提供了充足机会。

GBut why have cetaceans evolved the ability to learn from other group members? Experts in whale biology believe that ecological factors and the need to adapt to sudden changes in the environment played a large part in the emergence of culture. Although the ocean is a relatively stable habitat in many ways, it is highly changeable in one crucial respect – the availability of food. One moment there might be a plentiful supply of fish, the next they’ve disappeared. When that happens, the past experience of senior members of the group – and the ability to share this knowledge – is a huge asset. The dialects of killer whales allow members of the group to identify each other, enabling them to share information about food hot spots. Among resident killer whales, it also allows females to avoid inbreeding by picking out a mate with a strange dialect from outside their pod, says Barrett-Lennard.

但鲸类为什么演化出了向群体其他成员学习的能力？鲸类生物学专家认为，生态因素以及适应环境突然变化的需要，在文化的出现过程中发挥了很大作用。尽管从许多方面看，海洋是相对稳定的栖息地，但在一个关键方面却变化极大——食物是否充足。前一刻鱼类可能还供应充足，下一刻便消失无踪。出现这种情况时，群体中年长成员以往的经验，以及分享这些知识的能力，就会成为巨大的优势。虎鲸的方言使群体成员能够相互识别，从而可以分享有关食物富集区的信息。Barrett-Lennard 说，对定居型虎鲸而言，这还能让雌鲸从鲸群以外辨认出方言陌生的配偶，以避免近亲繁殖。

HThe importance of sharing information seems to have led to biological changes in at least some species of whale. Female killer whales, like humans, are very unusual in that they live up to a quarter of a century after they have had their descendants. And what whale matriarchs offer is the most important thing of all – cultural knowledge, vital for the group’s survival, passed directly from one generation to the next.

分享信息的重要性似乎已经促使至少某些鲸类发生了生物学变化。雌性虎鲸与人类一样，有一点非常不同寻常：在有了后代之后，它们还能活长达四分之一个世纪。而虎鲸群中的年长雌鲸所能提供的，是最重要的东西——文化知识。这些知识对群体生存至关重要，会从一代直接传给下一代。
