The fashion industry

时装产业

AThe fashion industry is a multibillion-dollar global enterprise devoted to the business of making and selling clothes. It encompasses all types of garments, from designer fashions to ordinary everyday clothing. Because data on the industry are typically reported for national economies and expressed in terms of its many separate sectors, total figures for world production of textiles and clothing are difficult to obtain. However, by any measure, the industry accounts for a significant share of world economic output.

时装产业是一项规模达数十亿 dollars 的全球性事业，专门从事服装的生产与销售。它涵盖各种服装，从设计师时装到普通日常衣物都包括在内。由于行业数据通常按国家统计，并按众多不同细分行业分别报告，全球纺织品和服装生产总量的数据很难获得。不过，无论采用哪种衡量方式，该行业都占世界经济产出的显著份额。

BThe fashion industry is a product of the modern age. Prior to the mid-19th century, virtually all clothing was handmade for individuals, either as home production or on order from dressmakers and tailors. By the beginning of the 20th century, with the development of new technologies such as the sewing machine, the factory system of production, and the growth of department stores and other retail outlets, clothing had increasingly come to be mass-produced in standard sizes and sold at fixed prices. Although the fashion industry developed first in Europe, today it is highly globalised, with garments often designed in one country, manufactured in another, and sold in a third. For example, an American fashion company might source fabric in China, have the clothes manufactured in Vietnam, finished in Italy, and shipped to a warehouse in the United States for distribution to retail outlets internationally.

时装产业是现代社会的产物。19世纪中叶以前，几乎所有衣服都为个人手工制作，要么在家中生产，要么向裁缝订制。到20世纪初，随着缝纫机等新技术的发展、工厂生产制度的建立，以及百货商店和其他零售网点的增长，服装越来越多地按标准尺码批量生产，并以固定价格出售。时装产业虽然最先在欧洲发展，如今却已高度全球化：服装往往在一个国家设计，在另一个国家制造，再到第三个国家销售。例如，一家美国时装公司可能从中国采购面料，在越南制衣，到意大利完成最后加工，再运往美国的仓库，分销到世界各地的零售网点。

COne of the first accomplishments of the Industrial Revolution in the 18th century was the partial automation of the spinning and weaving of wool, cotton, silk and other natural fibres. Today, these processes are highly automated and carried out by computer-controlled, high-speed machinery, and fabrics made from both natural fibres and synthetic fibres (such as nylon, acrylic and polyester) are produced. A growing interest in sustainable fashion (or ‘eco-fashion’) has led to greater use of environmentally friendly fibres, such as hemp. In addition, high-tech synthetic fabrics confer such properties as moisture absorption, stain resistance, retention or dissipation of body heat, and protection against fire, weapons, cold, ultraviolet radiation and other hazards. Fabrics are also produced with a wide range of visual effects through dyeing, weaving, printing and other processes. Together with fashion forecasters, fabric manufacturers work well in advance of the clothing production cycle to create fabrics with colours, textures and other qualities that anticipate consumer demand.

18世纪工业革命最早取得的成就之一，是让羊毛、棉花、丝绸及其他天然纤维的纺纱和织造实现部分自动化。如今，这些工序已高度自动化，由计算机控制的高速机器完成；人们既生产天然纤维面料，也生产尼龙、腈纶和聚酯纤维等合成纤维面料。人们对可持续时装，也就是“生态时装”（eco-fashion）的兴趣不断增加，推动了大麻纤维等环保纤维的更多使用。此外，高科技合成面料可以具备吸湿、防污、保留或散发体热等特性，还能防护火焰、武器、严寒、紫外线辐射和其他危险。通过染色、织造、印花及其他工艺，面料还可呈现多种多样的视觉效果。面料制造商会与时装趋势预测人员合作，在服装生产周期开始之前很早就着手开发面料，预先考虑消费者对颜色、质感及其他特性的需求。

DHistorically, very few fashion designers have become famous—brands such as Coco Chanel or Calvin Klein—who have been responsible for prestigious high-fashion collections. These designers are influential in the fashion world, but, contrary to popular belief, they do not dictate new fashions; rather, they endeavour to design clothes that will meet consumer demand. The vast majority of designers work anonymously for manufacturers, as part of design teams, adapting designs into marketable garments for average consumers. They draw inspiration from a wide range of sources, including film and television costumes, street clothing and active sportswear.

从历史上看，只有极少数时装设计师能够声名卓著，形成 Coco Chanel 或 Calvin Klein 这样的品牌，并负责打造享有盛誉的高级时装系列。这些设计师在时装界很有影响力，但与大众看法相反，他们并不决定新潮流；他们努力做的，是设计出能够满足消费者需求的服装。绝大多数设计师都默默无闻地为制造商工作，作为设计团队的一员，把设计改造成适合普通消费者、可以投放市场的服装。他们从众多来源汲取灵感，包括影视服装、街头衣着和运动服。

The fashion industry’s traditional design methods, such as paper sketches and the draping of fabric on mannequins, have been supplemented or replaced by computer-assisted design techniques. These allow designers to rapidly make changes to a proposed design and instantaneously share the proposed changes with colleagues—whether they are in the next room or on another continent.

时装产业采用纸上草图、在人台上披挂面料等传统设计方法，如今这些方法已由计算机辅助设计技术补充或取代。借助这些技术，设计师可以迅速修改拟议方案，并立即与同事分享改动，无论同事就在隔壁房间，还是身处另一个大陆。

EAn important stage in garment production is the translation of the clothing design into templates, in a range of sizes, for cutting the cloth. Because the proportions of the human body change with increases or decreases in weight, templates cannot simply be scaled up or down. Template-making was traditionally a highly skilled profession. Today, despite innovations in computer programming, designs in larger sizes are difficult to adjust for every body shape. Whatever the size, the template—whether drawn on paper or programmed as a set of computer instructions—determines how fabric is cut into the pieces that will be joined to make a garment. For all but the most expensive clothing, fabric cutting is accomplished by computer-guided knives or high-intensity lasers that can cut many layers of fabric at once.

服装生产的一个重要阶段，是把服装设计转化为一系列不同尺码的纸样，以便裁剪面料。人体比例会随着体重增加或减少而改变，因此纸样不能简单地按比例放大或缩小。制作纸样过去是一项技术要求很高的职业。如今，尽管计算机编程不断创新，大尺码设计仍然很难针对每一种体形进行调整。不论尺码大小，纸样可能画在纸上，也可能编成一套计算机指令；它决定面料如何裁成各个部分，再把这些部分拼接成一件衣服。除最昂贵的服装外，面料裁剪都由计算机控制的刀具或高强度激光完成，它们一次可以切割多层面料。

FThe next stage of production is the assembly process. Some companies use their own production facilities for some or all of the manufacturing process, but the majority rely on separately owned manufacturing firms or contractors to produce garments to their specifications. In the field of women’s clothing, manufacturers typically produce several product lines a year, which they deliver to retailers on predetermined dates. Technological innovation, including the development of computer-guided machinery, has resulted in the automation of some stages of assembly. Nevertheless, the fundamental process of sewing remains labour-intensive. In the late 20th century, China emerged as the world’s largest producer of clothing because of its low labour costs and highly disciplined workforce.

生产的下一阶段是把裁片拼接成衣。一些公司在部分或全部制造环节使用自己的生产设施，但大多数公司依靠独立的制造企业或承包商，按照委托公司的规格要求生产服装。在女装领域，制造商通常每年生产多个产品系列，并按预先确定的日期交付给零售商。包括计算机控制机器在内的技术创新，使部分成衣拼接环节实现了自动化。尽管如此，最基本的缝纫工序仍需投入大量人力。20世纪后期，中国凭借较低的劳动力成本和纪律严明的劳动力队伍，成为世界最大的服装生产国。

Assembled items then go through various processes collectively known as ‘finishing’. These include the addition of decorative elements, fasteners, brand-name labels and other labels (often legally required) specifying fibre content, laundry instructions and country of manufacture. Finished items are then pressed and packed for shipment.

拼接完成的服装随后要经过统称为“后整理”（finishing）的多种工序。这些工序包括添加装饰元素、扣件、品牌标签和其他标签；后者通常为法律所要求，要注明纤维成分、洗涤说明和制造国。完成加工后，服装会被熨压、包装，准备发运。

GFor much of the period following World War II, trade in textiles and garments was strictly regulated by purchasing countries, which imposed quotas and tariffs. Since the 1980s, these protectionist measures, which were intended (ultimately without success) to prevent textile and clothing production from moving from high-wage to low-wage countries, have gradually been abandoned. They have been replaced by a free-trade approach, under the regulatory control of global organisations. The advent of metal shipping containers and relatively inexpensive air freight has also made it possible for production to be closely tied to market conditions, even across globe-spanning distances.

第二次世界大战后的很长一段时期，纺织品和服装贸易受到采购国的严格管制，这些国家会实行配额和关税。此类保护主义措施意在阻止纺织品和服装生产从高工资国家转向低工资国家，但最终并未成功；自20世纪80年代以来，这些措施逐渐被取消，取而代之的是由全球性组织监管的自由贸易方式。金属货运集装箱的出现和价格相对低廉的航空货运，也使生产能够与市场状况紧密衔接，即使跨越全球距离也是如此。
