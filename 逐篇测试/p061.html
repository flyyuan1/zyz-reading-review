Bird Migration

鸟类迁徙

ABirds are highly mobile and among the swiftest of living creatures. Flight gives them the power to move in any direction for as long as they have the energy to keep going. They are equipped with lightweight, hollow bones, a finely-tuned navigation system and an ingenious heat-conserving design that allows them to face life in the harshest of climates. Their respiratory systems have to perform efficiently during sustained flights at altitude, so they have evolved a system of extracting air from their lungs that far exceeds that of any other animal. These features enable birds to come and go with the seasons. On the way, they face constant danger and a daily requirement to find food, water and a safe place to rest. So why do millions of birds migrate rather than stay in one place?

鸟类的活动能力极强，也是速度最快的生物之一。只要还有能量继续前进，飞行就使它们能够朝任何方向移动。鸟类有轻盈的中空骨骼、精密的导航系统和巧妙的保温结构，因而能够在最严酷的气候中生存。它们在高空持续飞行时，呼吸系统必须高效运作，因此进化出一套从肺部抽取空气的系统，其效率远超任何其他动物。这些特征使鸟类能够随季节来去迁移。途中，它们时刻面临危险，每天还必须找到食物、水和安全的休息地。那么，为什么数以百万计的鸟会选择迁徙，而不是留在一个地方？

BMost of the explanation revolves around food. In temperate and arctic regions of the northern hemisphere, food is abundant during a short growing season. Migration evolved as a way for birds to exploit resources that are seasonally abundant and, equally important, to go elsewhere when the resources become scarce or harsh weather arrives. Many species can tolerate cold temperatures if food is plentiful but when food is not available they must migrate.

大部分原因都与食物有关。在北半球温带和北极地区，食物会在短暂的生长季中大量出现。迁徙行为由此进化而来，使鸟类能够利用季节性丰富的资源；同样重要的是，当资源变得匮乏或恶劣天气来临时，它们可以前往别处。许多物种在食物充足时能够忍受低温，但一旦找不到食物，就必须迁徙。

CWhile this simple explanation for why birds migrate is essentially true, intriguing questions remain. One puzzling fact is that many birds journey much further than would be necessary to find food and good weather. Another apparent conundrum involves the huge migrations performed by arctic birds and mudflat-probing shore birds that breed close to polar regions. In general, the further north a migrant species breeds, the further south it spends the winter. Yet, en route to a final destination in southern latitudes, all these individuals overfly areas of seemingly suitable habitat spanning two hemispheres. Such compulsive journeys probably evolved over time and in response to fluctuations in weather but we do not really know.

这种关于鸟类为何迁徙的简单解释基本属实，但仍留下了一些耐人寻味的问题。一个令人困惑的事实是，许多鸟飞行的距离远远超过寻找食物和宜人天气所必需的距离。另一个明显的谜团，涉及北极鸟类和在泥滩探寻食物、靠近极地繁殖的滨鸟所进行的大规模迁徙。一般来说，一种候鸟繁殖地越靠北，越会到更靠南的地方越冬。然而，在飞往南部纬度最终目的地的途中，这些鸟会越过横跨两个半球、看似适宜栖居的地区。这种近乎无法抑制的迁徙可能是在漫长时间中为应对天气波动而进化形成的，但我们其实并不清楚。

DBut just how do birds reach their destination and, more to the point, how do young birds migrate over vast distances without parental assistance? Very few adults migrate accompanied by their offspring and youngsters may have little or no inkling of their parents' appearance. Cuckoos are famous for depositing their eggs in the nests of other birds that unwittingly serve as foster-parents. Astonishing as this piece of evolutionary behaviour is, it is even more remarkable to consider that, once grown, the young cuckoo makes its own way to ancestral wintering grounds in the tropics before returning single-handedly to northern Europe to seek out a mate among its own kind.

不过，鸟究竟如何抵达目的地？更关键的是，幼鸟没有亲鸟帮助，怎样能迁徙如此遥远的距离？极少有成年鸟带着后代迁徙，幼鸟甚至可能对亲鸟的外形所知甚少或一无所知。杜鹃（cuckoos）会把卵产在其他鸟的巢中，让毫不知情的鸟充当养亲，这一点十分著名。这种进化行为已经令人惊叹，但更不可思议的是，小杜鹃长大后会独自前往祖先在热带的越冬地，随后又独自返回北欧，在同类中寻找配偶。

EAs we continue to study bird migration, its mystery is better understood but also compounded. Mounting evidence has confirmed that birds use the sun and stars to obtain compass directions but true navigation requires an awareness of position and time, especially when lost. Experiments have shown that, after being taken thousands of miles over an unfamiliar landmass, birds are still capable of returning rapidly to nest sites. Such phenomenal powers are the product of computing a number of sophisticated cues including an innate map of the night sky and the pull of the Earth's magnetic field. How the birds use their 'instruments' remains unknown, but one thing is clear – they see the world with a sensory perception superior to ours. Most small birds migrate at night and take their direction from the position of the setting sun. However, as well as seeing the sun go down, they also seem to see the plane of polarised light it causes. This calibrates their compass. Travelling at night provides other benefits: the danger of meeting predators as well as the risk of dehydration due to flying for long periods in warm sunlit skies is reduced. Furthermore, at night the air structure is cool and smooth and conducive to sustained, stable flight.

随着我们不断研究鸟类迁徙，人们对其中的奥秘了解得更多，但同时也发现它更加复杂。越来越多的证据已经证实，鸟类利用太阳和星辰获取罗盘方向；但真正的导航还要求它们了解自己的位置和时间，尤其是在迷失方向时。实验表明，即使被带到数千英里外一片陌生的陆地上，鸟类仍能迅速返回筑巢地点。这种非凡能力来自对多种复杂线索的综合计算，其中包括与生俱来的夜空地图和地球磁场的牵引力。鸟类究竟如何使用这些“仪器”仍是未知，但有一点很清楚：它们感知世界的能力胜过我们。大多数小型鸟类在夜间迁徙，并根据落日的位置确定方向。不过，它们似乎不仅能看到太阳落下，还能看见由此形成的偏振光平面，从而校准自己的罗盘。夜间飞行还有其他好处：遇到捕食者的危险较小，也能降低长时间在温暖、阳光充足的天空中飞行所造成的脱水风险。此外，夜间空气凉爽平稳，有利于长时间稳定飞行。

FNevertheless, all journeys involve considerable risk and part of the skill in arriving safely is setting off at the right time. This means accurate weather forecasting and utilising favourable winds. Birds are adept at both and, in tests, some can detect the minute difference in barometric pressure between the floor and ceiling of a room. Often birds react to weather changes before there is any visible sign of them. Certain species that feed on grassland flee at the onset of a cold snap because when the ground surface freezes the birds could starve. Yet they return ahead of a thaw, their arrival linked to a pressure change presaging an improvement in the weather.

尽管如此，所有旅程都伴随巨大风险，而安全抵达的部分技巧在于选择正确的出发时间。这需要准确预测天气并利用有利风向。鸟类对这两点都十分擅长；在测试中，有些鸟甚至能察觉一个房间地面与天花板之间极其细微的气压差。鸟类往往在天气变化尚未出现任何可见迹象前就作出反应。某些在草地上觅食的物种，会在寒潮开始时逃离，因为地表一旦冻结，它们就可能饿死。然而，它们会在解冻前返回；其到来与预示天气好转的气压变化有关。

GUnfortunately, some never complete their journey. Not so long ago the assumption was that a bird in the air on a dark windy night with no visible reference points to judge position was, like a pilot without radar, hopelessly lost. We now know this is not the case. Losses do occur, but mainly because birds get swept off course by strong air masses against which battle is futile, and this results in a watery grave. Indeed, it is not unusual for ocean fish to be caught with bird remains in their stomachs. But sometimes there's a silver lining. Each autumn a small number of North American birds are blown across the Atlantic by fast-moving westerly tail winds. Not only do they arrive safely in Europe but, based on tracking evidence, some make it back to North America in the following spring – probably after spending the winter with European migrants in sunny African climes.

遗憾的是，有些鸟永远无法完成旅程。不久以前，人们还以为，在一个黑暗多风的夜晚，空中的鸟看不到任何可用来判断位置的参照物，就会像没有雷达的飞行员一样彻底迷失方向。如今我们知道事实并非如此。迁徙途中确实会有损失，但主要原因是鸟类被强劲气团吹离航线；与之抗争毫无希望，最终只能葬身水中。事实上，海鱼被捕获时胃里带有鸟类残骸，并不罕见。不过，有时也会因祸得福。每年秋天，少量北美鸟类会被高速西风顺势吹过大西洋。它们不仅能够安全抵达欧洲；追踪证据还表明，其中一些会在第二年春天返回北美——它们此前可能与欧洲候鸟一起，在阳光充足的非洲地区度过了冬天。
