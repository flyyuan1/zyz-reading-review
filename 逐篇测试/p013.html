Dust and the American West

尘土与美国西部

Images of deserts in the United States show dusty, barren landscapes, but the land may not always have been this way. Ever since settlers moved west across the US, there has been dust, clouds of it, everywhere. It was part of the landscape, or so it seemed to them. But there were no records of the landscape of the West until the settlers arrived. Now, evidence is starting to emerge which suggests that before the settlers, there was very little dust.

美国沙漠的图像展现出尘土飞扬、寸草不生的景观，但这里或许并非一直如此。自从定居者横跨美国向西迁移，尘土便随处可见，甚至漫天成云。在他们看来，尘土似乎就是当地景观的一部分。然而，定居者到来前，没有人记录过西部的景观。如今，逐渐出现的证据表明，在定居者到来以前，那里的尘土其实非常少。

The evidence comes from the San Juan Mountains of southwest Colorado, downwind of Arizona and New Mexico. There, Jason Neff, a geochemist from the University of Colorado, has been analyzing sediments, the sand, stones and mud, laid down over the past 5,000 years. Atmospheric dust was minimal throughout those five millennia until the mid-19th century, he says, but then, ‘from about 1860 to 1900, dust deposition rates shot up.’

证据来自科罗拉多州（Colorado）西南部的圣胡安山脉（San Juan Mountains），那里位于亚利桑那州（Arizona）和新墨西哥州（New Mexico）的下风向。科罗拉多大学（University of Colorado）地球化学家 Jason Neff 一直在分析过去5,000年间沉积下来的物质，包括沙、石头和泥土。他说，在这5,000年中的绝大部分时间里，大气尘埃都极少，直到19世纪中叶才改变：“大约从1860年到1900年，尘埃沉积速度急剧上升。”

This is surprising because usually dry means dusty, and the American West has almost always been dry, often drier than today. There was a near-permanent drought between 900 and 1300 which was so intense that it destroyed a series of Native American civilizations, including the Anasazi, whose cliff homes are now US national treasures. Yet the evidence from the San Juan lakes is that it was not dusty. Even as their civilization was collapsing, the Anasazi seem to have protected their soils from erosion.

这令人意外，因为干旱通常意味着尘土多，而美国西部几乎一直很干旱，过去往往比今天还要干。900年至1300年间发生过一场近乎持续不断的旱灾，严重到摧毁了一系列美洲原住民文明，其中包括阿纳萨齐（Anasazi）文明；他们建在悬崖上的居所如今已成为美国国宝。然而，圣胡安地区湖泊中的证据表明，当时并没有多少尘土。即使文明正在崩溃，阿纳萨齐人似乎仍保护住了土壤，使其免遭侵蚀。

This was not the case with the European settlers once they brought their cows. The landscape the cattle were introduced to was remarkably ill-equipped to cope with grazing animals, says Neff. ‘Unlike most other parts of the US, there were few grazers in the American Southwest until the Europeans came. No bison and few antelope or deer.’

然而，欧洲定居者把牛带来后，情况便不再如此。Neff 说，牛被引入的这片土地很难承受食草动物的啃食和踩踏。“欧洲人到来前，美国西南部与美国其他大多数地区不同，食草动物很少。这里没有野牛，羚羊或鹿也很少。”

In the Great Plains to the east and north, bison roamed in vast herds. Their regular grazing had created tough grass, while the herds manured the soil. In the Southwest, the land had few defenses against a sudden invasion of millions of livestock, whose teeth stripped the grass and whose hooves punctured the hard crust of desert soils that protected them from the wind. The invasion was sudden, funded by a bubble of speculative investment, much of it from Britain. The money went into railroads and herds of cattle and sheep that rode the rails to the wide open pastures. By 1900, when sedimentation rates peaked, there were 20 million cattle and 25 million sheep in the West.

在东部和北部的大平原（Great Plains）上，大群野牛四处游荡。野牛经常啃食，使当地形成了坚韧的草，而牛群的粪便还能肥沃土壤。西南部的土地却几乎没有防御能力，难以抵挡数百万头牲畜突然涌入：它们用牙齿啃光青草，蹄子则踩穿沙漠土壤表面那层抵御风蚀的坚硬结皮。这场入侵来得突然，资金来自一场投机性投资泡沫，其中很大一部分来自英国。资金被投入铁路和牛羊群，牲畜沿铁路运往开阔牧场。到1900年沉积速度达到峰值时，西部已有2,000万头牛和2,500万只羊。

One of the biggest ranches was owned by the Aztec Land and Cattle Company, which owned a million acres of land by 1884. Each acre had cost the company a mere 50 cents, and like many other speculators, it was only interested in quick profits and had little incentive to protect the soils from overgrazing. By the time Aztec sold the ranch in 1901, it was barren, with cattle carcasses scattered across the exhausted land. Such was the damage to the grasslands that even now few of the pastures have recovered. The parched and exposed soil simply blew away. The 1862 Homestead Act was passed in order to encourage the populating of the West. Any family willing to make the journey was entitled to claim 160 acres and farm it. Yet by the time people moved west in large numbers, the cattle companies had taken most of the land and surrounded it with barbed wire. The water sources were therefore cut off, and were aggressively guarded. It was only in the 1930s, with the passage of the Taylor Grazing Act, that federal authorities finally sought to limit cattle herds.

最大的牧场之一属于 Aztec Land and Cattle Company。到1884年，该公司已拥有100万英亩土地。每英亩土地仅花费公司50美分；和许多其他投机者一样，公司只想迅速获利，几乎没有动力保护土壤免受过度放牧破坏。到 Aztec 公司1901年出售牧场时，那里已经一片荒芜，地力耗尽的土地上散落着牛的尸体。草原受损如此严重，以至于时至今日也只有少数牧场恢复。干透而裸露的土壤就这样被风吹走。美国1862年通过《宅地法》（Homestead Act），目的是鼓励人们迁居西部。任何愿意远行的家庭都有权申领160英亩土地并进行耕种。然而，等到大批居民向西迁移时，畜牧公司已经占据大部分土地，并用带刺铁丝网围了起来。人们因此无法接近水源，而水源周围还受到严密把守。直到20世纪30年代《泰勒放牧法》（Taylor Grazing Act）通过后，联邦当局才终于设法限制牛群规模。

Soil scientists have known for a while about the importance of the hard crust that forms on arid soils. ‘These crusts can survive winds of up to a hundred miles an hour, but cattle hooves break the crust,’ says Jayne Belnap, a soil ecologist at the US Geological Survey, Utah. The scale of the dust clouds created by the livestock invasion has until now been largely unknown. When Neff first discovered dust in Colorado lake sediments laid down in the 19th century, he was initially unsure where it came from. Maybe it had crossed the Pacific from China’s Gobi Desert.

土壤科学家早已知道，干旱土壤表面形成的坚硬结皮十分重要。“这些结皮能抵挡时速高达100英里的风，但牛蹄会踩碎它们，”美国地质调查局（US Geological Survey）驻犹他州（Utah）的土壤生态学家 Jayne Belnap 说。牲畜入侵所产生的尘云究竟有多大，此前一直基本无人知晓。Neff 最初在科罗拉多州19世纪形成的湖泊沉积物中发现尘土时，并不确定其来源。它或许是从中国戈壁沙漠越过太平洋而来的。

But after investigating the size and chemical composition of the dust, Neff was clear that it mostly came from the American Southwest, mainly Arizona and New Mexico. Now, with the soil crusts gone, dust clouds still head north and are having significant ecological effects in the Colorado mountains. They carry nutrients with them into areas which previously evolved and survived without them. But perhaps the most dramatic impact of the dust has been on snowfields in the Rocky Mountain Range. Even a thin sprinkling of dark material means snow absorbs more solar radiation, meaning that snowmelt occurs far more rapidly during springtime. The impact on the ski tourism industry is obvious.

然而，Neff 研究尘土的颗粒大小和化学成分后确定，它主要来自美国西南部，尤其是亚利桑那州和新墨西哥州。如今，土壤结皮已经消失，尘云仍在向北飘移，并对科罗拉多山区的生态造成显著影响。尘土把养分带到一些过去在没有这些养分的情况下演化并生存的地区。但尘土最显著的影响或许发生在落基山脉（Rocky Mountain Range）的雪原上。即使只撒落薄薄一层深色物质，也会使积雪吸收更多太阳辐射，导致春季融雪速度大幅加快。它对滑雪旅游业的影响显而易见。

The loss of snow and the shrinking of glaciers across the American West in the past century have been dramatic. Glacier National Park in Montana, for example, has lost three-quarters of its snow cover since 1910. All this is frequently attributed to global warming. While this almost certainly plays a role, Neff’s findings suggest that dust may also contribute.

过去一个世纪，美国西部积雪减少、冰川缩小的程度十分惊人。例如，蒙大拿州（Montana）冰川国家公园（Glacier National Park）自1910年以来已经失去四分之三的积雪覆盖。人们经常把这一切归因于全球变暖。全球变暖几乎肯定发挥了作用，但 Neff 的研究结果表明，尘土可能也有影响。
