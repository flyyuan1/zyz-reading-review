Neanderthal Technology

尼安德特人的技术

AWe think of our prehistoric ancestors as people of the ice and snow, living in caves, and for many of the West European Neanderthalers that is just a picture of their life. But where there were no caves, further to the east on the Russian steppe, for example, open-air sites with some sort of constructed shelter were the only option.

我们常把史前祖先想象成生活在冰天雪地、居住在洞穴里的人；对许多西欧尼安德特人来说，这确实就是他们生活的写照。但在更靠东的地区，例如俄罗斯草原上，没有洞穴可用，露天居址加上某种人工搭建的遮蔽物便成了唯一选择。

We now know much more about the cave sites than the open-air ones because, historically, it was the cave sites of Western Europe that were first explored by archaeologists and also because open-air sites are harder to find – many of them have disappeared under deep mud deposits or under the rising postglacial seas. Caves, moreover, aid the survival of archaeological material and can preserve the records of remote millennia.

如今，我们对洞穴居址的了解远多于露天居址。一方面，历史上考古学家最早考察的是西欧的洞穴遗址；另一方面，露天遗址更难发现，其中许多已经消失在厚厚的泥土沉积之下，或被冰期后上涨的海水淹没。此外，洞穴有利于考古材料留存，能够保存遥远数千年前的记录。

BIn south-west France, the limestone caves of the Périgord region made ideal homes for the Neanderthal people. There were good supplies of flint to hand for axes and the like, and the caves were often sited in small river valleys that offered protection against the worst of the weather. The Neanderthalers liked south-facing caves, for obvious reasons of sunshine and wind avoidance, and caves at some height above the valley floor offered refuge from floods and good game-watching vantage points.

在法国西南部，佩里戈尔地区（Périgord）的石灰岩洞穴是尼安德特人的理想居所。附近有充足的燧石，可用来制作石斧等工具；这些洞穴又往往位于小河谷中，能够抵御最恶劣的天气。尼安德特人喜欢朝南的洞穴，原因显而易见：既能获得阳光，又可避风。位置高出谷底一定距离的洞穴还能躲避洪水，并提供观察猎物的有利制高点。

The Périgord region during the last ice age was, in fact, an exceptionally benign habitat for humans. It enjoyed a rather maritime climate with cooler summers that permitted the extension of tundra and steppe over its higher plateaux, and its year-round high levels of sunshine favoured the growth of the ground plants needed by reindeer, bison and horse. Winters were mildish for the ice age, animals never needed to migrate far from summer to winter, and men never needed to travel far from home to find abundant supplies of meat.

事实上，在上一个冰期，佩里戈尔地区是一个格外适合人类生存的栖息地。那里受海洋性气候影响，夏季较凉，使苔原和草原能够扩展到海拔较高的高原地带；全年充足的日照则有利于地表植物生长，而驯鹿、野牛和马需要以这些植物为食。以冰期的标准衡量，冬季也算温和，动物从夏季到冬季都不必迁徙很远，人们也无须远离居所便能找到充足的肉食。

CIn Central and Eastern Europe, where caves are less readily available, such open-air sites as have been discovered were mostly located near water: bottom-land was a good area to be for people and animals, and also because the sedimentation pattern of lakes and stream courses has aided archaeological preservation – whereas erosion has presumably blown away sites which were out in the open. Some of the open-air sites in Germany, Central Europe and Russia have provided valuable information about Neanderthal man and his way of life. From Moldova, for example, comes evidence that has been interpreted as the remains of wind-breaks, or even a large tent-ring, up to about 8 x 5 m in size, of mainly mammoth bones enclosing a dense concentration of stone tools, animal bones and ash.

在洞穴较难找到的中欧和东欧，已经发现的露天遗址大多位于水边。低洼地带适合人类和动物活动；湖泊和河道的沉积模式也有助于保存考古遗存，而位于完全开阔地带的遗址很可能已被侵蚀作用吹散。德国、中欧其他地区和俄罗斯的一些露天遗址，为了解尼安德特人及其生活方式提供了宝贵资料。例如，摩尔多瓦（Moldova）出土的证据被解释为挡风设施的遗迹，甚至可能是一圈大型帐篷的遗迹。这一结构最大约为8米×5米，主要由猛犸象骨骼构成，围住了密集分布的石器、动物骨骼和灰烬。

DFrom the West European caves more evidence of built structures is available, and some of it goes back a long way in time. In the Grotte du Lazaret, near Nice, at a date during the last ice age but one, claims for some sort of skin tent within the cave have been advanced, on the basis of arrangements of large stones out from the cave wall that might have supported timber struts for a covering of skins up to the rock face above.

西欧洞穴提供了更多人工建筑结构的证据，其中一些年代极为久远。在尼斯附近的拉扎雷洞穴（Grotte du Lazaret），有人提出，倒数第二次冰期的某个时期，洞内可能搭有某种兽皮帐篷。其依据是从洞壁向外排列的大石块；这些石块可能曾支撑木制斜撑，以便把兽皮一直铺接到上方岩壁。

At Lazaret, what might be openings in the hypothesised tents seem to point away from the cave mouth, and finds of wolf and fox foot bones, without the rest of the skeletons, inside these ‘tents’ have been thought to indicate the use of animal pelts as bed coverings. The two patches of ash at Lazaret that mark ancient fires, with stone tools around them evidently made and used on the spot, are edged with small marine mollusc shells, prompting the excavator to suggest that seaweed had been used as bedding around the fires. The cave of Baume-Bonne in the Basses-Alpes region of France, another early site, boasts ten square metres of cobbles brought up from the local river and laid down, as though to take care of a puddle area in the cave, with the smoothest and roundest surfaces of the stones uppermost, and there are other similar cases.

在拉扎雷，被推测为帐篷入口的部位似乎背向洞口。人们还在这些“帐篷”内发现了狼和狐狸的足骨，却没有发现骨架的其他部分，因此有人认为，兽皮曾被用作床铺覆盖物。拉扎雷有两片灰烬标示出古代火堆，周围还有显然在现场制作和使用的石器。灰烬边缘分布着小型海洋软体动物的贝壳，这促使发掘者推测，人们曾把海藻铺在火堆周围当作寝具。法国下阿尔卑斯地区（Basses-Alpes）的博姆博讷洞穴（Baume-Bonne）也是一处早期遗址。洞内铺着面积达十平方米的卵石，这些卵石从当地河里运来，仿佛是为了处理洞内的一片积水区；石头最光滑、最圆润的一面都朝上，其他地方也有类似实例。

EThe ash encountered in concentrations at some sites testifies to the Neanderthal people’s use of fire: not surprising, since use of fire was, by Neanderthal times, an already ancient accomplishment of evolving humanity, and survival in the sub-arctic conditions faced by the Neanderthalers is inconceivable without control of fire. Fire gave warmth, light, heat for cooking and defence against predatory animals. A charred piece of birch from Krapina in Croatia is thought to be the remains of a fire-making twirl stick. But Neanderthal hearths, in the sense of specially constructed places for fire, are fewer and harder to identify with certainty than the mere ash piles that are a regular feature of their sites. They seem often to have just lit a small fire (40–50 cm across) on the existing ground surface of the cave, without preparation. Judging from the shallow penetration of heat effects under the ash, this fire was only of short duration. Sometimes the fires were larger in size, up to one metre across, and quite irregular in shape. It is not always easy to decide how much additional structure some fires possessed: claims of stone circles to contain the fire run up against the fact that stones tend to litter the cave floors everywhere and those around a fire can quite accidentally look as though they were arranged in a circle.

一些遗址中成片集中出现的灰烬，证明尼安德特人使用过火。这并不令人意外，因为到尼安德特人生活的时代，使用火已经是不断进化的人类掌握已久的技能；若不能控制火，便无法想象他们如何在亚北极环境中生存。火能够提供温暖和照明，可用于烹饪，也能抵御捕食动物。克罗地亚克拉皮纳（Krapina）出土的一块烧焦桦木，被认为是钻木取火所用旋转木棍的残片。然而，如果把炉灶定义为专门修建的用火场所，那么尼安德特人的炉灶比遗址中常见的普通灰堆更少，也更难以确证。他们似乎经常不作任何准备，直接在洞穴原有地面上点起一堆直径40至50厘米的小火。从灰烬下方受热痕迹很浅来看，这种火只燃烧了很短时间。有时火堆较大，直径可达一米，而且形状很不规则。某些火堆究竟带有多少附加结构，并不总是容易判断。有人声称他们用一圈石块围住火堆，但洞穴地面本就到处散落着石头，火堆周围的石块完全可能只是偶然看起来像围成了一个圆圈。
