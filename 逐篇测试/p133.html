On art and artists

论艺术与艺术家

AThere really is no such thing as art, only artists. Once these were people who took coloured earth and roughed out the forms of a bison on the wall of a cave; today some buy their paints, and others design posters for hoardings; they did and do many other things. There is no harm in calling all these activities art as long as we keep in mind that such a word may mean very different things in different times and places, and as long as we realize that Art with a capital A has no existence: it has become something of an obsession. You may crush an artist by telling him that what he has just done may be quite good in its own way, only it is not true art.

其实并没有所谓的艺术，只有艺术家。从前，他们把带颜色的泥土涂到洞穴墙壁上，粗略勾勒出野牛的形状；如今，有些人购买颜料，有些人为广告牌设计海报。他们过去和现在还做着许多其他事情。只要我们记得“艺术”一词在不同时代和地点可能含义迥异，把所有这些活动都称为艺术并无不妥；我们也要明白，首字母大写、被奉为抽象概念的“艺术”并不存在，它已经成了某种执念。你若对一名艺术家说，他刚完成的作品以自身标准来看也许相当不错，只不过不是真正的艺术，就可能把他击垮。

BActually, I do not think that there are any wrong reasons for liking a statue or a picture. We may like a painting of a village scene because it reminds us of home. There is nothing wrong with that. All of us, when we see a painting, are bound to be reminded of a hundred-and-one things which influence our likes and dislikes. As long as these memories help us to enjoy what we see, we need not worry.

事实上，我认为喜欢一座雕像或一幅画并没有什么错误的理由。我们可能喜欢一幅乡村景色画，是因为它让我们想起故乡。这并没有错。我们每个人看到一幅画时，都必然会联想到许许多多影响自己好恶的事情。只要这些回忆能帮助我们欣赏眼前的作品，就不必担心。

CIt is only when some irrelevant memory makes us prejudiced, when we instinctively turn away from a magnificent picture of an alpine scene because we dislike climbing, that we should search our mind for the reason for the aversion which spoils a pleasure we might otherwise have had. There are wrong reasons for disliking a work of art.

只有当某段无关的回忆使我们产生偏见时，例如仅仅因为不喜欢登山，便本能地避开一幅壮丽的高山风景画，我们才应当反思内心，找出这种反感的原因；它破坏了我们原本可能获得的乐趣。讨厌一件艺术品，确实可能出于错误的理由。

DMost people like to see in pictures what they would also like to see in reality. This is quite a natural preference. We all like beauty in nature, and are grateful to the artists who have preserved it in their works. Nor would these artists themselves have rebuffed us for our taste. When the great Flemish painter Rubens made a drawing of his young son, he was surely proud of his good looks, and wanted us, too, to delight in the child's appearance. But this bias for the pretty and engaging subject is apt to become a stumbling block if it leads us to reject works which represent a less appealing subject.

大多数人都喜欢在画中看到自己在现实中也愿意看到的事物。这是一种十分自然的偏好。我们都喜爱自然之美，也会感谢那些把这种美保存在作品中的艺术家。这些艺术家自己也不会因我们的这种品味而对我们冷眼相待。佛兰德斯大画家 Rubens 为年幼的儿子作画时，必定为孩子俊美的容貌感到骄傲，也希望我们同样欣赏他的外表。然而，如果我们偏爱漂亮、讨喜的题材，以至于拒绝描绘不那么吸引人的题材的作品，这种偏好就很容易成为障碍。

EThe great German painter Dürer certainly drew his mother with as much devotion and love as Rubens felt for his child. His truthful study of old age may give us a shock which makes us turn away from it - and yet, if we fight against that first reaction we may be richly rewarded. For Dürer's drawing in its tremendous sincerity is a great work. The beauty of a picture does not really lie in the beauty of its subject matter. The trouble about beauty is that tastes and standards of what is beautiful vary so much.

德国大画家 Dürer 描绘母亲时，所倾注的用心与爱意肯定不亚于 Rubens 对孩子的感情。他对老年真实不饰的描绘或许会令我们震惊，使我们转身避开；然而，如果能克服第一反应，我们可能会得到丰厚回报，因为 Dürer 的这幅素描极其真挚，是一件杰作。画作之美其实并不在于题材本身是否美丽。谈论美的难处在于，人们对美的品味和标准差异极大。

FWhat is true of beauty is also true of facial expression. In fact, it is often the expression of a figure in the painting which makes us like or loathe the work. Some people like an expression - perhaps of suffering or of compassion - which they can readily understand, and which therefore moves them profoundly. But we should not, for that reason, turn away from works whose expression is perhaps less easy to understand. Often we must first learn to know the artist's methods of drawing to understand the feelings he or she wishes to portray, and may even come to prefer works of art in which expression is not obvious. Just as some of us would rather people use few words and gestures and leave something to be guessed, so some of us would rather look at paintings or sculptures which are open to interpretation.

适用于美的道理，也同样适用于面部表情。事实上，常常正是画中人物的表情，使我们喜欢或厌恶一件作品。有些人喜欢自己很容易理解、因而能深受感动的表情，例如痛苦或怜悯。但我们不应因此避开那些表情可能较难理解的作品。我们往往必须先了解艺术家的绘画方法，才能理解他或她想表现的感情；我们甚至可能逐渐更偏爱那些情感表达并不直白的艺术作品。正如有些人宁愿别人少用言语和手势，留下一些内容供人揣摩，也有人更愿意欣赏可以有多种解读的绘画或雕塑。

GBut here newcomers to art are often brought up against another difficulty. They want to admire the artist's skill in representing the things they see. What they like best are paintings which 'look real'. This is an important consideration, and the patience and skill which go into the faithful rendering of the visible world are indeed to be admired. Great artists of the past have devoted much labour to works in which every tiny detail is carefully recorded. Dürer's watercolour study of a hare is one of the most famous examples of this loving patience. But who would say that Rembrandt's drawing of an elephant is necessarily less good because it shows fewer details? Indeed Rembrandt gave us the feel of the elephant's wrinkly skin with just a few lines of his chalk.

不过，刚接触艺术的人常常会在这里遇到另一项困难。他们希望赞叹艺术家再现所见事物的技巧，最喜欢的是那些“看起来真实”的画作。这确实是一项重要考量；为了忠实描绘可见世界而付出的耐心与技巧，也的确值得赞赏。过去的伟大艺术家在作品中倾注大量心血，细致记录每一个微小细节。Dürer 以水彩描绘野兔的习作，就是这种满怀热爱的耐心最著名的例子之一。但谁会因为 Rembrandt 画的大象细节较少，就断言它一定比较差呢？事实上，Rembrandt 只用寥寥几笔粉笔线条，就让我们感受到大象皱褶皮肤的质感。

HIf we find fault with the accuracy of a picture, we must remember that artists may have their reasons for changing the appearance of what they see. We should also never condemn a work for being incorrectly drawn unless we are quite sure that we are right and the painter is wrong. We have a curious habit of thinking that nature must always look like the pictures we are accustomed to. For generations, paintings showed horses galloping with outstretched legs in full flight through the air, and we came to believe that this was indeed how they moved. It was only the development of photography which proved that horses simply do not gallop in that way. And yet, when painters began to apply this new discovery, and painted horses moving as they actually do, everyone complained that their pictures looked wrong.

如果我们挑剔一幅画不够准确，就必须记得，艺术家改变所见事物的外观可能自有理由。除非完全确定自己正确而画家有误，否则绝不应仅因一件作品画得“不准确”就加以谴责。我们有一种奇怪的习惯，总以为自然界必然与自己看惯的图画一样。几代人的画作都把奔马画成四肢伸展、整个身体腾空飞驰的样子，于是我们开始相信马确实是这样奔跑的。直到摄影技术发展起来，才证明马根本不会以这种方式奔驰。然而，当画家开始运用这一新发现，按照马的实际动作作画时，人人却都抱怨这些画看起来不对。
