Child’s Play in Medieval England

中世纪英格兰的儿童游戏

Professor Nicholas Orme investigates childhood in the Middle Ages

Nicholas Orme 教授考察中世纪的童年生活

A‘Play up! Play up! And play the game!’ The ringing chorus of Sir Henry Newbolt’s celebrated cricket poem ‘Vitai Lampada’ (1908) sums up views about play in 19th- and early 20th-century Britain. Children’s play was important, and adults should regulate and direct it. Games promoted endurance, self-discipline and team spirit – qualities needed for the health of society and government. Newbolt was one in a long line of people who thought in this way. The notion that children’s play should be used for educational and social purposes goes back at least to the ancient Greeks and the toys and games of medieval England tell us much about how adults then saw childhood. But they also reveal a good deal about children themselves, and cast light on what has recently become a controversial issue.

“加油！加油！好好比赛！”Henry Newbolt 爵士的著名板球诗《Vitai Lampada》（1908）中这句响亮的叠句，概括了19世纪及20世纪初英国人对游戏的看法。儿童游戏很重要，成年人应当加以规范和引导。游戏能够培养耐力、自律和团队精神，而这些品质是社会和政府健康运转所需要的。Newbolt 只是众多持这种观点者中的一员。儿童游戏应当服务于教育和社会目的，这种观念至少可以追溯到古希腊；中世纪英格兰的玩具和游戏让我们充分了解当时成年人如何看待童年。不过，它们也揭示了许多儿童自身的情况，并为一个近来颇具争议的问题提供了线索。

BForty years ago, the French historian Philippe Aries argued, in Centuries of Childhood, that childhood in the Middle Ages did not exist in its modern sense. Children were regarded by adults with relatively little affection and followed a way of life not very different from that of their elders. More recent historians have disputed this, pointing to plentiful signs of parental affection and arguing that childhood, by its very nature, must always have been much the same.

40年前，法国历史学家 Philippe Aries 在《童年的世纪》（Centuries of Childhood）中提出，中世纪并不存在现代意义上的童年。成年人对儿童的关爱相对较少，而儿童的生活方式与年长者也没有太大区别。较近时期的历史学家对此提出异议：他们指出，父母关爱的迹象十分丰富，并认为从童年的本质来看，它必定一直都大体相同。

CIn this debate play is crucial. Did adults encourage it? If so, did they see it as recreational (by providing toys, for example) or as educational (making children play in particular ways)? Did children play as their elders told them, or did they invent their own games, away from adults and even against their wishes? These questions can be answered from a rich body of evidence including actual toys of the period, pictures of children’s activities in contemporary manuscripts and literary sources such as religious works and dictionaries. We know not only how medieval English children played but what their elders thought on the subject.

在这场争论中，游戏至关重要。成年人会鼓励儿童游戏吗？如果会，他们把游戏视为娱乐，例如给孩子提供玩具，还是把它视为教育，让儿童以特定方式玩耍？儿童会按照长辈的要求游戏，还是会远离成年人、甚至违背他们的意愿，创造自己的游戏？大量丰富证据可以回答这些问题，其中包括当时的实物玩具、同时代手稿中描绘儿童活动的图像，以及宗教著作和词典等文字资料。我们不仅知道中世纪英格兰儿童如何游戏，也知道他们的长辈对此有何看法。

DWe know, for example, that adults gave children toys from infancy onwards. In 1398 the writer John Trevisa describes babies playing with ‘a child’s brooch’, an object similar in function to the bright plastic toys given to babies today to bite and handle. William Horman, 16th-century author of a Latin textbook, talks of buying a rattle to stop a baby crying. Indeed, by 1300 it appears there was a toy industry in England. Boys’ toys often took military forms, such as the two metal soldiers of that date, found in London, and made from a mould. Girls had dolls, known before the 17th century as ‘poppets’ or puppets, commercially manufactured, imported and, in 1582, taxed at a halfpenny each. Not that children were by any means dependent on things that were bought for them. Gerald of Wales recalls how he and his brothers built towns, palaces, churches and monasteries from sand at Manorbier Castle in about 1150 (perhaps on the nearby beach).

例如，我们知道，成年人从孩子还是婴儿时起就会给他们玩具。1398年，作家 John Trevisa 描述婴儿玩一种“儿童胸针”（a child’s brooch）；它在功能上类似于今天给婴儿啃咬和抓握的鲜艳塑料玩具。16世纪拉丁语教科书作者 William Horman 提到过买摇铃来让婴儿停止哭泣。事实上，到1300年时，英格兰似乎已经出现了玩具产业。男孩的玩具常以军事事物为造型，例如在伦敦发现的两件约于当时用模具铸成的金属士兵玩具。女孩则有玩偶；17世纪以前，这类玩偶称为“poppets”或“puppets”，由商家生产，也从国外进口，并在1582年按每个半便士征税。不过，儿童绝不是只能依靠成年人为他们购买东西。Gerald of Wales 回忆说，约1150年，他和兄弟们曾在曼诺比尔城堡（Manorbier Castle，或许是在附近的海滩上）用沙子建造城镇、宫殿、教堂和修道院。

EActive games were universal. In his English-to-Latin dictionary of 1440, a mysterious recluse known as Geoffrey mentions children playing ‘tennis’ and swinging on what he calls a ‘totter’ or ‘merrytotter’. Children chased each other, swam and played ball games. Boys in the later Middle Ages shot arrows with bows, and archery is an example of a sport encouraged by adults, who wanted boys to grow up to play their part in what was, for most men, a warrior society.

需要身体活动的游戏十分普遍。在一本1440年的英拉词典中，一位人称 Geoffrey 的神秘隐士提到，儿童会玩“网球”，也会在他所谓的“totter”或“merrytotter”上荡来荡去。孩子们互相追逐、游泳并进行球类游戏。中世纪后期，男孩会用弓射箭；射箭正是一项受到成年人鼓励的运动，因为他们希望男孩长大后能在社会中尽到自己的职责，而对当时大多数男子来说，那是一个尚武的社会。

FHowever, by no means all children’s military activity was directed by adults. In 1400, six months after King Richard II had been overthrown by Henry IV, the children of London gathered together and chose themselves kings. Adam of Usk tells us they congregated ‘in thousands … and made war upon each other … whereby many died’. The new king had to order their parents and masters to stop them.

然而，儿童的军事活动绝非全都由成年人引导。1400年，Richard II 国王被 Henry IV 推翻6个月后，伦敦的儿童聚集起来，自行选出国王。Adam of Usk 告诉我们，他们“数以千计地聚集……彼此交战……因此许多人死亡”。新国王不得不命令他们的父母和师长制止他们。

GThis willingness of children to take the initiative in play, not just to wait for adults to direct them, is evident in the way they observed the calendar. Medieval life was regulated by light, weather, crops and the Church’s cycle of fasts and festivals. Children shared in the adult round of religious festivals, but had their own observances as well, semi-detached from their elders. In the 1200s, the priest Thomas Docking remarked that in spring a child ‘follows the ploughman; in autumn he accompanies the grape-gatherers’. In 1518, the poet Alexander Barclay records children playing with tops in March, looking for fruit in summer and making footballs by filling pigs’ bladders with dried peas in autumn.

儿童愿意在游戏中主动行事，而不只等成年人引导；他们按照历法安排活动的方式，就体现了这一点。中世纪生活受日照、天气、农作物以及教会斋戒与节庆周期的支配。儿童会参加成年人一轮又一轮的宗教节庆，但也有自己的一套活动习俗，与长辈的活动相对独立。13世纪，牧师 Thomas Docking 说，春天里，儿童“跟着耕田人；到了秋天，他又陪伴采葡萄的人”。1518年，诗人 Alexander Barclay 记载，儿童3月玩陀螺，夏天寻找水果，秋天则把干豌豆装进猪膀胱做成足球。

HIt is clear from this evidence that the relationship between adults and children in medieval society was, in major respects, a modern one. Parents took an indulgent interest in their children, providing them with toys and giving them time to play. Children played in a wide variety of ways – imaginatively, skilfully, athletically and violently – developing their minds, bodies and social skills. Adults tried at times to direct play – partly to keep children in order, partly to give them skills for adult life – but children gravitated to one another and formed a culture of their own. We can answer Aries’s thesis, then, with a resounding ‘No!’. Childhood has always been much the same.

这些证据清楚表明，中世纪社会中成年人和儿童的关系，在主要方面与现代相似。父母怀着宽容宠爱的态度关心孩子，给他们玩具，也给他们游戏的时间。儿童以各种各样的方式玩耍——充满想象、技巧娴熟、富有运动性，有时也很暴力——并在此过程中发展心智、身体和社交技能。成年人有时试图引导游戏，一方面是让儿童守规矩，另一方面是培养他们成年生活所需的技能；但儿童会自然聚在一起，形成自己的文化。因此，我们可以用一声响亮的“不！”回应 Aries 的论点。童年一直以来都大体相同。
