The History of the Pencil

铅笔的历史

The history of the pencil starts with a violent thunderstorm. When some particularly ferocious weather struck the Lake District in North West England in the sixteenth century, locals in the village of Borrowdale discovered a large uprooted tree. Underneath the tree lay an unknown black substance which we now know as graphite. It was slightly shiny and smooth to the touch. And it left a black smear on the hands of all who touched it.

铅笔的历史始于一场猛烈的雷暴。16世纪，英格兰西北部湖区（Lake District）遭遇一次格外凶猛的暴风雨，博罗代尔（Borrowdale）村的居民发现了一棵被连根拔起的大树。树下有一种当时不为人知的黑色物质，也就是我们今天所说的石墨。它略带光泽，摸起来很光滑，任何人碰过以后，手上都会留下黑色痕迹。

Initially, the local farmers used the newly discovered material as a handy way to identify their sheep. However, others quickly realised the potential for using this intriguing substance to write on paper. When it was untreated the material was very soft, which meant that it was messy to handle. To make it fit for use with paper, people enclosed a thin core of the substance in stiff sheep hides or rope. At this time chemistry was still in its infancy. People searched for a word to describe this increasingly useful substance and came up with plumbago which, in Latin, means acts or writes like lead. Later the name was changed to graphite. But because words have remarkable staying power, we still call graphite the lead of a pencil even though it is now known that there is no trace of real lead in graphite.

起初，当地农民把这种新发现的材料当作给自家绵羊做标记的方便工具。不过，其他人很快意识到，这种奇特物质还可以用来在纸上书写。未经处理时，这种材料很软，使用起来容易弄脏。为了让它适合写在纸上，人们用硬羊皮或绳子包住一根细细的材料芯。当时，化学仍处于起步阶段。人们寻找一个词来描述这种越来越有用的物质，最后称它为 plumbago；这个词在拉丁语中意为“性质或书写效果像铅”。后来，它被改名为石墨。但词语具有惊人的延续力，所以即使如今人们已经知道石墨中没有一丝真正的铅，我们仍将铅笔中的石墨芯称作 lead（铅）。

Graphite has a very high melting point at around 3,500 degrees Celsius. This made it invaluable to the British army and navy as a secret ingredient in the manufacture of cannon balls. The Royal Ordnance, or weaponry section of the British armed forces, used graphite as a lining inside the moulds for cannon balls, which, as a result, the British could turn out faster and more cheaply than their European rivals. In addition to its value to the armed forces, the government quickly realised the commercial potential of the graphite at Borrowdale, and assumed control of all the mines there during the sixteenth century. Armed guards accompanied the precious graphite all the way down to the metal foundries by the naval shipyards in the south of England. The graphite was so valuable that the locals, who called it wad, started to steal it. As a deterrent, an act of Parliament in 1752 made this offence punishable by time in prison.

石墨的熔点非常高，约为3,500摄氏度，因此成为英国陆军和海军制造炮弹时极有价值的一种秘密材料。英国武装部队负责军械的皇家军械部门（Royal Ordnance），用石墨为炮弹模具制作内衬；这样一来，英国生产炮弹的速度比欧洲对手更快，成本也更低。除了对军队的重要性，政府也很快意识到博罗代尔石墨的商业潜力，于16世纪接管了当地所有矿井。武装守卫一路护送珍贵的石墨，把它运往英格兰南部海军造船厂附近的金属铸造厂。石墨价值极高，当地人把它称为 wad，并开始偷取。为了震慑偷盗者，1752年议会通过法案，规定这一罪行可判处监禁。

The Italians originally invented the wooden casing to hold a thin rod of plumbago firmly in place for ease of writing. Italian craftsmen hollowed out two small sections of cedar wood, into one of which they laid the lead. They then glued the other section over the top and left the two halves to set. When dry, the whole apparatus formed what today we know as a pencil. The Germans took this technique and developed it further by applying mass-production techniques to pencils. At the same time Nicolas-Jacques Conté, a French officer in Napoleon Bonaparte’s army during the late 1700s, developed a method of mixing powdered graphite and clay together for firing in a kiln. Adding more clay to the mixture helped make the pencil harder, sharper, and more precise in its mark. More graphite helped make a pencil mark that was softer, thicker and darker.

最初是意大利人发明了木制外壳，用来牢牢固定一根细 plumbago 芯，方便书写。意大利工匠把两小块雪松木挖空，在其中一块里放入铅笔芯，再把另一块粘在上面，等两半粘合牢固。晾干后，成品就是我们今天所说的铅笔。德国人在这项技术基础上继续改进，把大规模生产方法应用于铅笔制造。与此同时，18世纪末 Napoleon Bonaparte 军队中的法国军官 Nicolas-Jacques Conté 发明了一种方法，把石墨粉与黏土混合，再放入窑中烧制。混合物中加入更多黏土，会让铅笔更硬、更尖，笔迹也更精细；加入更多石墨，则会让笔迹更柔和、更粗、更黑。

The varying quality of pencil leads eventually gave rise to a system for categorizing the fineness of the pencil mark. Pencil manufacturers all over the world still use this so-called HB grading system today. The H stands for the Hardness of the pencil while the B stands for its Blackness. An HB pencil is a standard pencil and a variety of letters and numbers are used to designate different types of lead.

铅笔芯特性的差异，最终催生出一套划分笔迹粗细的系统。如今，世界各地的铅笔制造商仍在使用这套称为HB的分级制度。H代表铅笔的硬度，B代表笔迹的黑度。HB铅笔是标准铅笔，其他不同类型的笔芯则用各种字母和数字标示。

Significant seams of graphite exist in parts of China, which now produces most of the world’s pencils. Interestingly, the Borrowdale mine in the Lake District remains the only significant source of graphite in its near-pure form in the world. Nowadays the highest grade of graphite at Borrowdale is totally exhausted, although other grades can still be found, and England’s pencil industry continues to thrive in the nearby town of Keswick.

中国部分地区有规模可观的石墨矿层，如今世界上大多数铅笔都由中国生产。有意思的是，湖区的博罗代尔矿至今仍是世界上唯一重要的近乎纯净石墨来源。现在，博罗代尔最高品级的石墨已经完全枯竭，不过其他品级仍可找到；在附近的凯西克镇（Keswick），英格兰铅笔产业依旧蓬勃发展。

The pencil has turned out to be a remarkably resilient and valuable tool whose use has survived well into our high-tech times, as a well-known story shows. It is sometimes said that the American space programme spent millions of dollars to invent a pen capable of writing in the zero gravity of space. The Russians, by contrast, simply equipped their astronauts with good old-fashioned pencils that never let them down. It should be pointed out though, that the popular myth about Americans overlooking the practical advantages of pencils in zero gravity is merely fiction. In actual fact, both American and Russian astronauts were equipped with pencils in their respective countries’ first space flights. A private company later developed pens for writing in zero gravity. In fact, astronauts of every nation now use pens. But no matter — pencils remain in use in every classroom, every planning, building and drawing office, and in every art studio in the world. And there is nothing to suggest that we are likely to invent anything better than graphite to use in our pencils.

事实证明，铅笔是一种适应力极强、价值很高的工具，即使进入高科技时代，人们仍一直使用它；一则广为流传的故事体现了这一点。人们有时说，美国太空计划花费数百万美元，发明了一支能在太空失重环境中书写的笔。相比之下，俄罗斯人只是给宇航员配备了可靠的传统铅笔，从未让他们失望。不过需要指出的是，这个关于美国人忽视铅笔在失重环境中实用优势的流行说法纯属虚构。事实上，美国和俄罗斯在各自首次太空飞行中都给宇航员配备了铅笔。后来，一家私营公司开发出能在失重环境中书写的笔。如今，各国宇航员其实都使用笔。但这并不影响铅笔继续出现在世界各地的每间教室、每个规划、建筑和绘图办公室，以及每间艺术工作室中。而且，没有任何迹象表明，我们可能发明出比石墨更适合放进铅笔的材料。
