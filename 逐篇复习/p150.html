In a career spanning some fifty years, Margaret Mahy has come to occupy a unique place in New Zealand writing with innovative fiction and original characterisation. In our solidly realist tradition, the linguistic fireworks of Mahy’s children’s fiction and the explorations of human behaviour at the heart of her supernatural teen fiction stand gloriously alone. Mahy’s clearest heir, I have always thought, is Elizabeth Knox — and maybe the true inheritance is the sheer singularity. Just as there is no one else like Knox in New Zealand writing, Mahy, too, has ventured into imaginative territory unknown to other local writers. So it was with great pleasure that I visited Mahy recently at her Governor’s Bay home to talk about her new book. As with all journalists, she is generous to a fault with her time and attention; and, as always — despite her careful consideration of questions and thoughtful answers — I’m reminded she isn’t truly comfortable making herself or her life the heart of any conversation.

在约50年的写作生涯中，Margaret Mahy 凭借创新的小说和独到的人物塑造，在新西兰文学中占据了独一无二的位置。在我们根基深厚的现实主义传统中，她的儿童小说绽放出绚丽的语言烟花，以超自然题材写成的青少年小说则把对人类行为的探索置于核心，两者都卓然独立。我一直认为，Elizabeth Knox 最明确地继承了 Mahy 的衣钵——或许真正传承下来的，正是这种无可比拟的独特性。正如新西兰文学中无人可与 Knox 相比，Mahy 也曾闯入本地其他作家从未涉足的想象疆域。因此，最近我怀着极大的喜悦前往 Mahy 位于总督湾的家中，与她谈论新书。她对所有记者都慷慨得过了头，毫不吝惜时间和关注；而且一如既往，尽管她会仔细思考问题并认真作答，我仍意识到，让自己或自己的生活成为谈话中心，其实会让她感到不自在。

Certainly, any consideration of the style of Mahy’s novels and picture books throws up some irresistible theories. As a writer committed to supporting herself through her art, she has seldom had the time for formal research. Rather, it has been a matter of going out and finding inspiration from her immediate environment: the writing on the side of a bus; a spelling mistake in a note to herself; the similarity between a cat and a fur hat. But despite these sometimes mundane origins, the settings for her stories are delightfully varied, as the books celebrate the dramatic plot twists and unpredictability of adventures on the high seas or in Antarctica, and also in quiet, unassuming places like the library or even down the back of a chair at home. Mahy has a lifelong affection for characters who are agents of upheaval and disturbance. Her junior and picture books are peppered with pirates, robbers and lions, though they appear alongside librarians, mothers and children, working against comfortable stereotype. Her fictions often have at their heart a young adult burdened with special powers, such as the ability to cast spells in order to transform their world in supernatural and fantastical ways. Another common feature is that, while the conclusions of her tales are usually predictable, they leave the reader feeling absolutely complete, the moral questions resulting from our hero’s powers having been resolved.

当然，只要思考 Mahy 小说和图画书的风格，就会涌现出一些令人难以抗拒的推断。作为一名致力于靠创作养活自己的作家，她很少有时间开展正式研究。她更多是走出去，从身边环境中寻找灵感：公交车车身上的文字、写给自己的便条里一个拼写错误、猫与皮帽之间的相似之处。然而，这些灵感有时虽源自平凡日常，她的故事背景却多姿多彩，令人愉悦。书中既有公海或南极洲冒险那戏剧性的情节转折和不可预测性，也有图书馆这样安静、不起眼的地方，甚至还有家中椅背后面的空间。Mahy 一生都喜爱那些带来动荡与骚乱的角色。她为低龄儿童创作的书和图画书中遍布海盗、强盗和狮子，但这些角色又与图书管理员、母亲和孩子同时出现，颠覆了人们习以为常的刻板印象。她的小说常以一名身负特殊力量的年轻人为核心，例如拥有施展法术的能力，能以超自然的奇幻方式改变自己的世界。另一个常见特点是，她的故事结局通常可以预料，却会让读者感到圆满无缺，因为主人公的力量引发的道德问题都得到了解决。

Many of these themes can be found in her new novel, The Magician of Hoad. The book was begun more than 15 years ago and envisioned as an ‘entire’ fantasy — one set in a fully imagined world with detailed history and complex tribal inter-relationships, a classical hero quest at its heart. The story ballooned at one point to 800 pages and has been through at least two substantial rewrites. Now half its original size, it is a fascinating read — an adventure, a romance, and a gold mine of Mahy literary preoccupations.

她的新小说《霍德的魔法师》中可以看到许多这类主题。这本书始于15年多以前，最初构想为一部“完整的”奇幻作品：故事设在一个完全虚构的世界中，有详尽的历史和复杂的部族关系，核心是一场经典的英雄探索之旅。故事篇幅一度膨胀到800页，至少经历了两次大幅重写。如今它只有原来一半的篇幅，读来十分迷人——既是冒险故事，也是爱情故事，更是一座蕴藏 Mahy 文学关注主题的金矿。

The other splendid Mahy publication this year is a re-issue of Bubble Trouble in a now illustrated edition. This tongue-twister tale first appeared in 1991, was included in 100 New Zealand Poems (1993) and has been recited by Mahy at countless private and public functions. Perhaps more than any other work, Bubble Trouble is the Mahy that New Zealand children and their parents know so well, the rollicking story of a clown who serves up a joyous torrent of word play and unexpected rhyme.

Mahy 今年出版的另一部佳作，是重新发行的《泡泡麻烦》，这次推出了插图版。这个绕口令般的故事最早发表于1991年，后被收入《新西兰诗歌100首》一书，1993年出版；Mahy 也曾在不计其数的私人和公共活动上朗诵它。新西兰儿童及其父母所熟悉的 Mahy，或许在《泡泡麻烦》中体现得比任何其他作品都更鲜明。这是一个热闹欢腾的小丑故事，奉上一股令人愉悦的文字游戏与意外押韵的洪流。

Those connections are four or five deep now. I read The Lion in the Meadow in the School Journal in the late 1960s; my stepdaughter listened to The Boy Who Was Followed Home over and over in the 1970s; my own children sat very still throughout The Great White Man-Eating Shark in the 80s and 90s; now in the 21st century, my grandchildren have heard Down the Dragon’s Tongue, A Summery Saturday Morning and Dashing Dog many times.

如今，这种联系已延续四五代。20世纪60年代末，我在《学校期刊》上读到《草地上的狮子》；70年代，我的继女反复听《被一路跟回家的男孩》；80和90年代，我自己的孩子听《大白食人鲨》时从头到尾都安静地坐着；如今到了21世纪，我的孙辈也已多次听过《沿龙舌而下》《夏意盎然的星期六早晨》和《飞奔的狗》。

Of course, readers are important to any working writer, but Mahy’s espousal of the act of reading goes beyond that: a book is not properly finished, she has often said, until it has been read, because a reader brings something important to the book. So, doubtless out of need to build a market – she’s not ignorant of her popularity – but out of genuine care for that other dynamic part of the author-reader relationship, Mahy has, until recently, kept up a punishing schedule of public appearances.

当然，读者对任何以写作为业的作家都很重要，但 Mahy 对阅读行为的推崇不止于此。她常说，一本书在被阅读以前并没有真正完成，因为读者会为它带来某种重要的东西。因此，无疑既是出于建立市场的需要——她并非不知道自己广受欢迎——也是出于对作者与读者关系中另一方——读者——的真切关怀，直到最近，Mahy 都一直保持着令人不堪重负的公开露面日程。

Private conversation with Mahy has always been a wild ride — marvellous, in the true sense of the word, the product of a hungry head and an infinite capacity to be astonished. She races away at one stage to consult an encyclopedia for L M Montgomery’s date of death, and speculates about the ‘real’ Montgomery, creator of the ever-popular Anne of Green Gables. Although I’d come to talk about her new book, I couldn’t help but be captivated by her infectious curiosity.

与 Mahy 私下交谈总像经历一场狂野之旅——以这个词最本真的含义来说，奇妙非凡；这是她求知若渴的头脑和永远能感到惊奇的能力共同造就的。谈到一半，她突然快步离开，去查百科全书上 L M Montgomery 的逝世日期，又推想这位长盛不衰的《绿山墙的安妮》的创作者“真实”是什么样子。虽然我是为谈她的新书而来，却不由自主地被她那富有感染力的好奇心迷住。

A few years ago, the writer David Hill told a funny story. Though his writing was, he conceded, very different from Mahy’s, he had been affected by her peculiarly alert way of looking at the world, particularly the mad, slippery life of language. Once, Hill said, in a motel room, a sign on a door caught his eye: ‘This door is alarmed.’ Mahy would like that, thought Hill. She would enjoy the comedy just below the surface of the formal warning; she would leap immediately to the possibilities for story and language play: ‘Yes, and this window is concerned, this light fitting is irritated.’ His story was a wonderful comment on Mahy’s vision.

几年前，作家 David Hill 讲过一个有趣的故事。他承认，自己的写作与 Mahy 截然不同，却受到她那种格外敏锐的观察世界方式影响，尤其是她看待语言那疯狂而难以捉摸的生命力的方式。Hill 说，有一次在汽车旅馆房间里，门上的一块告示吸引了他的目光：“This door is alarmed。”这句话既表示门装有警报器，也可理解为门“受惊了”。Hill 心想，Mahy 一定会喜欢。她会欣赏正式警示语表层下方的喜剧意味，并立刻跃入故事和文字游戏的种种可能：“是啊，这扇窗很担心，这盏灯具很恼火。”他的故事是对 Mahy 观察世界方式的绝妙评说。
