An uncertain future

不确定的未来

AFew people pay much attention to coins; we take them for granted. Since the advent of paper currency and the abandonment of a monetary system based on intrinsic or actual value, they have been demoted to the status of change: what is left when you have paid for something with ‘paper’ money. There is even a belief that they will one day be obsolete, as will paper or plastic notes, and money will at last have achieved the pure status of a mathematical formulation, a system of electronic credits and debits.

很少有人特别留意硬币，我们把它们视为理所当然。纸币出现、以货币内在或实际价值为基础的货币制度被弃用以后，硬币的地位降为零钱，也就是用纸币付款后找回的零头。甚至有人认为，有朝一日硬币将被淘汰，纸质或塑料钞票也会如此；到那时，货币终于会达到纯粹的数学表达形式，成为一套电子收支系统。

BIn the modern media environment, the sheer number of visual images is overwhelming, but it makes one stop to think that coins are among the most durable of our artefacts, and future archaeologists will scrutinise coins in order to learn about their distant ancestors. This is exactly the way ancient coins have informed us for over four millennia. There are thousands of different coins, each designed to carry meaning as well as value, tiny but rich sources of documentary evidence about events and cultural attitudes, frequently produced with exquisite craftsmanship.

现代媒体环境中的视觉图像数量之多，令人难以招架。然而，想到硬币是我们最耐久的人工制品之一，不免让人停下来思考：未来的考古学家会仔细研究硬币，以了解他们遥远的祖先。四千多年来，古代硬币正是以这种方式向我们提供信息。不同硬币数以千计，每一种都经过设计，既承载价值，也传达含义。它们体积虽小，却是有关历史事件和文化态度的丰富文献证据来源，而且往往工艺极为精美。

Q16 · 正确标题未作答

A simpler, more efficient way of trading

更简单、更高效的交易方式

你的标题

未作答

CCoins in Europe date from 2,500 to 2,700 years ago, and seem to have originated in the ancient kingdom of Lydia, in what is now Turkey. Before coins, trade was based on barter: the direct trade of commodities. Barter required the physical presence of both commodities that were being traded. Metal coins were far more portable, and not subject to the same spoilage on long sea journeys as a commodity like grain might be if it became wet. However, they could also be accumulated, and these hoards could be stolen. Coins were therefore a temptation to criminals. The Greeks, however, were the first to realise the full potential of the new invention. They were a trading people of seafarers. Their civilisation, which was composed of dozens of city-states, was highly decentralised. Despite the potential for theft, the Greeks realised that they could benefit enormously from a system that used coins, because it meant that they no longer had to carry goods with them to barter.

欧洲的硬币可追溯到2,500至2,700年前，似乎起源于古代吕底亚王国（Lydia），即今天的土耳其境内。硬币出现以前，贸易以物易物，也就是直接交换商品。以物易物要求双方要交换的商品同时在场。金属硬币便于携带得多；在漫长海上旅程中，它们也不会像谷物等商品那样，因受潮而腐坏。然而，硬币也可以大量积存，而这些积存的硬币可能被盗，因此会成为罪犯觊觎的目标。不过，希腊人最先认识到这项新发明的全部潜力。他们是从事贸易的航海民族，其文明由数十个城邦组成，权力高度分散。尽管存在失窃风险，希腊人仍意识到，硬币制度能带来巨大利益，因为他们不再需要随身携带商品去交换。

Q17 · 正确标题未作答

Calculating what a coin was worth

计算一枚硬币的价值

你的标题

未作答

DThe value of coins was generally based on the intrinsic value of the metal they were made of. At first this was mostly silver, and this meant that the discovery of silver mines on Greek territory actually increased the quantity of money they could produce. Had the Greek mining been on a larger scale, it could have caused problems similar to those faced by the Spanish centuries later, when so much gold from the New World was brought into their economy that it resulted in serious inflation. One advantage of intrinsic value was that coins could be weighed to determine the amount of metal they contained, so that the only thing an issuing state had to guarantee was the purity of the metal to prevent accusations of fraud and cheating, which could cause fighting and unrest. This was quite difficult to do, especially with coins made of gold, until Archimedes realised that different metals have different weight-volume ratios, and that by measuring the amount of displacement when coins were immersed in water, it was possible to establish whether gold was alloyed with silver.

硬币的价值通常取决于铸造金属本身的价值。最初使用的金属主要是银，这意味着在希腊领土上发现银矿，确实增加了他们能够制造的货币数量。假如希腊采矿规模更大，就可能引发类似几个世纪后西班牙面临的问题：大量来自新大陆（New World）的黄金进入西班牙经济，造成严重通货膨胀。内在价值制度的一项优势是，人们可以给硬币称重，以确定其中所含金属的数量。因此，发行硬币的国家只需保证金属纯度，防止欺诈和作弊指控；这类指控可能导致冲突和动荡。不过，这一点相当难以做到，金币尤其如此；直到 Archimedes 认识到不同金属的重量与体积之比不同，并发现通过测量硬币浸入水中时的排水量，可以判断黄金是否掺入了白银，问题才得以解决。

Q18 · 正确标题未作答

A form of regional promotion

一种地区宣传方式

你的标题

未作答

EAll ancient coins were made by hand using engraved metal moulds. Many hundreds of coins were made, and as a result the moulds often cracked and new ones had to be engraved. This explains the endless variety encountered in coins with the same designs because the moulds had to be remade repeatedly, by many different artisans. One of the distinctive features of coins, in fact, is that they are the work of anonymous craftsmen, and yet each coin is an intimate reflection of the character of its city of origin. It was the Greeks who realised that coins could have a double function, as a unit of value and as a symbol of the identity of the city that issued them. The Greek view of life was deeply motivated by a spirit of competition, and coins, which circulated around the trading world, were a way of advertising the qualities of each particular city-state.

所有古代硬币都用雕刻过的金属模具手工制作。人们会用同一模具制造数百枚硬币，模具因此经常开裂，必须重新雕刻。即使设计相同的硬币也变化无穷，原因就在于模具要由许多不同工匠反复重制。事实上，硬币的一项鲜明特点是，它们出自无名工匠之手，但每一枚都细致反映了发行城市的特质。希腊人认识到，硬币可以发挥双重作用：既是价值单位，也是发行城市的身份象征。希腊人的人生观深受竞争精神驱动，而在贸易世界中流通的硬币，正是宣传各个城邦特质的一种方式。

Q19 · 正确标题未作答

Unchanging coins signalled trustworthiness

保持不变的硬币传递可信度

你的标题

未作答

FInterestingly, Greek coins from the city of Athens, which was the home of the greatest sculptors of the classical period, seem to have been completely disconnected from the high sculptural tradition. The beautiful features of the city’s patron, Athena (with her owl on the reverse of the coin), seem to have been repeated virtually unaltered for many years. Perhaps this was because the Athenian currency had grown increasingly to be the international standard and, like the US dollar today, a uniform design implied integrity.

有意思的是，雅典是古典时期最伟大雕塑家的故乡，但该城的希腊硬币似乎与这种高水平雕塑传统完全脱节。城邦守护神 Athena 美丽的面容，以及硬币背面的猫头鹰，多年来似乎一直被反复采用，几乎没有变化。或许这是因为雅典货币日益成为国际标准；如同今天的美元，统一的设计意味着可靠和诚信。

Q20 · 正确标题未作答

Putting a face to famous historical names

让著名历史人物有了具体面孔

你的标题

未作答

GOne side of a coin was always occupied by the head of a divinity, so it was a relatively small step, at around 323 BC, for Greek coins to replace that head with the portrait of the reigning monarch, a tradition that has continued in monarchies to the present day. Republics may have either symbolic heads going back to the older tradition of divinities or the features of a distinguished past head of state. The Romans adopted the Greek practice of coin design and as a result, the features of many great Romans have survived and are known to modern generations, despite the fact that the art of portrait painting was completely lost for more than a millennium following the fall of the Roman Empire.

硬币的一面原本总是铸有神祇头像，因此大约到公元前323年，希腊硬币把这一头像换成在位君主的肖像，只是相对不大的一步。这一传统在君主制国家一直延续至今。共和国的硬币上可能使用象征性头像，沿袭更早的神祇传统；也可能采用过去某位杰出国家元首的面容。罗马人沿用了希腊的硬币设计做法，因此许多伟大罗马人的面貌得以留存，并为现代人所知，尽管罗马帝国灭亡后，肖像画艺术曾彻底失传一千多年。
