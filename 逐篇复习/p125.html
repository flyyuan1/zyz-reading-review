How watching sport affects the brain

观看体育比赛如何影响大脑

AAt about the same time that the poet Homer invented the epic there, the ancient Greeks started a festival in which men competed in a single race, about 200 metres long. The winner received a branch of wild olives. The Greeks called this celebration the Olympics. Though the ancient sprint remains, today the Olympics are far more than that. Indeed, the Games seem to celebrate the dream of progress as embodied in the human form. That the Games are intoxicating to watch is beyond question. During the Athens Olympics in 2004, 3.4 billion people, half the world, watched them on television. Certainly, being a spectator is a thrilling experience—but why?

大约就在诗人 Homer 于古希腊创作史诗的同时，古希腊人开始举办一项节庆活动，男子在其中参加一场约200米长的赛跑。获胜者会得到一枝野生橄榄树枝。希腊人把这项庆典称为奥林匹克运动会。尽管古代的短跑项目保留至今，今天的奥运会所包含的内容早已远不止于此。事实上，奥运会似乎在颂扬一种由人的身体体现出来的进步梦想。毫无疑问，观看奥运会令人陶醉。2004年雅典奥运会期间，全球有34亿人通过电视观看赛事，约占世界人口的一半。作为观众当然令人兴奋，但原因何在？

BIn 1996, three Italian neuroscientists, Giacomo Rizzolatti, Leonardo Fogassi and Vittorio Gallese, examined the premotor cortex of monkeys. They discovered that inside these primate brains there were groups of cells that ‘store vocabularies of motor actions’, just as there are grammars of movement. These networks of cells are the bodily ‘sentences’ we use every day, the ones our brain has chosen to retain and refine. Think, for example, about a golf swing. To those who have only watched the Masters Tournament on TV, golfing seems easy. To the novice, however, the skill of casting a smooth arc with a lopsided metal stick is virtually impossible. This is because most novices swing with their consciousness, using an area of the brain next to the premotor cortex. To the expert, on the other hand, a perfectly balanced stroke is second nature. For him, the motor action has become memorized, and the movements are embedded in the neurons of his premotor cortex. He hits the ball with the tranquility of his perfected autopilot.

1996年，三位意大利神经科学家 Giacomo Rizzolatti、Leonardo Fogassi 和 Vittorio Gallese 研究了猴子的前运动皮层（premotor cortex）。他们发现，在这些灵长类动物的大脑中，有一些细胞群会“储存运动动作的词汇”，正如运动也有自己的语法一样。这些细胞网络构成了我们每天使用的身体“句子”，也就是大脑选择保留并不断完善的那些动作。以高尔夫挥杆为例。对于只在电视上看过美国大师赛（Masters Tournament）的人来说，打高尔夫似乎很容易。然而，对新手而言，要用一根形状不对称的金属球杆划出平滑的弧线，几乎是不可能的。这是因为大多数新手会有意识地挥杆，使用的是大脑中邻近前运动皮层的一个区域。相反，对高手来说，完美平衡的一击已经成为本能。对他而言，这个运动动作已经被记住，相关动作嵌入了前运动皮层的神经元中。他在那套已臻完善的“自动驾驶”系统支配下，从容地把球击出。

CThese neurons in the premotor cortex, besides explaining why certain athletes seem to possess almost unbelievable levels of skill, have an even more amazing characteristic, one that caused Rizzolatti, Fogassi and Gallese to give them the lofty title ‘mirror neurons’. They note that ‘the main functional characteristic of mirror neurons is that they become active both when the monkey performs a particular action (for example, grasping an object or holding it) and, astonishingly, when it sees another individual performing a similar action’. Humans have an even more elaborate mirror neuron system. These peculiar cells mirror, inside the brain, the outside world: they enable us to internalize the actions of another. In order to be activated, though, these cells require what the scientists call ‘goal-orientated movements’. If we are staring at a photograph, a fixed image of a runner mid-stride, our mirror neurons are totally silent. They only fire when the runner is active: running, moving or sprinting.

前运动皮层中的这些神经元不仅可以解释为何某些运动员似乎拥有近乎不可思议的高超技能，还有一个更加惊人的特点。正因如此，Rizzolatti、Fogassi 和 Gallese 给它们取了一个响亮的名称：“镜像神经元”（mirror neurons）。他们指出：“镜像神经元的主要功能特征是，当猴子做出某个特定动作时，例如抓取或拿住物体，它们会变得活跃；令人惊讶的是，当猴子看到另一个个体做出类似动作时，它们也会变得活跃。”人类拥有一套更加复杂的镜像神经元系统。这些奇特的细胞在大脑内部映照外部世界，使我们能够把他人的动作内化。不过，要激活这些细胞，动作必须是科学家所说的“目标导向型动作”。如果我们盯着一张照片，画面中的跑步者定格在迈步途中，我们的镜像神经元会完全沉默。只有跑步者真正活动起来，奔跑、移动或冲刺时，它们才会放电。

DWhat these electrophysiological studies indicate is that when we watch a golfer or a runner in action, the mirror neurons in our own premotor cortex light up as if we were the ones competing. This phenomenon of neural mirroring was first discovered in 1954, when two French physiologists, Gastaut and Bert, found that the brains of humans vibrate with two distinct wavelengths, alpha and mu. The mu system is involved in neural mirroring. It is active when our bodies are still, and disappears whenever we do something active, like playing a sport or changing the TV channel. The surprising fact is that the mu signal is also quiet when we watch someone else being active, as on TV. These results are the effect of mirror neurons.

这些电生理研究表明，当我们观看高尔夫球手或跑步者运动时，我们自己前运动皮层中的镜像神经元也会活跃起来，仿佛参赛的是我们自己。1954年，两位法国生理学家 Gastaut 和 Bert 发现，人脑会以阿尔法波（alpha）和缪波（mu）这两种不同波长振荡，由此首次发现了这种神经镜像现象。缪波系统参与神经镜像。我们的身体静止时，它处于活跃状态；每当我们做出主动动作，如参加体育活动或切换电视频道时，它便会消失。令人意外的是，当我们看到别人活动时，例如在电视上观看，缪波信号也会沉寂。这些结果正是镜像神经元作用所致。

ERizzolatti, Fogassi and Gallese call the idea of mirror neurons the ‘direct matching hypothesis’. They believe that we only understand the movement of sports stars when we ‘map the visual representation of the observed action onto our motor representation of the same action’. According to this theory, watching an Olympic athlete ‘causes the motor system of the observer to resonate. The ‘motor knowledge’ of the observer is used to understand the observed action.’ But mirror neurons are more than just the neural basis for our attitude to sport. It turns out that watching a great golfer makes us better golfers, and watching a great sprinter actually makes us run faster. This ability to learn by watching is a crucial skill. From the acquisition of language as infants to learning facial expressions, mimesis (copying) is an essential part of being conscious. The best athletes are those with a premotor cortex capable of imagining the movements of victory, together with the physical properties to make those movements real.

Rizzolatti、Fogassi 和 Gallese 把镜像神经元这一观点称为“直接匹配假说”（direct matching hypothesis）。他们认为，只有当我们“把观察到的动作的视觉表征映射到自身对同一动作的运动表征上”时，才能理解体育明星的动作。按照这一理论，观看奥运选手会“使观察者的运动系统产生共振。观察者会运用自身的‘运动知识’来理解所观察到的动作”。不过，镜像神经元的作用并不只是构成我们对体育运动所持态度的神经基础。事实证明，观看优秀的高尔夫球手会让我们的球技提高，观看优秀的短跑运动员甚至真的会让我们跑得更快。这种通过观察来学习的能力至关重要。从婴儿期习得语言到学习面部表情，模仿（mimesis）是意识活动不可或缺的一部分。最优秀的运动员，不仅具备能想象胜利动作的前运动皮层，也拥有将这些动作变为现实所需的身体条件。

FBut how many of us regularly watch sports in order to be a better athlete? Rather, we watch sport for the feeling, the human drama. This feeling also derives from mirror neurons. By letting spectators share in the motions of victory, they also allow us to share in its feelings. This is because they are directly connected to the amygdala, one of the main brain regions involved in emotion. During the Olympics, the mirror neurons of whole nations will be electrically identical, their athletes causing spectators to feel, just for a second or two, the same thing. Watching sports brings people together. Most of us will never run a mile in under four minutes, or hit a home run. Our consolation comes in watching. When we gather around the TV, we all feel, just for a moment, what it is to do something perfectly.

但我们之中有多少人经常观看体育比赛，是为了成为更优秀的运动员呢？其实，我们观看体育比赛，是为了那份感受，为了其中富有戏剧性的人与事。这种感受同样源自镜像神经元。镜像神经元让观众共同体验胜利的动作，也让我们分享胜利的情感。这是因为它们与杏仁核（amygdala）直接相连，而杏仁核是参与情绪活动的主要脑区之一。奥运会期间，整个国家的人，其镜像神经元在电活动上都会变得相同；他们的运动员会让观众在短短一两秒内产生同样的感受。观看体育比赛把人们凝聚在一起。我们大多数人永远无法在四分钟内跑完一英里，也无法击出本垒打。观看比赛给了我们慰藉。当大家围坐在电视机旁时，我们都会在片刻之间，体会到把一件事做到尽善尽美是什么感觉。
