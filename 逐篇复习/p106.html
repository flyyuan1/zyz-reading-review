Could the writings of 18th-century sailors unlock the complexities of today’s climate changes?

18世纪水手的文字记录，能否帮助我们解开当今气候变化的复杂问题？

Are the climatic changes the world is seeing now really unprecedented, or have there been other times when change was just as swift? ‘If we go back to the past and find there really hasn’t been anything like this before, then that strengthens the case that human activity is responsible for the rapid changes we are seeing today,’ says climatologist Dennis Wheeler of the University of Sunderland, UK.

世界如今正在经历的气候变化真的前所未有，还是过去也有过变化同样迅速的时期？英国桑德兰大学（University of Sunderland）气候学家 Dennis Wheeler 说：“如果我们回顾过去，发现此前确实从未发生过这样的情况，就更能支持这样一种观点：人类活动要为我们今天看到的快速变化负责。”

But the past is tricky territory for climatologists. While tree rings and ice cores chart annual changes in climate going back millennia, they cannot show what happened on a month-to-month basis. There are some long-running chronicles of temperature and air pressure over land dating back to the 17th century, but systematic records of air movements over the oceans — which govern most of our weather — were almost non-existent before the mid-19th century, by which time the Industrial Revolution was already well under way, and factory chimneys were pouring carbon dioxide into the atmosphere. In order to establish whether today’s climate changes indeed had any precedent in the past, what Wheeler needed was detailed information from earlier times, and he soon became convinced that the multitude of jottings in old ships’ records could provide just that.

不过，对气候学家来说，过去是一个难以研究的领域。树木年轮和冰芯虽然能够记录数千年来气候逐年的变化，却无法显示逐月发生的情况。从17世纪开始，陆地上确有一些长期连续的温度和气压记录；但海洋上空的气流支配着我们的大部分天气，而在19世纪中叶以前，相关系统记录几乎不存在。到那时，工业革命早已全面展开，工厂烟囱正把二氧化碳排入大气。要判断今天的气候变化在过去是否确有先例，Wheeler 需要早期年代的详细资料；他很快确信，旧航海记录中大量的随手记载恰好能提供这些信息。

By the late 17th century, the seafaring nations of Europe had fleets of ships criss-crossing the seas, all keeping meticulous accounts of their voyages, including regular observations of weather conditions. In 1731, it became obligatory for British naval vessels to keep a detailed logbook, and by 1750 the practice was universal. A vast number of such records survive to this day. Despite initial reluctance among climatologists to use these historical documents for scientific research, Wheeler and his team secured funding for a project to sift through two thousand old logbooks. They pored over records of English, Dutch, French and Spanish fleets, with the aim of creating a climatological database for the world’s oceans between 1750 and 1850.

到17世纪末，欧洲各航海国家的船队往来穿梭于海上，所有船只都细致记录航程，其中包括定期观察天气状况。1731年，英国海军舰船被规定必须保存详细航海日志；到1750年，这一做法已全面实行。大量此类记录一直保存至今。气候学家起初不愿把这些历史文献用于科学研究；尽管如此，Wheeler 及其团队仍获得了一个项目的资助，筛查两千本旧航海日志。他们仔细查阅英国、荷兰、法国和西班牙船队的记录，目标是建立一个涵盖1750至1850年世界海洋情况的气候数据库。

In order to reconstruct air pressure fields as they move across the sea, climate ‘modellers’ need details of wind strength and direction at as many locations as possible. The team’s research produced the equivalent of two million days of information but, plentiful as this data was, much of it needed translation. Until 1805, even a day wasn’t a day as we know it, but a nautical day, which started at noon. Before 1752, the year began on 25 March, and until September of that year the English calendar lagged 10 days behind the one used in the rest of Europe.

气候“建模人员”要重建气压场在海面上移动的情况，就需要尽可能多地点的风力和风向详情。团队的研究所得资料相当于两百万天的记录；数据虽然丰富，其中许多却需要转换。在1805年以前，就连“一天”也不是我们今天所理解的一天，而是从中午开始的航海日。1752年以前，一年从3月25日开始；直到该年9月，英国历法仍比欧洲其他地区使用的历法落后10天。

Even the wind’s direction posed a problem. It took a long time to work out if the direction noted in the log was based on magnetic north or true north. Get it wrong and the data could be out by as much as 30 degrees. ‘On British vessels, most observations were magnetic. Once a day, officers would convert the readings to true north — but the logbooks don’t tell you that,’ says Wheeler. ‘It took some detective work to find out what was going on.’

就连风向也会造成问题。研究人员花了很长时间，才弄清日志中记载的方向是以磁北还是真北为基准。基准一旦弄错，数据偏差可能高达30度。“英国船只上的大多数观测都以磁北为准。军官每天会把读数换算成真北一次，但航海日志没有告诉你这一点，”Wheeler 说。“我们做了一番侦探般的调查，才弄明白实际情况。”

Harder still was interpreting observations on the strength of the wind. It was 1850 before most ships were equipped with accurate instruments to collect climatic data, previous to which officers had to rely on judgement and experience. And, as the numerical Beaufort scale of wind strength did not become official until 1838, most of the logbooks in Wheeler’s archives describe weather conditions using words alone — and often archaic or peculiar ones. A sample of English logs from 1685 to 1700 produced 73 different ways of describing the wind’s force. To everyone’s relief, closer scrutiny showed that many terms were used just once or twice: most log-keepers stuck to a similar set of terms.

更困难的是解读风力观测记录。直到1850年，大多数船只才配备准确收集气候数据的仪器；此前，军官只能依靠判断和经验。数值化的蒲福风级（Beaufort scale）到1838年才成为正式标准，因此 Wheeler 档案中的大部分航海日志只用文字描述天气状况，而且经常使用古旧或奇特的词语。从1685至1700年的英国日志中抽取一批样本，便出现了73种描述风力的不同方式。令所有人松一口气的是，进一步仔细研究显示，许多说法只使用过一两次；大多数日志记录者采用的词汇体系较为相似。

So how strong was a wind described as ‘moderate’, or a gale described as ‘fine’? And did all seafarers have the same thing in mind when they used them? This was tested by checking how far the ships sailed each day, another fact carefully recorded in the logbook. The speed a sailing ship travelled was directly linked to the force of the wind, at least up to Beaufort force 7. ‘If you have a large enough sample you can pick out all the days when officers described, for example, a “fine breeze”, and check the sea-miles covered,’ says Wheeler. ‘That gives a measure of the relative wind strength.’

那么，用“适中”（moderate）形容的风究竟有多强，用“良好”（fine）形容的大风又有多大？不同水手使用这些词时，心里所指的情况都一样吗？研究人员通过核查船只每天航行多远来检验这一点；航行距离也是日志中认真记录的事实。至少在蒲福风级7级以内，帆船的航速与风力直接相关。Wheeler 说：“如果样本足够大，例如，可以找出军官把风描述为‘好风’（fine breeze）的所有日子，再核对当日航行的海里数。这样就能衡量相对风力。”

Having to deal with logs from four nations, from both naval and merchant vessels, made the task of translating archaic terms into hard numbers doubly daunting. Toughest of all was unravelling the terms used aboard trading ships. These had their own complex vocabulary of gales, trade winds, and monsoons, each in several grades, Wheeler discovered. But, by checking sea-miles covered, and cross-checking with the logs of occasional naval escorts, the team eventually worked out which terms meant what. Pairs of ships in convoy, or squadrons in the same locality, should have the same records, so allowing the researchers to check for consistency.

研究人员必须处理四个国家海军舰船和商船的日志，把古旧词语转化为确切数字的任务因此加倍艰巨。最困难的是弄清商船上使用的术语的含义。Wheeler 发现，商船有自己一套复杂词汇，用来描述大风、信风和季风，而且每种风又分为若干等级。不过，团队核对航行海里数，并与偶尔护航的海军舰船日志交叉核查，最终弄清了各个术语的含义。结队航行的两艘船，或同处一地的分舰队，应该留下相同记录，因此研究人员可以检验记录是否一致。

The wealth of data Wheeler was able to call on enabled him to show statistically that the expressions used in the logbooks were not vague approximations, but precise terms falling into distinct categories. The researchers have now fitted nearly all the terms used into the Beaufort scale, and have high hopes that the seafarers of the pre-instrumental age will, in time, give today’s scientists a valuable insight into the causes of global climate change.

Wheeler 能够调用的丰富数据，使他得以从统计上证明，航海日志所用表达并非模糊的近似描述，而是分别归入明确类别的精确术语。如今，研究人员已把几乎所有术语纳入蒲福风级，并十分期待仪器出现以前的水手最终能让今天的科学家深入了解全球气候变化的成因。
