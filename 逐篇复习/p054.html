Talbot Park, a housing project in Auckland, New Zealand, was once described as a ghetto, troubled by high rates of crime and vandalism. However, it has just been rebuilt at a cost of $48 m and the project reflects some new thinking about urban design.

塔尔博特公园是新西兰奥克兰（Auckland）的一处住房项目，过去曾被形容为贫民区，犯罪和蓄意破坏现象十分严重。不过，这里刚刚耗资4800万完成重建，项目体现了城市设计方面的一些新理念。

AThe new Talbot Park is immediately eye-catching because the buildings look quite different to other state-housing projects in Auckland. ‘There is no reason why state housing should look cheap in my view,’ says architect Neil Cotton, one of the design team. ‘In fact, I was anticipating a backlash by those who objected to the quality of what is provided with government money.’ The tidy brick and wood apartments and townhouses would not look out of place in some of the city’s most affluent suburbs, and this is a central theme of the Talbot Park philosophy.

新的塔尔博特公园格外引人注目，因为这里的建筑看起来与奥克兰其他公营住房项目很不一样。设计团队成员、建筑师 Neil Cotton 说：“在我看来，公营住房没有理由显得廉价。事实上，我当时就预料，政府花钱把住房建得这么好，会招来强烈反对。”这些整洁的砖木公寓和联排住宅即使放在奥克兰一些最富裕的郊区也毫不突兀，而这正是塔尔博特公园理念的核心。

BTalbot Park is a triangle of government-owned land, which in the early 1960s was developed for state housing built around a linear garden that ran through the middle. Initially, there was a strong sense of neighbourliness. Former residents recall how the garden played a big part in their childhoods — a place where kids came together to play softball, cricket and bullrush. ‘We had respect for our neighbours and addressed them by title — Mr and Mrs so-and-so,’ recalls Georgie Thompson, who grew up there in the 1960s.

塔尔博特公园是一块三角形的政府用地。20世纪60年代初，这里被开发为公营住宅区，住房环绕着一条贯穿中央的狭长花园而建。最初，居民之间有很强的邻里情谊。昔日居民回忆，这座花园在他们童年时发挥了重要作用，孩子们会聚在那里玩垒球、板球和冲阵游戏（bullrush）。20世纪60年代在此长大的 Georgie Thompson 回忆道：“我们尊重邻居，会用称谓叫他们，比如某某先生、某某太太。”

CExactly what went wrong with Talbot Park is unclear. The community began to change in the late 1970s as more immigrants moved in. The new arrivals didn’t always integrate with the community and a ‘them and us’ mentality developed. In the process, standards dropped and the neighbourhood began to look shabbier. The buildings themselves were also deteriorating and becoming run-down, petty crime was on the rise and the garden was considered unsafe. In 2002, Housing New Zealand decided the properties needed upgrading. The question was, how to avoid repeating the mistakes of the past?

塔尔博特公园究竟出了什么问题，并不清楚。20世纪70年代末，随着更多移民迁入，社区开始发生变化。新来者并不总能融入社区，居民之间逐渐形成“他们和我们”的对立心态。在这一过程中，整体水准下降，社区也开始显得越来越破旧。建筑本身同样不断老化、失修；轻微犯罪日益增多，花园也被认为不再安全。2002年，新西兰住房局（Housing New Zealand）决定必须升级这些房产。问题在于，怎样才能避免重蹈覆辙？

DOne controversial aspect of the upgrade is that the new development has actually made the density of housing in Talbot Park greater, putting 52 more homes on the same site. Doing this required a fresh approach that can be summed up as ‘mix and match’. The first priority was to mix up the housing by employing a variety of plans by different architects: some of the accommodation is free-standing houses, some semi-detached, some low-level, multi-apartment blocks. By doing this, the development avoids the uniform appearance of so many state-housing projects, which residents complain denies them any sense of individual identity. The next goal was to prevent overspending by using efficient designs to maximise the sense of space from minimum room sizes. There was also a no-frills, industrial approach to kitchens, bathrooms and flooring, to optimise durability and ensure the project did not go over budget. Architecturally, the buildings are relatively conservative: fairly plain houses standing in a small garden. There’s a slight reflection of the traditional Pacific beach house (a fale) but it’s not over-played. ‘It seems to us that low-cost housing is about getting as much amenity as you can for the money,’ says architect Michael Thompson. Another key aspect of the ‘mix and match’ approach is openness: one that not only lets residents see what is going on but also lets them know they are seen. The plan ensures there are no cul-de-sacs or properties hidden from view, that the gardens are not enclosed by trees and that most boundary fences are see-through — a community contained but without walls.

此次改造中一个有争议之处，是新项目实际上提高了塔尔博特公园的住房密度，在同一块土地上又增加了52套住宅。要做到这一点，就需要一种可以概括为“混合搭配”的新方法。首要任务是采用不同建筑师设计的多种方案，把各种住房混合起来：有独立式住宅，有半独立式住宅，也有低层多户公寓楼。这样一来，项目便避免了许多公营住宅区千篇一律的外观；居民抱怨，这种统一外观会让他们失去任何个体身份感。下一个目标是避免超支，为此，设计力求高效，以最小的房间面积营造最大的空间感。厨房、浴室和地板也采用简朴实用的工业化方案，以尽可能提高耐用性，并确保项目不超出预算。从建筑风格看，这些建筑相对保守：房屋相当朴素，坐落在小花园中。设计稍稍借鉴了传统太平洋海滨住宅（fale），但没有过分强调。建筑师 Michael Thompson 说：“在我们看来，低成本住房就是要用有限的资金获得尽可能多的便利。”这种“混合搭配”方法的另一个关键是开放性：既让居民能看见周围发生的事情，也让他们知道自己同样会被看见。规划确保这里没有死胡同，没有住宅藏在视线之外；花园不被树木围住，而且能透过大多数边界围栏看见另一侧——这是一个有边界却没有围墙的社区。

EThe population today is cosmopolitan: 50% Pacific Islanders, 20% Māori, 15% Asian, 10% New Zealand European and the rest composed of immigrants from Russia, Ukraine and Iran. ‘It was important that the buildings were sufficiently flexible to cater for the needs of people from a wide variety of cultural backgrounds,’ explains designer James Lundy.

如今这里的人口构成十分多元：太平洋岛民占50%，毛利人（Māori）占20%，亚洲人占15%，新西兰欧裔占10%，其余居民是来自俄罗斯、乌克兰和伊朗的移民。设计师 James Lundy 解释说：“建筑必须足够灵活，能够满足来自各种文化背景人群的需要，这一点很重要。”

FDespite the quality of the buildings, however, there should be no doubt that Talbot Park and its surrounding suburb of Tāmaki are low socio-economic areas. Of the 5,000 houses there, 55% are state houses, 28% privately owned (compared to about 65% nationally) and 17% private rental. The area has a high density of households with incomes in the $5,000 to $15,000 range and very few with an income over $70,000. That’s in sharp contrast to the more affluent suburbs in Auckland.

然而，尽管建筑质量很高，塔尔博特公园及其周边的塔马基郊区（Tāmaki）无疑仍属于社会经济水平较低的地区。当地5000套住房中，55%是公营住房，28%为私人所有，而全国这一比例约为65%，另有17%属于私人出租房。当地收入在 $5,000 至 $15,000 之间的家庭比例很高，收入超过 $70,000 的家庭则很少。这与奥克兰较富裕的郊区形成鲜明对比。

GAnother important part of the new development is what Housing New Zealand calls ‘intensive tenancy management’. Opponents of the project call it social control. ‘The focus is on frequent inspections and setting clear guidelines and boundaries regarding the sort of behaviour we expect from tenants,’ says Graham Bodman, Housing New Zealand’s regional manager. The result is a code of sometimes strict rules: no loud parties after 10 pm; no washing hung over balcony rails; and a requirement to mow lawns and keep the property tidy. The Tenancy Manager walks the site every day, knows everyone by name and deals with problems quickly. ‘It’s all based on the intensification,’ says project manager Stuart Bracey. ‘We acknowledge that if you are going to ask people to live in these quite tightly-packed communities, you have to actually help them to get to know each other by organising morning teas and street barbecues.’ So far it seems to be working and many involved in the project believe Talbot Park represents the way forward for state housing.

新项目的另一个重要部分，是新西兰住房局所谓的“强化租务管理”（intensive tenancy management）。反对项目的人则称之为社会控制。新西兰住房局区域经理 Graham Bodman 说：“重点是频繁检查，并就我们期望租户遵守的行为设定明确准则和界限。”由此形成了一套有时相当严格的规则：晚上10点后不得举办喧闹聚会；不得把洗好的衣物晾在阳台栏杆上；必须修剪草坪并保持房产整洁。租务经理每天都会巡视整个住宅区，知道每个人的名字，并迅速处理问题。项目经理 Stuart Bracey 说：“这一切都建立在提高住房密度之上。我们承认，如果要让人们住在这样相当密集的社区里，就必须通过组织上午茶和街头烧烤等活动，真正帮助他们相互认识。”到目前为止，这种做法似乎有效；许多参与项目的人都认为，塔尔博特公园代表了公营住房未来的发展方向。
