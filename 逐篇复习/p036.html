Wool is part of Britain’s history and heritage, more so than any other commodity ever produced in that country. It was made into cloth there in the Bronze Age, which began about 1900 BC. By the time the Romans invaded in 55 BC the Britons had developed a wool industry, and this was encouraged by their new masters. Roman emperors appreciated the fineness of British woollen cloth. Although Saxon invasions in the fifth century nearly destroyed the industry, it is known that in the eighth century Britain was exporting woollen fabrics to continental Europe, and after the arrival of the Norman conquerors in 1066 the industry expanded. By the twelfth century, wool was becoming England’s greatest national asset. Cloth making was widespread, particularly in the large towns of southern and eastern England, nearest to France. But the greatest wealth came from exports of raw wool.

在英国生产过的各种商品中，羊毛与这个国家的历史和传统联系最为紧密。大约始于公元前1900年的青铜时代，英国人就已用羊毛织布。到罗马人于公元前55年入侵时，不列颠人已经发展出羊毛产业，新统治者又推动了这个行业。罗马皇帝十分欣赏英国毛织品的细腻。尽管5世纪撒克逊人的入侵几乎摧毁了这一产业，但可以确定的是，8世纪时英国已向欧洲大陆出口毛织品；1066年诺曼征服者到来后，该产业进一步扩张。到12世纪，羊毛逐渐成为英格兰最重要的国家资产。制布业分布广泛，在最靠近法国的英格兰南部和东部大城镇尤其兴盛。不过，最大的财富来自未经加工的羊毛出口。

Kings and their ministers welcomed the revenue that resulted from exports and export taxes – and also the power it gave to the king, who could grant or withdraw permits for the wool towns and for the industry. Trade associations, known as ‘guilds’, were founded to guarantee good work by experienced weavers (people who produce cloth from woollen threads), and were powerful for hundreds of years. The peak of cloth production was reached in the thirteenth century. Then the wool trade declined for a long period because of political conflict.

国王及其大臣乐于看到出口和出口税带来的收入，也看重这项贸易赋予国王的权力：国王可以向羊毛城镇和整个行业颁发或撤销许可证。人们成立了称为“行会”（guilds）的行业协会，以保证熟练织工（用羊毛线织布的人）的工作质量；这些行会在数百年间都很有权势。毛织品产量在13世纪达到顶峰。随后，羊毛贸易因政治冲突而长期衰退。

In 1331, King Edward III encouraged master weavers from Flanders (an area of present-day Belgium) to settle in England. These Flemish weavers and their descendants were to play a part in the final development of English cloth. The export trade in raw wool recovered and the first half of the fourteenth century was a time of prosperity for English wool farmers. But it was overshadowed by a long war with France (export taxes on wool were one of the principal means of financing the war) and by bubonic plague (the Black Death), which in 1349 caused devastation: in many villages as much as three-quarters of the population died. This led to an increase of the sheep flocks, for there were not enough people left to cultivate the land for arable crops.

1331年，Edward III 国王鼓励来自佛兰德斯（Flanders）——即今天比利时的一部分——的熟练织工定居英格兰。这些佛兰德斯织工及其后代，在英格兰毛织业后来的发展中发挥了作用。未经加工的羊毛出口贸易恢复，14世纪上半叶成为英格兰养羊人的繁荣时期。然而，长期对法战争——羊毛出口税是战争经费的主要来源之一——以及腺鼠疫，也就是黑死病，给这段繁荣蒙上阴影。1349年的黑死病造成严重破坏，许多村庄多达四分之三的人口死亡。这使羊群数量增加，因为剩下的人已不足以耕种土地、种植农作物。

Despite setbacks, raw wool exporting expanded, and so also did manufacturing of wool fabrics. This was becoming both specialised and localised. The area of England known as the West Country had three advantages – extensive sheep pastures, a supply of soft water for washing, scouring and dyeing wool, and water-power to drive machinery. Similarly, the hills of Yorkshire and Lancashire in the north of England had soft water and fast running streams. Water from the latter could be used to drive mills for ‘fulling’, a shrinking process which makes the fabric firmer and its surface more compact.

尽管遭遇挫折，未经加工的羊毛出口仍在扩大，毛织品制造业也随之发展。这个行业逐渐专业化，并集中到特定地区。英格兰西南部（West Country）拥有三项优势：广阔的牧羊场、可用于洗涤、去脂和染色羊毛的软水，以及驱动机器的水力。英格兰北部约克郡（Yorkshire）和兰开夏郡（Lancashire）的丘陵同样有软水和湍急溪流。溪水可以驱动缩绒用的水磨；缩绒是一种收缩工艺，能使织物更加结实、表面更加紧密。

In East Anglia there was soft water, but no hills or fast-running streams to provide power for fulling mills. Instead, East Anglia used the long, fine wool from its native sheep breeds to produce a cloth which did not require the fulling process. This was the type of cloth which is now called ‘worsted’, after the village of Worstead. For four hundred years East Anglia dominated the worsted trade, with skills inherited from the Flemish settlers of 1331.

东盎格利亚（East Anglia）有软水，却没有山丘或湍急溪流为缩绒水磨提供动力。因此，当地使用本土绵羊所产的细长羊毛，织出一种不需要缩绒的布料。这类布料如今称为“精纺毛料”（worsted），名称来自 Worstead 村。凭借从1331年到来的佛兰德斯移民那里继承的技艺，东盎格利亚在精纺毛料贸易中占据主导地位长达400年。

English cloth quickly achieved an international reputation. From being primarily a raw wool exporter, the country became in the fourteenth and fifteenth centuries a manufacturer and exporter of cloth. At the end of the fifteenth century, it was said that England was largely a nation of sheep farmers and cloth manufacturers. The next two centuries saw continued expansion of the industry despite conflicts at home and abroad. In the sixteenth century, French weavers, persecuted for their Protestant religion, sought refuge in England and took their skills with them. England began to surpass Flanders in woollen manufacture; by the end of the seventeenth century it comprised two-thirds of the value of its exports. Radical changes lay ahead, in the geographical location of the industry, in labour use and in manufacturing processes. By 1770, output of worsted from Yorkshire equalled that of East Anglia, and its cloth manufacturing district began to take shape with the expansion of major towns: Leeds, Bradford, Halifax, Huddersfield, and Wakefield.

英格兰毛织品很快赢得国际声誉。这个国家原本主要出口未经加工的羊毛，到14和15世纪转变为毛织品制造国和出口国。15世纪末，据说英格兰大体上成了一个由养羊人和毛织品制造者组成的国家。此后两个世纪，尽管国内外冲突不断，该产业仍持续扩张。16世纪，因信奉新教而遭受迫害的法国织工前往英格兰避难，也带来了自己的技艺。英格兰的毛织品制造开始超过佛兰德斯；到17世纪末，毛织品占英格兰出口总值的三分之二。这个行业的地理分布、劳动力使用和制造工艺都将迎来根本变化。到1770年，约克郡的精纺毛料产量已与东盎格利亚相当；随着利兹（Leeds）、布拉德福德（Bradford）、哈利法克斯（Halifax）、哈德斯菲尔德（Huddersfield）和韦克菲尔德（Wakefield）等主要城镇扩张，当地的毛织品制造区开始成形。

The Industrial Revolution of 1750–1850 also brought change. It led the way for new inventions stemming from the Lancashire cotton industry, to mechanize and speed dramatically the processes of spinning and weaving. Manufacturing methods, unchanged since the revival of the trade in the fourteenth century, were now superseded. Mechanization had been opposed in the past and it was again. The widespread unrest of 1812 led to the destruction of equipment by bands of rioters, who feared they would lose employment. But machinery won the day.

1750至1850年的工业革命也带来变化。工业革命为源自兰开夏棉纺业的新发明铺平了道路，使纺纱和织造实现机械化，生产速度也大幅提高。自14世纪行业复兴以来一直未变的制造方法，如今被新方法取代。过去机械化曾遭到反对，这次也不例外。1812年，大范围的动荡导致成群的暴动者捣毁设备，因为他们担心失去工作。但最终还是机器占了上风。

Over the course of the nineteenth century, the older industries in areas such as East Anglia, where opposition had been most bitter, permanently declined. They were overtaken by Yorkshire, where machinery was more readily accepted. The younger industry jumped ahead and never lost its lead, supported by abundant supplies of inexpensive coal to generate steam and, later, electrical power. Other specialised types of manufacturing developed in Scotland, famed for its tweeds (a range of coloured woollen cloth with characteristic designs), and in the West Country, which focused on the production of high-quality, woven carpets.

整个19世纪，东盎格利亚等反对最激烈地区的旧产业永久衰退，并被更愿意接受机器的约克郡赶超。这个起步较晚的产业迅速取得领先，此后再也没有失去优势；当地供应充足、价格低廉的煤炭，可用来产生蒸汽动力，后来又用于发电，为它提供了支持。其他专业化制造业则在苏格兰和英格兰西南部发展起来。苏格兰以花呢（tweeds）闻名；花呢是一类带有独特图案的彩色毛织品。英格兰西南部则专门生产高品质的机织地毯。
