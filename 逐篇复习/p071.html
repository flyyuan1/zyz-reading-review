AWhen you read do you also listen to music? Do you talk to friends or colleagues at the office? As you are reading online, are you also emailing or instant-messaging? Since the 1990s, virtually all of us have spent part of our day multitasking – that is, either switching from one task to another or juggling two or more things at the same time. Although doing many things at once can be a way of making tasks fun and energizing, according to author Thomas Lehman, ‘You sacrifice focus when you do this. Multitasking is shifting focus from one task to another in rapid succession. It gives the illusion that you’re simultaneously tasking, but you’re not really. You can only shift back and forth. It’s like playing tennis with three balls.’ Of course, it depends on what you’re doing. For some people, listening to music while working actually makes them more imaginative because they are using their brains for different cognitive functions. But we’re all familiar with what Lehman calls ‘email voice’, when someone you’re talking to on the phone suddenly appears not to be fully engaged with the conversation.

阅读时，你也会听音乐吗？在办公室里，你会和朋友或同事说话吗？在线阅读的同时，你还会收发电子邮件或即时消息吗？自20世纪90年代以来，我们几乎每个人每天都会花一部分时间处理多项任务，也就是在不同任务之间切换，或同时应付两件乃至更多事情。作家 Thomas Lehman 指出，虽然同时做许多事情能让任务变得有趣，使人更有干劲，但“这样做会牺牲专注力。所谓多任务处理，是在不同任务之间快速转移注意力。它让你以为自己在同时做几件事，其实并非如此。你只能来回切换，就像同时用三个球打网球一样。”当然，这也取决于你在做什么。对有些人来说，工作时听音乐确实能增强想象力，因为大脑动用的是不同的认知功能。不过，我们都熟悉 Lehman 所说的“邮件腔”（email voice）——与你通电话的人突然显得没有全心投入谈话。

BWe all know that computers and their spin-offs, the smartphone and cellphone, have created a very different world from several decades ago, when a desk worker had a typewriter, a phone and an occasional colleague who dropped into the office. Think even of the home in the days before the cordless phone. Those old enough can remember when talking on the land line meant sitting down, putting your feet up and chatting – not doing laundry, cooking dinner, sweeping the floor or answering the door as well.

我们都知道，电脑及其衍生产品——智能手机和移动电话——创造了一个与几十年前截然不同的世界。当时，办公室职员手边只有一台打字机、一部电话，以及偶尔前来串门的同事。再想想无绳电话出现之前的家庭生活。年纪稍长的人还记得，那时用固定电话交谈，就意味着坐下来、跷起脚聊天，而不是一边洗衣、做饭、扫地或应门，一边通话。

CBut today, as we feel required to do more and more things in a shorter period of time, researchers are trying to figure out how the brain changes attention from one subject to another. Earl Miller, professor of neuroscience at the Massachusetts Institute of Technology, explains that humans have a very large prefrontal cortex, which is the part of the brain that helps us switch and prioritize tasks. In humans, he says, this area is about one-third of the entire cortex, while in dogs and cats, it is four or five percent and in monkeys about 15 percent. ‘With the growth of the prefrontal cortex, animals become more flexible in their behavior,’ according to Miller. We can do a couple of things at the same time if they are routine, but once they demand more cognitive processing, even the human brain has ‘a severe bottleneck’. Miller conducted studies where electrodes were attached to the heads of people to monitor them while performing different tasks. He found that ‘when there’re lots of visual stimulants out there in front of you, only one or two objects tend to activate your neurons, indicating that you’re really only focusing on one or two items at a time.’

然而今天，我们觉得自己必须在更短时间里做越来越多的事，研究人员则在设法弄清大脑如何把注意力从一个对象转向另一个对象。麻省理工学院（Massachusetts Institute of Technology）神经科学教授 Earl Miller 解释说，人类的前额叶皮层（prefrontal cortex）很大，正是大脑的这一部分帮助我们切换任务并确定任务的优先顺序。他说，这一区域在人类大脑中约占整个皮层的三分之一，而在猫和狗的大脑中只占4%或5%，在猴子的大脑中约占15%。Miller 表示：“随着前额叶皮层增大，动物的行为会变得更加灵活。”如果事情已成习惯，我们可以同时做两件事；但一旦任务需要更多认知加工，即使人脑也会遇到“严重瓶颈”。Miller 开展过一些研究，让受试者头戴电极执行不同任务，以便监测他们。他发现：“当眼前有许多视觉刺激时，往往只有一两个物体会激活你的神经元，这表明你实际上一次只专注于一两个对象。”

DDavid Meyer, professor of psychology at the University of Michigan, looked at young adults as they performed tasks that involved doing math or classifying geometrical objects. The study found that for all task types, the participants lost time when they had to move from one undertaking to another, and that it took significantly longer to complete what they were doing when switching between more complicated tasks. If we’re talking about doing two jobs requiring real concentration, like text-messaging and driving, this lost time, even a fraction of a second, can be fatal. The RAC, a British non-profit organization that focuses on driving issues, asked drivers to use a driving simulator to see how texting affected their driving. In fact, the participants’ reaction time was increased by 35 percent when writing a text message.

密歇根大学（University of Michigan）心理学教授 David Meyer 观察了年轻人做数学题或给几何图形分类时的表现。研究发现，无论任务属于哪种类型，参与者从一项任务转到另一项任务时都会损失时间；而在较复杂的任务之间切换时，完成手头工作所需的时间会显著增加。如果要同时做两件确实需要集中注意力的事，比如一边发短信一边开车，那么哪怕只损失几分之一秒，也可能致命。专注于驾驶问题的英国非营利组织 RAC 请司机使用驾驶模拟器，以了解发短信会怎样影响驾驶。事实表明，参与者写短信时，反应时间延长了35%。

EAlthough we may be advised to focus on one thing at a time, the real world doesn’t work that way. We are constantly interrupted. Gloria Mark, professor of informatics at the University of California, has studied this using people in offices as subjects. Mark found that the people were interrupted and moved from one project or task to another about every 11 minutes. According to Mark, ‘People actually performed faster in conditions where they were interrupted, but they were less efficient.’ Mark also found that people were as likely to self-interrupt as to be interrupted by someone else. As the researchers watched, every 12 minutes or so, for no apparent reason, a person working on a document would turn and call someone or write an email. After only 20 minutes of uninterrupted work, people reported significantly higher stress, frustration and pressure. Mark says that further research needs to be done to explain these work patterns, but points out that ‘our increasingly shorter attention spans probably have something to do with it.’

虽然我们可能会被建议一次只专注于一件事，现实世界却并非如此。我们总是受到打扰。加州大学（University of California）信息学教授 Gloria Mark 以办公室工作人员为研究对象，对此进行了研究。Mark 发现，人们大约每11分钟就会受到打扰，并从一个项目或任务转向另一个。Mark 说：“人们在受到打扰的情况下其实完成得更快，但效率更低。”Mark 还发现，人们主动打断自己的可能性，与被别人打断的可能性同样大。研究人员观察到，大约每隔12分钟，一个正在处理文件的人就会毫无明显缘由地转身给别人打电话或写电子邮件。仅仅连续工作20分钟没有受到打扰之后，人们报告的压力、挫败感和紧迫感就显著升高。Mark 表示，还需进一步研究才能解释这些工作模式，但她指出，“我们的注意力持续时间越来越短，很可能与此有关”。

FPsychiatrist Edward Hallowell has termed the effects of multitasking ‘attention deficit trait’, which he attributes entirely to the environment. ‘Our minds fill with noise – brain activity with little significance – and the brain gradually loses its capacity to attend fully to anything.’ Desperately trying to keep up with a multitude of jobs, we ‘feel a constant low level of panic and guilt.’ But Hallowell says that despite our belief that we cannot control this overload, we can. ‘We need to create boundaries,’ he says. That means devices like cellphones being banned at meetings. It means training yourself not to look at your computer every 20 seconds.

精神科医生 Edward Hallowell 把多任务处理造成的影响称为“注意缺失特质”（attention deficit trait），并认为它完全由环境所致。“我们的头脑充满噪声，也就是无甚意义的大脑活动，大脑于是逐渐失去全神贯注于任何事情的能力。”我们拼命设法跟上众多任务，因而“始终感到轻微的恐慌和内疚”。不过 Hallowell 说，虽然我们以为自己无法控制这种过载状态，其实是可以控制的。他说：“我们需要划定界限。”这意味着开会时禁止使用手机等设备，也意味着训练自己不要每20秒就看一次电脑。
