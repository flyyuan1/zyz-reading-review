What led to the disappearance of the giant mammals? Kate Wong examines the theories

是什么导致这些巨型哺乳动物消失？Kate Wong 审视了各种理论

Although it’s hard to imagine in this age of urban sprawl and automobiles, North America once belonged to huge, elephant-like mammoths, camels, bear-sized beavers and other giant beasts, collectively known as ‘megafauna’. Some 11,000 years ago, however, these large-bodied mammals—about 70 species in all—disappeared. Their demise coincided roughly with the arrival of humans on the continent and dramatic climate change—factors that have inspired several theories about the die-off. Yet despite decades of scientific investigation, the exact cause remains a mystery. Now new findings offer support to one of these controversial hypotheses: that human hunting drove these huge megafauna species to extinction.

在这个城市不断扩张、汽车随处可见的时代，很难想象北美曾经属于体型庞大、形似大象的猛犸象、骆驼、熊一般大的河狸和其他巨兽；它们统称为“巨型动物”（megafauna）。然而，大约11,000年前，这些大型哺乳动物消失了，总计约70个物种。它们的灭绝与人类抵达北美大陆及剧烈的气候变化大致同时发生，这些因素催生了几种解释这场物种大灭绝的理论。然而，科学界调查了数十年，确切原因仍是谜。如今，新发现为其中一项颇具争议的假说提供了支持：人类狩猎导致这些巨型动物灭绝。

This belief resulted in the overkill model which emerged in the 1960s, when it was put forth by Paul S. Martin of the University of Arizona. Since then, critics have charged that no archaeological remains exist to support the idea that the first Americans hunted to the extent necessary to cause these extinctions, but at the annual meeting of the Society of Vertebrate Paleontology in Mexico City in October 1999, specialist John Alroy of the University of California at Santa Barbara argued that, in fact, hunting-driven extinction is not only plausible, it was unavoidable. He has determined, using a computer simulation, that even a very modest amount of hunting would have wiped out these animals.

这一看法促成了20世纪60年代出现的“过度猎杀模型”（overkill model），最初由亚利桑那大学（University of Arizona）的 Paul S. Martin 提出。此后，批评者一直指责说，没有考古遗迹支持这样的观点：最早的美洲居民曾以足以造成这些物种灭绝的规模展开狩猎。然而，在1999年10月于墨西哥城举行的脊椎动物古生物学会（Society of Vertebrate Paleontology）年会上，加州大学圣巴巴拉分校（University of California at Santa Barbara）专家 John Alroy 主张，由狩猎造成灭绝不仅可信，而且不可避免。他通过计算机模拟确定，即使狩猎量非常有限，也足以使这些动物灭绝。

Assuming an initial human population of 100 people that grew no more than two per cent annually, Alroy determined that, if each band of, say, 50 people killed 15 to 20 large animals a year, humans could have eliminated the animal populations within 1,000 years. Large mammals in particular would have been vulnerable to the pressure because they have longer gestation periods than smaller mammals and their young require extended care.

Alroy 假定，最初有100人，人类数量每年增长不超过2%；他据此认定，假如每个约50人的群体每年捕杀15至20只大型动物，人类便能在1,000年内消灭这些动物种群。大型哺乳动物尤其容易承受不住这种压力，因为它们的妊娠期长于小型哺乳动物，幼崽也需要更长时间的照料。

However, not everyone agrees with Alroy’s assessment. For one thing, the results depend on population-size estimates for the extinct animals—estimates that are not necessarily reliable. But a more specific criticism comes from mammal expert Ross D. E. MacPhee of the American Museum of Natural History in New York City, who points out that the relevant archaeological record contains barely a dozen examples of stone points embedded in mammoth bones (and none, it should be noted, are known from other megafaunal remains)—hardly what one might expect if hunting drove these animals to extinction. Furthermore, some of these species had a vast range, covering the whole continent—the Jefferson’s ground sloth, for example, lived as far north as the Yukon and as far south as Mexico—which would have made hunting them in numbers sufficient to cause their extinction rather unlikely, he says.

然而，并非人人都同意 Alroy 的评估。首先，结果取决于对已灭绝动物种群规模的估算，而这些估算未必可靠。纽约美国自然历史博物馆（American Museum of Natural History）的哺乳动物专家 Ross D. E. MacPhee 还提出了一项更具体的批评。他指出，相关考古记录中，石制尖状器嵌在猛犸象骨骼里的例子仅有十来个；而且值得注意的是，在其他巨型动物遗骸上，一个这样的例子也没有。若狩猎真的导致这些动物灭绝，这显然很不符合预期。此外，他说，有些物种的分布范围极广，覆盖整片大陆。例如，杰斐逊地懒（Jefferson’s ground sloth）向北远至育空地区（Yukon），向南远至墨西哥都有分布；要捕杀足够数量从而使其灭绝，似乎不大可能。

MacPhee agrees that humans most likely brought about these extinctions (as well as others around the world that coincided with human arrival), but not directly. Rather than through hunting, he suggests that people may have introduced a deadly disease, perhaps through their dogs or accompanying vermin, which then spread wildly among the native species because of their low resistance to the new introductions. Repeated outbreaks of a deadly disease could thus quickly drive them to the point of no return. So far, MacPhee does not have empirical evidence for this theory, and it will not be easy to come by: such disease would kill far too quickly to leave its signature on the bones themselves. But he hopes that analyses of tissue and DNA from the most recent animal remains will eventually reveal the microbes responsible.

MacPhee 同意，人类很可能造成了这些动物的灭绝，以及世界其他地区与人类到来同时发生的其他灭绝事件，但人类并不是直接导致这些灭绝。他认为，导致灭绝的不是狩猎，而可能是人类引入的一种致命疾病，传播途径也许是狗或一同迁入的有害动物。当地物种对这种新传入的疾病抵抗力很低，疾病便在它们之间广泛传播。致命疾病反复暴发，因而可以迅速把它们推向无法挽回的境地。目前，MacPhee 还没有支持这一理论的实证证据，而获得这种证据也并不容易：疾病致死速度会快到根本不会在骨骼上留下痕迹。但他希望，通过分析年代最近的动物遗骸中的组织和 DNA，最终能够发现致病微生物。

The third explanation for what brought on this North American extinction does not involve human beings. Instead, its proponents blame the loss on the climate. The Pleistocene epoch in question witnessed considerable climate instability, explains Russell W. Graham of the Denver Museum of Nature and Science. As a result, their regular habitats disappeared, and species that had once formed communities split apart. For some animals, this brought opportunity. For much of the megafauna, however, the increasingly uniform terrain left them with shrinking geographical ranges—a death sentence for large animals, which need correspondingly large ranges. Although these creatures managed to maintain viable populations through most of the Pleistocene period, the final major climate fluctuation pushed them over the edge, Graham says.

解释北美这场物种灭绝的第三种理论与人类无关，其支持者把损失归咎于气候。丹佛自然科学博物馆（Denver Museum of Nature and Science）的 Russell W. Graham 解释说，相关的更新世（Pleistocene）时期气候相当不稳定。结果，动物原有的栖息地消失，曾经组成群落的物种也彼此分离。对有些动物而言，这带来了机会。然而，对许多巨型动物而言，地形日趋单一，使它们的地理分布范围不断缩小；这对大型动物无异于死刑，因为它们需要相应广阔的活动范围。Graham 说，尽管这些生物在更新世的大部分时间都维持着足以延续的种群，但最后一次重大气候波动还是把它们推过了生存极限。

For his part, Alroy is still convinced that human hunters were the destroyers of the giant animals. The overkill model explains everything the disease and climate scenarios explain, he asserts, and in addition makes accurate predictions about which species would eventually become extinct.

就 Alroy 而言，他仍然确信人类猎手是巨型动物的毁灭者。他断言，疾病说和气候说所能解释的一切，过度猎杀模型都能解释；此外，它还能准确预测哪些物种最终会灭绝。
