Rain-forest trees growing anew on Central American farmland are helping scientists find ways for monkey and agriculture to benefit one another.

中美洲农田上重新生长的雨林树木，正帮助科学家寻找让猴类与农业相互受益的方法。

AHacienda La Pacifica, a remote working cattle ranch in Guanacaste Province of northern Costa Rica, has for decades been home to a community of mantled howler monkeys. Other native primates—white-faced capuchin monkeys and spider monkeys—were once common in this area too, but vanished after the Pan-American Highway was built nearby in the 1950s and most of the surrounding land was cleared for cattle-raising. At Hacienda La Pacifica, however, an enlightened ranch owner chose to leave some strips of native trees growing. He used these as windbreaks to protect both cattle and their food crops from dry-season winds. In the process, the farmer unwittingly founded a unique laboratory for the study of monkeys.

La Pacifica 庄园（Hacienda La Pacifica）是哥斯达黎加（Costa Rica）北部瓜纳卡斯特省（Guanacaste Province）一座偏远、仍在经营的养牛场，几十年来一直生活着一个披风吼猴（mantled howler monkeys）群体。白面卷尾猴（white-faced capuchin monkeys）和蜘蛛猴（spider monkeys）等其他本土灵长类动物，过去在这一地区也很常见；但20世纪50年代附近建成泛美公路（Pan-American Highway）、周边大部分土地被清理用于养牛后，它们便消失了。不过，La Pacifica 庄园一位开明的农场主选择保留几条生长着本土树木的地带。他把这些树带用作防风林，在旱季的风中保护牛群及其饲料作物。这个过程中，他无意间建立了一座研究猴类的独特实验室。

BKen Glander, a primatologist from Duke University in the USA, is studying La Pacifica’s monkeys in an effort to understand the relationship between howlers and regenerating forests at the edges of grazing lands. Studying such disturbed woodlands is increasingly important because throughout much of the New World Tropics, these are the only forests left. In the 18th century, tropical dry forests once covered most of Central America, but by the 1980s less than two percent remained undisturbed, and less than one percent was protected.

美国杜克大学（Duke University）灵长类动物学家 Ken Glander 正在研究 La Pacifica 的猴群，希望了解吼猴与牧场边缘再生森林之间的关系。研究这种受过干扰的林地日益重要，因为在新大陆热带地区（New World Tropics）的许多地方，它们是仅存的森林。18世纪时，热带干旱森林曾覆盖中美洲大部分地区；但到20世纪80年代，保持原状的面积已不足2%，受到保护的面积更不足1%。

CHowlers persist at La Pacifica, Glander explains, because they are leaf-eaters. They eat fruit when it is available but, unlike capuchin and spider monkeys, do not depend on large areas of fruiting trees. Glander is particularly interested in howlers’ ability to thrive on leaves loaded with toxins—poisonous substances designed to protect the plants. For leaf-eaters, long-term exposure to a specific plant toxin can increase their ability to neutralise the poisonous substances and absorb the leaf nutrients. Watching generations of howlers at La Pacifica has shown Glander that the monkeys keep their systems primed by sampling a variety of plants and then focusing on a small number of the most nutritious food items. The leaves that grow in regenerating forests, like those at La Pacifica, are actually more howler-friendly than those produced by the centuries-old trees that survive farther south. In younger forests, trees put most of their limited energy into growing wood, leaves and fruit, so they produce much lower levels of toxin than do well-established, old-growth trees.

Glander 解释说，吼猴之所以能在 La Pacifica 延续下来，是因为它们以树叶为食。有水果时，它们也会吃，但与卷尾猴和蜘蛛猴不同，它们不依赖大片结果的树林。Glander 特别关注吼猴依靠含有大量毒素的树叶仍能生长良好的能力；这些有毒物质是植物用来保护自己的。对食叶动物来说，长期接触某种特定植物毒素，可以增强中和有毒物质并吸收叶片养分的能力。Glander 观察了 La Pacifica 数代吼猴，发现它们会先尝食多种植物，使身体保持处理毒素的能力，随后集中食用少数几种营养最丰富的食物。La Pacifica 这类再生森林中长出的树叶，实际上比南面那些存活了数百年的古树所长树叶更适合吼猴。较年轻森林里的树木把有限能量大多用于生长木质部分、叶片和果实，因此产生的毒素远少于成熟老林中的树木。

DThe value of maturing forests to primates is also a subject of study at Santa Rosa National Park, about 35 miles northwest of La Pacifica. Large areas of Santa Rosa’s forests had at one time been burnt to make space for cattle ranching and coffee farming, thereby devastating local monkey habitat. But in 1971 the government protected the area by designating it a national park, and species of indigenous trees which had been absent for decades began to invade the abandoned pastures. Capuchins were the first to begin using the reborn forests, followed by howlers. Eventually, even spider monkeys, fruit-eaters that need large areas of continuous forest, returned. In the first 28 years following protection of the area, the capuchin population doubled, while the number of howlers increased seven-fold.

正在成熟的森林对灵长类动物有何价值，也是圣罗莎国家公园（Santa Rosa National Park）的一项研究课题。该公园位于 La Pacifica 西北约35英里处。圣罗莎的大片森林一度遭焚烧，以便为养牛和咖啡种植腾出空间，当地猴类栖息地因此受到毁灭性破坏。但1971年，政府把该地区划为国家公园加以保护；消失数十年的本土树种开始进入废弃牧场。卷尾猴最先开始利用重生的森林，随后是吼猴。最终，就连需要大片连片森林的食果蜘蛛猴也回来了。该地区受到保护后的最初28年里，卷尾猴种群扩大了一倍，吼猴数量则增长到原来的七倍。

ESome of the same traits that allow howlers to survive at La Pacifica also explain their population boom in Santa Rosa. Howler reproduction is faster than that of other native monkey species. They give birth for the first time at about 3.5 years of age, compared with seven years for capuchins, and eight or more for spider monkeys. Also, while a female spider monkey will have a baby about once every four years, well-fed howlers can produce an infant every two years. Another factor is diet. Howlers are very adaptable feeders and need only a comparatively small home range. Spider monkeys, on the other hand, need to occupy a huge home range. Also crucial is the fact that the leaves howlers eat hold plenty of water, so the monkeys can survive away from open streams and water-holes. This ability gives them a real advantage over capuchin and spider monkeys, which have suffered during the long, ongoing drought in the area.

吼猴得以在 La Pacifica 生存的若干特征，也能解释它们的种群为何在圣罗莎迅速扩大。吼猴的繁殖速度比其他本土猴类更快。它们约3.5岁时首次产仔，而卷尾猴要到7岁，蜘蛛猴则要到8岁或更晚。此外，雌性蜘蛛猴大约每4年才产一只幼仔，食物充足的吼猴却能每2年产一只。另一个因素是饮食。吼猴的取食适应性很强，只需相对较小的活动范围；蜘蛛猴则需要极大的活动范围。同样关键的是，吼猴所吃的树叶含有充足水分，因此它们即使远离溪流和水塘也能生存。这项能力让它们比卷尾猴和蜘蛛猴拥有明显优势；后两种猴一直受到当地漫长且仍在持续的干旱影响。

FAlejandro Estrada, an ecologist at Estación de Biología Los Tuxtlas in Veracruz, Mexico, has been studying the ecology of a group of howler monkeys that thrive in a habitat totally altered by humans: a cacao plantation in Tabasco State, Mexico. Cacao plants need shade to grow, so 40 years ago the owners of Cholula Cacao Farm planted figs, monkey-pod and other tall trees to form a protective canopy over their crop. The howlers moved in about 25 years ago after nearby forests were cut. This strange habitat seems to support about as many monkeys as would a same-sized patch of wild forest. The howlers eat the leaves and fruit of the shade trees, leaving the valuable cacao pods alone.

墨西哥韦拉克鲁斯州（Veracruz）Los Tuxtlas 生物学站（Estación de Biología Los Tuxtlas）的生态学家 Alejandro Estrada，一直在研究一群生活在完全由人类改造的栖息地中、依然生长良好的吼猴：墨西哥塔巴斯科州（Tabasco State）的一座可可种植园。可可植株需要遮阴才能生长，因此40年前，Cholula 可可农场（Cholula Cacao Farm）的业主种植了无花果树、雨树（monkey-pod）和其他高大树木，在作物上方形成保护性树冠。附近森林被砍伐后，吼猴于大约25年前迁入。这个奇特栖息地所容纳的猴子数量，似乎与同等面积的野生森林大致相当。吼猴吃遮阴树的叶片和果实，却不碰价值很高的可可果实。

GEstrada believes the monkeys bring under-appreciated benefits to such plantations, dispersing the seeds of fruits such as fig and other shade trees, and fertilising the soil. Spider monkeys also forage for fruit here, though they need nearby areas of forest to survive in the long term. He hopes that farmers will begin to see the advantages of associating with wild monkeys, which could include potential ecotourism projects. ‘Conservation is usually viewed as a conflict between farming practices and the need to preserve nature,’ Estrada says. ‘We’re moving away from that vision and beginning to consider ways in which commercial activities may become a tool for the conservation of primates in human-modified landscapes.’

Estrada 认为，猴类会给这类种植园带来未获充分认识的好处，包括传播无花果等果树和其他遮阴树的种子，以及给土壤施肥。蜘蛛猴也会来这里觅食水果，不过从长远看，它们需要附近有森林才能生存。他希望农民开始看到与野生猴类共处的益处，其中可能包括潜在的生态旅游项目。Estrada 说：“人们通常把自然保护视为农业生产方式与保护自然需要之间的冲突。我们正在摆脱这种看法，开始思考如何让商业活动成为一种工具，用来保护人类改造景观中的灵长类动物。”
