Keeping the water away

让洪水远离

New approaches to flood control

防洪新方法

ARecently, winter floods on the rivers of central Europe have been among the worst for 600 to 700 years, and dams and dykes (protective sea walls) have failed to solve the problem. Traditionally, river engineers have tried to get rid of the water quickly, draining it off the land and down to the sea in rivers reengineered as high-performance drains. But however high they build the artificial riverbanks, the floods keep coming back. And when they come, they seem to be worse than ever.

近来，中欧河流的冬季洪水达到600至700年来最严重的程度之一，水坝和堤坝（防护性海堤）都未能解决问题。传统上，河流工程师设法迅速排走洪水：让水离开陆地，并通过改造成高效排水渠的河道流入大海。然而，无论人工河岸筑得多高，洪水都会卷土重来；而且每次来袭，似乎都比以往更严重。

BEngineers are now turning to a different plan: to sap the water’s destructive strength by dispersing it into fields, forgotten lakes and flood plains. They are reviving river bends and marshes to curb the flow, and even plugging city drains to encourage floodwater to use other means to go underground. Back in the days when rivers took a winding path to the sea, floodwaters lost force and volume while meandering across flood plains and inland deltas, but today the water tends to have a direct passage to the sea. This means that, when it rains in the uplands, the water comes down all at once.

如今，工程师转而采用另一种方案：把水分散到田野、被遗忘的湖泊和洪泛平原中，削弱其破坏力。他们正在恢复河流弯道和沼泽，以减缓水流，甚至堵住城市排水管，让洪水通过其他途径渗入地下。过去，河流蜿蜒入海，洪水迂回穿过洪泛平原和内陆三角洲时，会失去力量并减少水量；如今，水流往往直达大海。这意味着高地一下雨，水就会同时倾泻而下。

CWorse, when the flood plains are closed off, the river’s flow downstream becomes more violent and uncontrollable; by turning complex river systems into the simple mechanics of a water pipe, engineers have often created danger where they promised safety. The Rhine, Europe’s most engineered river, is a good example. For a long time engineers have erased its backwaters and cut it off from its plain. The aim was partly to improve navigation, and partly to speed floodwaters out of the Alps and down to the North Sea. Now, when it rains in the Alps, the peak flows from several branches of the Rhine coincide where once they arrived separately, and with four-fifths of the Lower Rhine’s flood plain barricaded off, the waters rise. The result is more frequent flooding and greater damage. The same thing has happened in the US on the Mississippi River, which drains the world’s second-largest river catchment into the Gulf of Mexico. Despite some $7 billion spent over the last century on levees (embankments), the situation is growing worse.

更糟的是，洪泛平原一旦被隔绝，河流下游的水势就会变得更加猛烈而失控。工程师把复杂的河流系统变成水管般简单的机械通道，常常在承诺安全之处反而制造了危险。欧洲受人工改造最多的河流莱茵河就是一个典型例子。长期以来，工程师不断消除它的回水区，并切断它与洪泛平原的联系；目的部分是改善航运，部分是让洪水更快流出阿尔卑斯山，奔向北海。如今，阿尔卑斯山一下雨，莱茵河数条支流的洪峰会在同一时间汇合，而过去它们是先后抵达的；再加上下莱茵河五分之四的洪泛平原被堤坝隔离，水位便会上涨，结果是洪水更频繁、破坏更严重。同样的情况也发生在美国密西西比河。它把世界第二大河流集水区的水排入墨西哥湾；尽管过去一个世纪投入约70亿修建防洪堤，情况仍在恶化。

DSpecialists in water control now say that a new approach is needed — one which takes the whole landscape into consideration. To help keep London’s feet dry, the UK Environment Agency is reflooding 10 square kilometres of the ancient flood plain of the River Thames outside Oxford. Nearer to London, it has spent £100 million creating new wetlands and a relief channel across 16 kilometres of flood plain. Similar ideas are being tested in Austria, in one of Europe’s largest river restorations to date. The engineers calculate that the restored flood plain of the Drava River can now store up to 10 million cubic metres of floodwater, and slow down storm surges coming out of the Alps by more than an hour, protecting towns not only in Austria, but as far downstream as Slovenia and Croatia.

如今，水利专家表示，需要一种把整个地貌纳入考虑的新方法。为帮助伦敦免遭水患，英国环境署（UK Environment Agency）正让牛津城外泰晤士河古老洪泛平原中的10平方公里区域重新蓄水。在更靠近伦敦的地方，该署投入1亿英镑，在16公里长的洪泛平原上建造新的湿地和分洪河道。奥地利也在试验类似构想，实施欧洲迄今规模最大的河流修复工程之一。工程师计算得出，修复后的德拉瓦河（Drava River）洪泛平原如今最多可储存1,000万立方米洪水，并使来自阿尔卑斯山的暴雨洪峰延迟一个多小时，不仅保护奥地利的城镇，也保护下游远至斯洛文尼亚和克罗地亚的城镇。

EThe Dutch, for whom preventing floods is a matter of survival, have gone furthest. This nation, built largely on drained marshes and seabed, has had several severe shocks in the last two decades, when very large numbers of people have had to be evacuated. Since that time, the Dutch have broken one of their most enduring national stereotypes by allowing engineers to punch holes in dykes. They plan to return up to a sixth of the country to its former waterlogged state in order to better protect the rest.

对荷兰人来说，防洪关乎生存，因此他们走得最远。这个国家大部分建在排干的沼泽和海床上，过去20年里曾遭受数次严重冲击，大量居民不得不撤离。自那以后，荷兰人打破了本国一种根深蒂固的典型形象，允许工程师在堤坝上开洞。他们计划让全国最多六分之一的土地恢复到从前的水涝状态，以便更好地保护其余地区。

FWater use in cities also needs to change. At the moment, cities seem to create floods; they are concreted and paved so that rains flow quickly into rivers. A new breed of ‘soft engineers’ wants cities to be porous; Berlin is one place where this is being done. Tough new rules for new developments mean that drains will be prevented from becoming overloaded after heavy rains. Architects of new urban buildings are diverting rainwater from the roofs for use in toilets and the irrigation of roof gardens, while water falling onto the ground is collected in ponds, or passes underground through porous paving. One high-tech urban development can store a sixth of its annual rainfall, and reuse most of the rest.

城市用水方式也需要改变。目前，城市似乎会制造洪水：地面由混凝土和铺装材料覆盖，雨水因而迅速流入河流。新一代“软性工程”工程师（soft engineers）希望城市能够透水，柏林便是正在实践这一构想的地方。针对新开发项目的严格新规意味着，暴雨过后排水系统不会再超负荷。新建城市建筑的设计师把屋顶雨水引去冲洗厕所、灌溉屋顶花园；落到地面的水则汇入池塘，或穿过透水铺装渗入地下。一个高科技城市开发区可以储存其全年降雨量的六分之一，并重新利用其余雨水中的大部分。

GCould this be expanded to protect a whole city? The test case could be Los Angeles. With non-porous surfaces covering 70% of the city, drainage is a huge challenge. Billions of dollars have been spent digging huge drains and concreting riverbeds, but many communities still flood regularly. Meanwhile, this desert city ships water from hundreds of kilometres away to fill its taps and swimming pool. Los Angeles has recently launched a new scheme to utilise floodwater in the Sun Valley section of the city. The plan is to catch the rain that falls on thousands of driveways, parking lots and rooftops in the valley. Trees will soak up water from parking lots; houses and public buildings will capture roof water to irrigate gardens and parks, and road drains will empty into old gravel pits to recharge the city’s underground water reserves. Result: less flooding and more water for the city. It may sound expensive, until we realise how much is spent trying to drain cities and protect areas from flooding, and how little this method achieves.

这种做法能否扩大到保护整座城市？洛杉矶可能成为试验案例。城市70%的面积由不透水表面覆盖，排水是一项巨大挑战。人们已经花费数十亿美元开挖巨型排水渠，并用混凝土铺设河床，但许多社区仍经常发生洪灾。与此同时，这座沙漠城市从数百公里外调水，供水龙头和游泳池使用。最近，洛杉矶启动了一项新计划，要在市内太阳谷（Sun Valley）地区利用洪水。计划将收集谷地数千条车道、停车场和屋顶上的降雨。树木会吸收停车场的积水；住宅和公共建筑将收集屋顶雨水来灌溉花园和公园；道路排水管则会把水排入废弃砾石坑，补充城市地下水储备。结果是洪灾减少，城市可用水增加。这个方案听来或许昂贵，但只要想到为了给城市排水、防止各地受淹，人们投入了多少钱，而这种传统方法成效又多么有限，就不会这样认为了。
