An important language development

文字发展中的重要一步

Cuneiform, the world’s first known system of handwriting, originated some 6,000 years ago in Sumer in what is now southern Iraq. It was most often inscribed on palm-sized, rectangular clay tablets measuring several centimetres across, although occasionally larger tablets or cylinders were used. Clay was an excellent medium for writing. Other surfaces which have been employed—for example, parchment, papyrus and paper—are not long-lasting and are easily destroyed by fire and water. But clay has proved to be resistant to those particular kinds of damage.

楔形文字是目前所知世界上最早的手写文字体系，约6,000年前起源于苏美尔（Sumer），也就是今天的伊拉克南部。它通常刻写在手掌大小的长方形泥板上，泥板宽度为数厘米；不过，人们偶尔也会使用更大的泥板或圆柱体。黏土是极佳的书写介质。羊皮纸、纸莎草纸和纸张等其他曾被用于书写的材料不耐久，也很容易被火和水毁坏；但事实证明，黏土能够抵御这些特定类型的破坏。

The word ‘cuneiform’ actually refers to the marks or signs inscribed in the clay. The original cuneiform signs consisted of a series of lines—triangular, vertical, diagonal, and horizontal. Sumerian writers would impress these lines into the wet clay with a stylus—a long, thin, pointed instrument which looked somewhat like a pen. Oddly, the signs were often almost too small to see with the naked eye. Cuneiform signs were used for the writing of at least a dozen languages. This is similar to how the Latin alphabet is used today for writing English, French, Spanish and German, for example.

“楔形文字”这个名称实际上指的是刻在黏土上的笔画或符号。最初的楔形符号由一系列三角形、竖直、斜向和水平线条组成。苏美尔书写者会用一种细长而尖、外形有点像笔的书写工具，把这些线条压进湿润的黏土中。奇怪的是，这些符号往往小得几乎无法用肉眼看清。楔形符号至少被用来书写12种语言。这类似于今天人们使用拉丁字母书写英语、法语、西班牙语和德语等语言。

Before the development of cuneiform, tokens were used by the Sumerians to record certain information. For example, they might take small stones and use them as tokens or representations of something else, like a goat. A number of tokens, then, might mean a herd of goats. These tokens might then be placed in a cloth container and provided to a buyer as a receipt for a transaction, perhaps five tokens for five animals. It was not that different from what we do today when we buy some bread and the clerk gives us back a piece of paper with numbers on it to confirm the exchange.

在楔形文字形成之前，苏美尔人使用小物件来记录某些信息。例如，他们可能取一些小石子，把它们当作代表其他事物的记数物，比如一只山羊。若干枚记数物就可能代表一群山羊。随后，这些记数物可能被放进布袋，交给买方作为交易凭证；比如，可能用5枚记数物代表5只动物。这与今天买面包时店员交给我们一张写有数字、用于确认交易的纸条并没有太大不同。

By the 4th century BCE, the Sumerians had adapted this system to a form of writing. They began putting tokens in a container resembling an envelope, one made of clay instead of cloth. They then stamped the outside to indicate the number and type of tokens inside. A person could then ‘read’ what was stamped on the container and know what was inside.

到了公元前4世纪，苏美尔人已将这一体系改造成一种书写形式。他们开始把记数物装进一个类似信封的容器中，只不过这个容器由黏土而非布制成。然后，他们会在容器外面压印标记，说明里面记数物的数量和种类。这样一来，人们只需“阅读”容器上的印记，就能知道里面装了什么。

Gradually, Sumerians developed symbols for words. When first developed, each symbol looked like the concrete thing it represented. For example, an image which resembled the drawing of a sheep meant just that. Then another level of abstraction was introduced when symbols were developed for intangible ideas such as ‘female’ or ‘hot’ or ‘God’. Cuneiform, in other words, evolved from a way used primarily to track and store information into a way to represent the world symbolically. Over the centuries, the marks became ever more abstract, finally evolving into signs that looked nothing like what they referred to, just as the letters ‘h-o-u-s-e’ have no visual connection to the place we live in. At this last stage in the evolution of cuneiform, the signs took the form of triangles, which became common cuneiform signs.

苏美尔人逐渐发展出表示词语的符号。最初出现时，每个符号都像它所代表的具体事物。例如，一个看起来像羊的图形就表示羊。后来，人们又进一步抽象，发展出表示“女性”“热”或“神”等无形概念的符号。换言之，楔形文字从一种主要用于追踪和保存信息的方法，演变成了用符号表示世界的方法。几个世纪间，这些笔画变得越来越抽象，最终演变成与所指事物毫无相似之处的符号，就像 h-o-u-s-e 这几个字母在外形上与我们居住的房屋没有任何联系一样。在楔形文字演变的最后阶段，这些符号呈三角形，成为常见的楔形符号。

As the marks became more abstract, the system became more efficient because there were fewer marks for a ‘reader’ to learn. But cuneiform also became more complex because society itself was becoming more complex, so there were more ideas and concepts that needed to be expressed. However, most linguists and historians agree cuneiform developed primarily as a tool for accounting. Of the cuneiform tablets that have been discovered, excavated and translated, about 75 percent contain this type of practical information, rather than artistic or imaginative work.

随着笔画变得更抽象，需要“读者”学习的笔画种类减少了，这套体系也因此变得更高效。然而，由于社会本身日益复杂，需要表达的观念和概念越来越多，楔形文字同时也变得更复杂。不过，大多数语言学家和历史学家都同意，楔形文字最初主要是作为记账工具发展起来的。在已经发现、发掘并翻译的楔形文字泥板中，约75%记录的是这类实用信息，而不是艺术或想象性作品。

Cuneiform writing was used for thousands of years, but it eventually ceased to be used in everyday life. In fact, it died out and remained unintelligible for almost 2,000 years. In the late 19th century, a British army officer, Henry Rawlinson, discovered cuneiform inscriptions which had been carved into the surface of rocks in the Behistun Mountains in what is present-day Iran. Rawlinson made impressions of the marks on large pieces of paper as he balanced dangerously on the surrounding rocks.

楔形文字使用了数千年，但最终退出了日常生活。事实上，它彻底失传，近2,000年间一直无人能够读懂。19世纪末，英国军官 Henry Rawlinson 在今伊朗境内的贝希斯敦山（Behistun Mountains）发现了刻在岩石表面的楔形文字铭文。Rawlinson 冒险在周围的岩石上保持身体平衡，同时用大张纸拓下这些笔画。

Rawlinson took his copies home to Britain and studied them for years to determine what each line stood for, and what each group of symbols meant. He found that, in the writing on those particular rocks, every word was repeated three times in three languages: Old Persian, Elamite and Babylonian. Since the meanings in these languages were already known to linguists, he could thus translate the cuneiform. Eventually, he fully decoded the cuneiform marks and discovered that they described the life of Darius, a king of the Persian Empire in the 5th century BCE.

Rawlinson 把拓本带回英国，研究多年，以确定每条线代表什么、每组符号又是什么意思。他发现，在那些特定岩石上的文字中，每个词都出现3次，分别以3种语言书写：古波斯语、埃兰语和巴比伦语。语言学家当时已经知道这些语言所表达的意思，因此他得以翻译楔形文字。最终，他完全破译了这些楔形符号，并发现铭文记述的是公元前5世纪波斯帝国国王 Darius 的一生。
