Laura Ingalls Wilder’s semi-autobiographical novel The Long Winter describes how the inhabitants of a small town called De Smet in the American Midwest narrowly avoided starvation during the severe winter of 1880–1881. Over three metres of snow fell on the northern plains, immobilising the railways and cutting off the settlers from the rest of the world. Laura and her neighbours were only saved when her fiancé and his friend trudged 30 kilometres through the snow to fetch food, risking their lives in the process. The story is a reminder of how lethal geographical isolation and crop failures could be before the advent of modern farming and transportation technologies. Not long ago, subsistence farmers in many countries had to cope with the ‘lean season’ – the period of greatest scarcity before new crops became available. In England, late spring was once referred to as the ‘hungry gap’. The situation was made worse by the cost of moving heavy things over muddy dirt roads; three centuries ago, moving goods 50 kilometres on land between, say, Liverpool and Manchester was as expensive as shipping them across the North Atlantic.

Laura Ingalls Wilder 的半自传体小说《漫长的冬季》（The Long Winter）讲述了1880至1881年严冬期间，美国中西部一个名为 De Smet 的小镇居民如何侥幸免于饿死。北部平原降雪超过三米，铁路完全瘫痪，定居者与外界隔绝。Laura 的未婚夫和他的一位朋友冒着生命危险，在雪中艰难跋涉30公里取回食物，她和邻居们才得以获救。这个故事提醒我们，在现代农业和运输技术出现以前，地理隔绝与作物歉收可能多么致命。就在不久前，许多国家的自给农民还不得不熬过“青黄不接期”（lean season），也就是新一季作物可供食用之前、食物最匮乏的时期。在英格兰，晚春曾被称为“饥饿空档期”（hungry gap）。泥泞土路上搬运重物的成本使情况雪上加霜。三个世纪前，假设要在利物浦与曼彻斯特之间通过陆路运输货物50公里，其费用与横渡北大西洋运送货物一样高。

The development of coal-powered railways and steamships in the 19th century revolutionized the lives of farmers. Instead of having to grow everything they needed, they could now specialise in what they did best and rely on other producers for their remaining needs. The result was not only food and ever-cheaper prices, but the end of widespread famine and starvation, as the surplus from regions with good harvests could now be transported to those that had experienced mediocre ones. Since then, petroleum-derived fuels have largely displaced coal because of their higher energy density, cleaner combustion and greater ease of extraction, further improving road and rail transportation systems.

19世纪，燃煤铁路和蒸汽船的发展彻底改变了农民的生活。他们不必再自行种植所需的一切，而可以专门生产自己最擅长的东西，其余需求则依赖其他生产者满足。结果不仅是食物得以供应、价格不断下降，大范围饥荒和饥饿也随之终结，因为丰收地区的余粮如今可以运往收成一般的地区。此后，石油衍生燃料凭借能量密度更高、燃烧更清洁且更容易开采等优势，在很大程度上取代了煤炭，进一步改善了公路和铁路运输系统。

While the convenience of modern methods of transportation is obvious, few people grasp their historical significance in terms of their beneficial impact on large cities and the health of residents. In 1898, delegates gathered in New York City for the world’s first international urban-planning conference.

现代运输方式十分便利，这一点显而易见；但很少有人理解，它们对大城市和居民健康产生有益影响，在历史上具有怎样的重要意义。1898年，各地代表齐聚纽约市，参加世界上第一次国际城市规划会议。

The topic that dominated discussions was not infrastructure or housing, but horse manure. The problem was that as the populations of cities like New York and London grew, the number of horses there also grew, and in New York these produced nearly two million kilograms of manure each day. If this problem continued, it was estimated that by 1950 every street in London would be buried three metres deep in horse manure. Unable to think of any solution, the delegates concluded that urban living was inherently unsustainable.

主导会议讨论的议题不是基础设施或住房，而是马粪。问题在于，随着纽约和伦敦等城市人口增长，马匹数量也随之增加；仅纽约的马每天就会产生近200万公斤粪便。据估计，如果问题持续下去，到1950年，伦敦每条街道都会被三米深的马粪掩埋。代表们想不出任何解决办法，因而得出结论：城市生活本质上无法持续。

Paradoxically, much of the urban manure problem was related to the growth of the railways. The ability to deliver perishable goods, such as meat and dairy products, from locations that benefited from better soil and climate, put many farms located near cities out of business. As these had relied on manure from city workhorses for fertiliser, the demand for this was greatly reduced.

矛盾的是，城市的马粪问题很大程度上与铁路发展有关。铁路能把肉类和奶制品等易腐商品从土壤与气候条件较好的地区运来，致使许多城市附近的农场倒闭。这些农场原本依靠城市役马的粪便作肥料，因此对马粪的需求大幅减少。

The impact of urban workhorses was felt both in the cities and in the countryside. In cities, apart from their overpowering stench, the manure piles were prime breeding grounds for house flies, perhaps three billion of which hatched each day in US cities in the early 20th century. With flies came outbreaks of typhoid, cholera and diphtheria. Workhorses sometimes panicked in heavy traffic and kicked or bit bystanders. The clatter of wagon wheels on cobblestone pavement could be deafening, and since a horse and wagon occupied more street space than a modern truck, they also created significant traffic congestion, while a horse that collapsed on the road created an obstruction that was difficult to remove. The countryside also suffered. To supply the workhorses with oats and hay, additional land had to be cleared of its natural animal life and vegetation, and sometimes water had to be diverted to irrigate it, with considerable negative effects on the whole area.

城市役马的影响既波及城市，也波及乡村。在城市里，粪堆不仅散发令人难以忍受的恶臭，还是家蝇的主要孳生地；20世纪初，美国城市每天孵化的家蝇或许多达30亿只。苍蝇随之带来了伤寒、霍乱和白喉的暴发。役马有时会在拥挤交通中受惊，踢伤或咬伤旁人。马车车轮在鹅卵石路面上发出的哐当声可能震耳欲聋；一匹马和一辆马车占用的街道空间比现代卡车还多，因此也会造成严重交通拥堵，而一匹倒在路上的马则会成为很难清除的障碍。乡村同样受害。为了给役马供应燕麦和干草，人们不得不开垦更多土地，清除原有的动物和植被；有时还要改道引水灌溉，对整个地区造成相当严重的负面影响。

So, while the trains, cars and trucks of the early 20th century were noisy and polluting by today’s standards, they were regarded as a significant improvement on what had gone before. Before they were available, poor soils often meant that a large amount of land was required to sustain a household, and much environmental damage, primarily in the form of soil erosion, was caused by trying to farm these soils. It could be argued that modern transportation allowed the development of remote regions like the Canadian Prairies and allowed more suitable crops to be grown in the poorer soils in Europe before being sold elsewhere.

因此，尽管以今天的标准来看，20世纪初的火车、汽车和卡车既嘈杂又造成污染，当时的人们仍认为，它们比此前的运输方式有了显著改善。在这些交通工具出现以前，贫瘠土壤往往意味着维持一个家庭需要大片土地；尝试耕种这类土壤还造成了大量环境破坏，主要表现为土壤侵蚀。可以说，现代运输使加拿大大草原（Canadian Prairies）等偏远地区得以开发，也让人们能够在欧洲较贫瘠的土地上种植更适合的作物，再运往别处销售。

Over time, the concentration of food production in the world’s best locations allowed some agricultural land to revert to a wild state. For instance, France benefited from an expansion of its forest area by one third between 1830 and 1960. This so-called ‘forest transition’ occurred in the context of a doubling of the French population and a dramatic increase in standards of living.

随着时间推移，食品生产集中到世界上条件最优越的地区，使一部分农地得以恢复为自然状态。例如，1830至1960年间，法国的森林面积扩大了三分之一。这种所谓的“森林转型”（forest transition），是在法国人口翻了一番、生活水平显著提高的背景下发生的。

Improvements in logistics also allowed the production and export of food from locations where water was abundant to regions where it was scarce, thus preventing the depletion of water resources there. It also made possible a drastic increase in the size of our cities. Contrary to what most people believe, the growth in cities is a positive development. In the words of economist Ed Glaeser: ‘Residing in a forest might seem to be a good way of showing one’s love of nature, but living in a concrete jungle is actually far more ecologically friendly… If you love nature, stay away from it.’ It could be argued that modern transportation technologies have been a major contributor to a wealthier, cleaner and more sustainable world.

物流改善还使水资源充足的地区能够生产并出口食物，供应水资源匮乏的地区，从而防止后者的水资源耗竭。这也使城市规模能够急剧扩大。与大多数人的看法相反，城市增长是一种积极发展。经济学家 Ed Glaeser 说：“居住在森林里似乎是表达热爱自然的好办法，但生活在混凝土丛林其实对生态环境友好得多……如果你热爱自然，就离它远一点。”可以说，现代运输技术为世界变得更富裕、更清洁、更可持续作出了重大贡献。
