Charles Darwin, the brilliant anthropologist and creator of the theory of evolution, is not normally associated with the modern business world. Nevertheless, Darwinian evolutionary theory is the foundation of a new wave of ideas about human behaviour in general and particularly the way people behave in the workplace; these ideas have been given the title of ‘evolutionary psychology’. Evolutionary psychology revolves around the notion that our brains, like our bodies, have an inherited evolutionary design that has scarcely changed for 10,000 years. As respected evolutionary psychology experts Leda Cosmides and John Tooby comment, ‘our modern skulls house a Stone Age mind’. The US biologist Edward O. Wilson sees evolutionary psychology as being a discipline which is based on both sociobiology, which is the study of the biological basis of social behaviour, and psychology, which is the systematic study of human behaviour.

杰出的人类学家、进化论创立者达尔文（Charles Darwin），通常不会让人联想到现代商业世界。然而，达尔文进化论构成了一股新思潮的基础；这股思潮探讨一般意义上的人类行为，尤其是人们在工作场所中的行为，并被冠以“进化心理学”之名。进化心理学围绕这样一种观点展开：我们的大脑与身体一样，具有遗传而来的进化设计，1万年来几乎没有改变。正如备受尊敬的进化心理学专家 Leda Cosmides 和 John Tooby 所说：“我们的现代头骨里装着石器时代的心智。”美国生物学家 Edward O. Wilson 认为，进化心理学以社会生物学和心理学为基础；前者研究社会行为的生物学基础，后者系统研究人类行为。

Nigel Nicholson, an organisational psychologist from the London Business School, is a strong supporter of evolutionary psychology and on this subject has published Managing the Human Animal. His book takes the reader on a journey from the Stone Age plains of the savannah to the modern office, and includes a discussion of Darwinism and behavioural psychology, together with a dissection of dysfunctional organisational behaviour. It is an effective approach explaining why people behave as they do, particularly at work. Evolutionary psychology is increasingly being cited in management circles, where managers are trying to understand puzzling aspects of human behaviour and, by doing so, improve the workplace. Nicholson believes that evolutionary psychology can help managers understand what goes wrong in organisational life and what they can do about it.

伦敦商学院（London Business School）的组织心理学家 Nigel Nicholson 是进化心理学的坚定支持者，并就此出版了《管理人类动物》（Managing the Human Animal）。他的书带领读者从石器时代的稀树草原一路走进现代办公室，既讨论达尔文主义和行为心理学，也剖析功能失调的组织行为。这种方法能够有效解释人们为何以特定方式行事，尤其是在工作中。管理界越来越多地引用进化心理学；管理者试图借此理解人类行为中令人困惑的方面，并以此改善工作场所。Nicholson 认为，进化心理学可以帮助管理者理解组织生活中哪里出了问题，以及他们能为此做些什么。

Nicholson maintains that evolutionary psychology dismisses the long-held assumption that our minds are like blank pages just waiting for culture and experience to write on them and shape our nature. He points out that sophisticated research shows the brain actually houses a store of knowledge when we are born, and now genetic research is establishing there are certain genes that account for abilities, tastes and tendencies. The stored knowledge in the human brain has not changed much since the Stone Age. As Tooby and Cosmides stress, there have not been enough generations for a brain that is well-adapted to our post-industrial life to evolve through natural selection.

Nicholson 认为，进化心理学摒弃了一个长期存在的假设：我们的心智就像空白书页，只等文化和经验在上面书写并塑造我们的天性。他指出，深入研究表明，我们出生时大脑中其实已经储存着知识；如今，遗传学研究还在证实，某些基因会影响能力、喜好和倾向。人脑中储存的这些知识自石器时代以来并没有发生太大变化。正如 Tooby 和 Cosmides 强调的那样，世代更替的次数还不够多，不足以让一个能够良好适应后工业时代生活的大脑通过自然选择进化出来。

The evolutionary psychology version of human nature revolves around some key elements which we have inherited from our hunter-gatherer minds. One key element is emotion. Emotion was originally essential to keep early man alive and safe from predators. Emotion was, and continues to be, our radar, guiding us throughout today’s techno-defined business world. Despite this, the business world emphasises rational, not emotional, behaviour, and does not admit the importance of emotion. We still use the emotional part of our minds to make sense of other people’s behaviour and to create an impression, so we can often be taken in by appearances. This mental predisposition actually works best in small communities (the tribe), not in much larger environments filled with people we barely know (the modern workplace). Our minds naturally try to re-create our ancestral communities with networks of no more than 150 people, where there are clear hierarchies and leaders. As a consequence, it takes very little to trigger people’s innate distrust of others because our safety in antiquity depended on supporting our near family and friends, whom we valued more than other people.

进化心理学对人性的解释，围绕着我们从狩猎采集者心智中继承的一些关键要素，其中之一就是情绪。情绪最初对早期人类躲避捕食者、保障生存与安全至关重要。无论过去还是现在，情绪都是我们的雷达，引导我们穿行于如今由科技塑造的商业世界。尽管如此，商业世界强调理性行为而非情绪行为，也不承认情绪的重要性。我们仍会用心智中的情绪部分来理解他人的行为并形成印象，因此常常可能被表象蒙骗。这种心理倾向在小型群体，也就是部落中最有效；在挤满我们几乎不认识之人的大环境，也就是现代工作场所中，却并非如此。我们的心智会自然地尝试重建祖先的群体，形成不超过150人的网络，其中有明确的等级和首领。因此，只需很小的诱因就会触发人们对他人的本能不信任，因为在古代，我们的安全取决于支持近亲和朋友，而我们对他们的重视也超过其他人。

So what advice does Nicholson have for the corporate world? He thinks that by knowing the reasons for people’s behaviour it is possible to mould corporate environments into places that have more chance of working efficiently and being pleasant places to work in. Nicholson admits that not everybody in the business world agrees with his belief in the effectiveness of evolutionary psychology in the workplace. One group that resists the theory of evolutionary psychology is young MBA graduates who are just beginning their careers and feel that evolutionary psychology will make their lives at work more difficult. Older and wiser executives point out that they still tend to cling to the idea of a magic formula to bring people into line with corporate strategy. But that is back-to-front thinking, according to Nicholson, who contends that we should be reinventing our business structures, not our fundamental human nature.

那么，Nicholson 对企业界有什么建议？他认为，只要了解人们行为背后的原因，就可以把企业环境塑造成更有可能高效运转、也更宜于工作的地方。Nicholson 承认，商界并非所有人都认同他关于进化心理学在职场中有效的看法。抵制进化心理学理论的一类人，是刚开始职业生涯的年轻工商管理硕士（MBA）毕业生；他们觉得，进化心理学会让自己的职场生活变得更困难。年龄更长、经验更丰富的高管指出，就连他们自己也往往仍执着于寻找一种神奇公式，让员工与企业战略保持一致。但 Nicholson 认为，这种思路本末倒置：我们应当重塑企业结构，而不是重塑人类的基本天性。

At the end of his book, Nicholson gives his forecast of what will and will not change in the business world. He believes that most people will still prefer more traditional forms of work and throughout their lives will continue to aim at lifelong status advancement. He also maintains that the line between work and home will be less defined, but that people will prefer traditional working patterns if working from home leaves them isolated from their work community. He doubts that the high-tech ideas of virtual companies will ever be very successful because people will still want to meet each other face-to-face. Nicholson describes his ideal organisation in the future: it would be decentralised, with small sub-units; the staff would be from diverse backgrounds and be allowed a high degree of self-determination. New endeavours and creativity would replace systems and rationality. Nicholson acknowledges that there is a long way to go in terms of the translation of his ideas of evolutionary psychology into practical propositions, but he is confident more and more people will come round to his way of thinking.

在书的结尾，Nicholson 预测了商业世界中哪些方面会改变，哪些不会。他认为，大多数人仍会偏爱较为传统的工作形式，并会终其一生继续追求地位提升。他还认为，工作与家庭之间的界线将变得不那么清晰；但如果在家工作使人与工作群体隔绝，人们仍会偏爱传统的工作模式。他怀疑，虚拟公司这种高科技构想是否会取得很大成功，因为人们仍希望面对面交流。Nicholson 还描述了自己心目中未来的理想组织：组织应当分散管理，下设小型单位；员工来自不同背景，并获准拥有高度自主决定权。新的尝试和创造力将取代制度和理性。Nicholson 承认，要把他的进化心理学观点转化为实际方案，还有很长的路要走；但他相信，会有越来越多的人转而接受他的思路。
