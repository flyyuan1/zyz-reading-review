There are many differences between playing soccer in the street and joining a youth team in an organized league in the USA

在街头踢球，与加入美国正规联赛中的一支青少年球队，存在许多差别

AStreet soccer, as its name implies, is an informal variation of the sport, often played on the street, particularly in urban areas. There are many reasons for the widespread popularity of street soccer. Unlike youth soccer, its more formally organized counterpart, no large space is needed, and goal posts, corner markers, and marked lines, associated with the formal game, are typically absent, as are game officials or referees. Another attraction of street soccer is that it is played frequently and competitively, but does not necessarily require standard 11-a-side teams or fixed playing positions. Unlike in youth soccer, inexperienced street soccer players rarely learn from repetitive technical and tactical drills. Instead, they learn from their poor performance in competition, unconscious of the skills they are nonetheless developing, and without older adults or coaches present. Players learn without effort through playing the game, and soon attain an almost natural feeling for the sport.

顾名思义，街头足球是这种运动的一种非正式变体，常常在街道上进行，尤其是在城区。街头足球广受欢迎的原因很多。与组织更正式的青少年足球不同，它不需要大片场地；正式比赛中的球门柱、角区标志和场地标线通常都没有，比赛官员或裁判通常也不在场。街头足球的另一个吸引力在于，比赛经常进行，而且具有竞争性，却不一定需要标准的11人制球队或固定位置。与青少年足球不同，经验不足的街头球员很少通过反复练习技术和战术来学习。相反，他们会从比赛中表现不佳的经历中学习；虽然没有意识到自己正在培养相关技巧，但这些技巧仍在发展，而且身边也没有年长者或教练。球员通过踢比赛毫不费力地学习，很快便对这项运动形成一种近乎本能的感觉。

BHowever, there are lots of cities in the world today where conditions are such that street soccer is no longer possible. Congested traffic now dominates where games were once played. Parks and open fields are used as hangouts for older teenagers with other interests. Add to this the requirement in many localities for official permits to use public spaces and the managed schedules that many young people have today, and spontaneous play of any kind is hard to imagine.

然而，如今世界上许多城市的环境已经让街头足球无法进行。过去踢球的地方，如今被拥堵的交通占据；公园和空地成了兴趣不同的年长青少年的聚集处。再加上许多地方要求取得官方许可才能使用公共空间，以及当今年轻人的日程往往都被安排妥当，任何形式的即兴玩耍都很难想象。

CIn spite of all these obstacles, which are probably solvable in most instances, there is another sociological explanation of why in many places street soccer doesn’t enjoy the same popularity it once did. In his book How Soccer Explains the World, US writer Franklin Foer observes: But for all the talk of freedom, the 1960s parenting style had a far less relaxed side too. Like the 1960s consumer movement which brought seat belts and airbags to cars, the (youth) soccer movement felt like it could create a set of … regulations that would protect both the child’s body and mind from damage. Soccer leagues like the one I played in as a child handed out ‘participation’ prizes to every player, no matter how few games his (or her) team won. Where most of the world accepts the practice of using your head to hit the ball as an essential element of the game, some (youth) soccer parents have worried over the potential for injury to the brain. An entire industry grew up to manufacture protective headgear. Even though very little medical evidence supports this fear, some youth leagues prohibited heading the ball altogether.

尽管所有这些障碍在大多数情况下可能都能解决，但许多地方的街头足球不再像从前那样流行，还有另一种社会学解释。美国作家 Franklin Foer 在 How Soccer Explains the World 一书中指出：尽管人们大谈自由，20世纪60年代的育儿方式也有远没有那么宽松的一面。就像20世纪60年代的消费者运动给汽车带来了安全带和安全气囊一样，青少年足球运动认为，它可以建立一套……规章，保护儿童的身心免受伤害。我小时候参加的那类足球联赛，会给每名球员颁发“参与奖”，即使他或她所在的球队赢的比赛再少也一样。世界大部分地区都接受用头顶球是足球运动不可或缺的一环，但一些青少年足球运动员的家长担心，这可能会损伤大脑。于是，一个制造防护头具的完整产业应运而生。尽管支持这种担忧的医学证据极少，一些青少年联赛还是完全禁止了头球。

DA growing body of people don’t believe street soccer involves a legitimate educational method. They argue that children need to be taught by experts. Youth soccer instruction now begins with four-year-olds, so that they will have an advantage as six-year-olds. This need to get ahead brings with it a fear of falling behind that only expert instruction can prevent. This type of instruction leaves no room for the trial-and-error approach of street soccer.

越来越多人不相信街头足球包含合理的教育方法。他们认为，儿童需要由专家教导。如今，青少年足球训练从4岁就开始，以便孩子到6岁时占据优势。这种争先的需要也带来对落后的恐惧，而人们认为只有专家指导才能防止落后。这类指导不给街头足球通过试错学习的方法留下任何空间。

EOne of the basic ideas of street soccer is that young players are assigned a particular role by a better player and are expected to play for the good of the team. Such an assignment runs counter to the idea of youth soccer that every child needs to learn every position and will benefit from doing so. In street soccer, you fill the role that you are best able to at a particular time. While this role assignment can change from game to game, the purpose is always the same: to get the best out of each individual at any given moment.

街头足球的一个基本观念是，水平更高的球员会为年轻球员分配特定角色，而后者应当为全队利益踢球。这样的安排与青少年足球的观念相抵触；青少年足球认为，每个孩子都需要学习所有位置，而且会从中受益。在街头足球中，你会在特定时刻担任自己最能胜任的角色。这项角色安排可以每场比赛都发生变化，但目的始终相同：在任何时刻都充分发挥每个人的长处。

FIn street soccer, children have to learn patience, to wait their turn, to realize that they are not entitled to make decisions, or even be listened to simply because they show up. Positions of responsibility are earned through competition within the team. Younger players in street soccer must wait to attain those positions. In youth soccer, however, with its overly democratic values, youngsters are guaranteed their time in the spotlight. Whether it’s their turn to be captain, to play a central position or to take a crucial shot, youth soccer players come to believe that hard work and patience aren’t really necessary.

在街头足球中，孩子必须学会耐心，等待轮到自己，还要明白，不能仅仅因为自己来了，就理所当然地认为自己有权作决定，甚至有权让别人听取自己的意见。负有责任的位置要靠队内竞争赢得，年幼的街头球员必须等待，直到自己能够获得这些位置。然而，青少年足球的价值观过于民主，确保年轻人都能拥有受瞩目的时刻。无论轮到他们当队长、踢中路位置还是完成关键一射，青少年球员都会逐渐相信，努力和耐心其实并非必需。

GNot only does every youth soccer player get a chance, it is assumed that each individual has played well. ‘Everyone’s a winner; no one’s a loser’ is a guiding principle of youth soccer. This ensures each individual goes away positive about themselves. No one can leave a game or a practice feeling bad. But, if there really are no losers, then why try at all? Since giving less than your best receives the same reward as giving your best, why go to any extra effort?

每个青少年球员不仅都会获得机会，人们还会认定每个人都踢得很好。“人人都是赢家，没有人是输家”是青少年足球的一项指导原则。这样可以确保每个人离开时都对自己抱有正面看法，谁也不能在赛后或训练后心情低落。但如果真的没有输家，又何必努力？既然不尽全力与全力以赴得到的奖励相同，为什么还要多下功夫？

In street soccer, every game results in a winner and a loser and everyone knows who is who. Losing a game is a common experience and players learn early on how to handle this. As a result, unlike most youth soccer players, they acquire resilience.

街头足球的每场比赛都有赢家和输家，每个人都知道谁胜谁负。输球是一种常见经历，球员很早就学会如何应对。因此，与大多数青少年足球运动员不同，他们培养出了韧性。

A further difference between these two strands of soccer is that in street soccer a formal record is not kept. You can lose one day and win the next. The results are only temporary and are forgotten within minutes of the end of the match. But in organized youth soccer, the position each person plays and the results are formally noted and maintained throughout a season.

这两类足球的另一个差别在于，街头足球不保留正式记录。你可以今天输，明天赢；比赛结果只是暂时的，终场几分钟后便会被遗忘。但在有组织的青少年足球中，每个人踢的位置和比赛结果都会正式记下，并在整个赛季中保存。
