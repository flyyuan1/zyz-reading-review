Research shows that our olfactory sense is more influential than we realise

研究表明，嗅觉对我们的影响比我们意识到的更大

ADogs’ noses are renowned for sensitivity to smells, while human noses are thought to be poor by comparison, yet that might be a misconception. According to recent studies, our noses are in fact acutely sensitive instruments that guide our everyday lives to a surprising extent. Subtle smells can change your mood, behaviour and the choices you make, often without you realising it. Our own odours, meanwhile, indicate emotional states such as fear or sadness to those around us. The big mystery is why we aren’t more aware of our nasal activity. Noses have certainly never been at the forefront of sensory research, and were pushed aside until recently in favour of the seemingly more vital senses of vision and hearing. ‘There has been a lot of prejudice that people are not that influenced by olfactory stimuli, especially compared to other mammals,’ says Lilianne Mujica-Parodi, who studies the neurobiology of human stress at Stony Brook University in New York, in the United States.

狗鼻子对气味的敏感性广为人知，相比之下，人们认为人类鼻子的嗅觉很差，但这或许是一种误解。近期研究显示，我们的鼻子其实是极其敏锐的感知工具，对日常生活的引导作用大得出人意料。细微的气味能够改变人的情绪、行为和所作选择，而当事人往往毫无察觉。与此同时，我们自身的气味也会向周围的人传达恐惧或悲伤等情绪状态。最大的谜团在于，我们为何没有更清楚地意识到自身的嗅觉活动。鼻子从来都不是感觉研究的重点；直到最近，它还一直让位于视觉和听觉，因为后两者看似更加重要。研究人类压力神经生物学的 Lilianne Mujica-Parodi 任职于美国纽约石溪大学（Stony Brook University）。她说：“人们长期抱有偏见，认为嗅觉刺激对人类的影响没有那么大，尤其是与其他哺乳动物相比时。”

BOne of the first people to assert the relative unimportance of human smelling was Pierre Paul Broca, an influential 19th-century anatomist. After comparing the proportion of the brain devoted to smell in different animals, he suggested that mammals can be classed into two broad groups: macrosmatic mammals, such as dogs, have a finely tuned sense of smell which they rely on to perceive the world, while we, along with other primates and the marine mammals, are microsmatic – we have small olfactory organs that we only rely on to a small extent. That idea seemed to fit with more recent studies which found that the majority of mammals have genes coding for about 1000 different types of smell receptor. Most of these genes aren’t expressed in humans, giving our noses just 400 different types of receptor.

最早主张人类嗅觉相对不重要的人之一，是19世纪颇具影响力的解剖学家 Pierre Paul Broca。他比较了不同动物大脑中用于嗅觉的部分所占比例，提出哺乳动物大致可以分为两类：狗等嗅觉发达型动物（macrosmatic mammals）拥有十分敏锐的嗅觉，并依靠它感知世界；而人类与其他灵长类动物和海洋哺乳动物一样，属于嗅觉弱化型（microsmatic），嗅觉器官较小，对它的依赖程度也很低。这一观点似乎与较新的研究结果吻合：大多数哺乳动物都有编码约1,000种不同嗅觉受体的基因。人类体内的大部分此类基因并不表达，因此我们的鼻子只有400种不同受体。

CYet these findings may have been misleading. Brain scans now show that more of the brain is devoted to smell processing than Broca’s anatomical studies suggested. And although we may have fewer types of receptor than other mammals, Charles Greer at Yale University in the United States has shown that the human nose and brain are unusually well connected, with each group of receptors linking to many more neural regions than is the case in other animals. That should give us a good ability to process incoming scents. Once researchers began looking, they found the nose to be far more sensitive than its reputation suggested. One study, for example, found that we can detect certain chemicals diluted in water to less than one part per billion. That means that a person can detect just a few drops of a strong smell like ethyl mercaptan in an Olympic-sized pool.

不过，这些发现可能具有误导性。如今的脑部扫描显示，大脑中用于处理嗅觉的部分比 Broca 的解剖学研究所表明的更多。美国耶鲁大学（Yale University）的 Charles Greer 还证明，尽管人类的受体种类可能少于其他哺乳动物，但人类鼻子与大脑之间的连接异常紧密：每组受体连接的神经区域远多于其他动物。这应当使我们具备良好的气味处理能力。研究人员一旦开始仔细考察，就发现鼻子的敏感程度远远超过人们原先的认识。例如，一项研究发现，我们能够检测出水中稀释至十亿分之一以下浓度的某些化学物质。也就是说，在奥运会标准泳池中，只需加入几滴乙硫醇（ethyl mercaptan）这样气味强烈的物质，人就能察觉。

D‘We are also exceptionally gifted at telling smells apart, even in the case of two molecules whose only difference is that their structures are mirror images of one another. That is fantastic sensitivity,’ says George Dodd, a perfumer and researcher at the olfaction group of the University of Warwick, in the United Kingdom. What’s more, it’s becoming clear that the brain’s olfactory centres are intimately linked to its limbic system, which is involved in emotion, fear and memory. That suggests a link between smell and the way we think.

“我们分辨气味的能力也非同寻常，即使两个分子的唯一区别只是结构互为镜像，我们也能区分。这种敏感度令人惊叹，”英国华威大学（University of Warwick）嗅觉研究组的调香师兼研究人员 George Dodd 说。此外，越来越明显的是，大脑的嗅觉中枢与边缘系统（limbic system）紧密相连，而边缘系统参与情绪、恐惧和记忆活动。这提示嗅觉与我们的思维方式之间可能存在联系。

EThe power of smell will be no news to estate agents, who often advocate the smell of baking bread or brewing coffee to promote the sale of a house. But there are more subtle and surprising effects too. When Hendrik Schifferstein and colleagues, from Delft University of Technology in the Netherlands, pumped the smell of orange, seawater or peppermint into a nightclub, the revellers danced more, rated their night as more enjoyable, and even thought the music was better than when there was no added scent. Meanwhile, Rob Holland, of the University of Utrecht in the Netherlands, found that the hint of aroma wafting out of a hidden bucket of citrus-scented cleaner was enough to persuade students in a hostel to clean up after themselves.

嗅觉的力量对房产经纪人来说并不新鲜，他们常建议用烤面包或煮咖啡的香味来促进房屋销售。不过，气味还有一些更加微妙、出人意料的作用。荷兰代尔夫特理工大学（Delft University of Technology）的 Hendrik Schifferstein 及其同事把橙子、海水或薄荷的气味送入一家夜总会，结果人们跳舞更多，认为当晚更加愉快，甚至觉得音乐也比没有添加气味时更好。与此同时，荷兰乌得勒支大学（University of Utrecht）的 Rob Holland 发现，一桶藏起来的柑橘香清洁剂散发出的淡淡气味，就足以促使宿舍里的学生收拾自己留下的东西。

FOther work has found that scent can influence our cognitive skills. A study by William Overman and colleagues at the University of North Carolina in the United States found that when men were subjected to a novel smell – either good or bad – during a task used to test decision-making skills, they performed significantly worse than normal. The researchers conclude the scent stimulated brain areas connected with emotion, making their decisions emotional rather than rational.

其他研究发现，气味还会影响认知能力。美国北卡罗来纳大学（University of North Carolina）的 William Overman 及其同事开展了一项研究：男性在进行一项测试决策能力的任务时，如果闻到一种陌生气味，无论好闻还是难闻，表现都会显著低于正常水平。研究人员得出结论，气味刺激了与情绪有关的脑区，使他们的决定偏向感性，而非理性。

GSmells are especially good memory evokers, but it is actually a myth that odours trigger more detailed memories than other stimuli. ‘The memory is not more accurate and you don’t remember more details,’ says Yaara Yeshurun at the Weizmann Institute of Science in Rehovot, Israel, ‘but it is unique in that it is more emotional.’ This isn’t surprising when you consider that there are certain brain areas dedicated to both emotion and olfaction, such as the amygdala, and there is a strong link between emotion and memory. In 2009, Yeshurun found that the link between a memory and a smell is stronger if the smell is unpleasant rather than pleasant. She also discovered that the very first time we attach a smell to an object, it evokes a much greater response in our brains than for any subsequent encounter with the smell or object, laying down stronger foundations for the memory. That doesn’t happen with any other sense. Since those first encounters with a smell would have happened at a young age, this explains why smells often transport us back to our childhood.

气味特别容易唤起记忆，但认为气味比其他刺激能触发更详细记忆的说法其实是误区。以色列雷霍沃特（Rehovot）魏茨曼科学研究所（Weizmann Institute of Science）的 Yaara Yeshurun 说：“记忆并不会更准确，人们也不会想起更多细节；其独特之处在于，它带有更强烈的情绪。”考虑到杏仁核（amygdala）等某些脑区同时参与情绪和嗅觉活动，而且情绪与记忆密切相关，这一点并不奇怪。2009年，Yeshurun 发现，如果气味令人不快，它与记忆之间的联系会比气味宜人时更强。她还发现，我们第一次把某种气味与某个物体联系起来时，大脑产生的反应远大于后来任何一次再次接触这种气味或物体时的反应，因而为记忆打下更牢固的基础。其他任何感官都不会出现这种情况。人们最初接触某种气味时通常年纪尚小，这就解释了为什么气味经常把我们带回童年。
