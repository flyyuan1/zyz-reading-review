Insect-inspired robots

仿昆虫机器人

A recent conference reports on developments in biorobotics

近期一场会议报告了仿生机器人技术的发展

AA tiny insect navigates its way across featureless salt-pans. A cockroach successfully works out how to scramble over an obstacle. The mantis shrimp scans its aquatic world through hyperspectral eyes. Using the most basic of equipment and brains tinier than a pin-head, insects constantly solve complex problems of movement, vision and navigation – processing data that would challenge a super-computer. How they do it is driving one of the most exciting new fields of technology – biomimetics and biorobotics, the imitation of insect systems to control man-made machines. Delegates at a recent conference presented some outcomes of their work in this area.

一只小小的昆虫在毫无明显地标的盐碱地上辨路前行。一只蟑螂成功想出越过障碍的方法。螳螂虾（mantis shrimp）用高光谱眼睛扫视水下世界。昆虫只凭最基本的身体装置和比针头还小的大脑，却不断解决运动、视觉和导航方面的复杂问题，处理的数据甚至会令超级计算机为难。它们究竟如何做到这一点，正在推动一个最令人振奋的新技术领域：仿生学与仿生机器人技术（biomimetics and biorobotics），也就是模仿昆虫系统来控制人造机器。近期一场会议的与会者展示了他们在这一领域取得的部分成果。

BDr Alex Zelinsky suggested that the method by which wasps use landmarks to find their way back to the nest may one day be part of a system for navigating cars that know where to go. A research team led by Dr Zelinsky has shown that a robot can navigate its way among 50 different landmarks by recognising them individually using a panoramic camera. “The inspiration came from biology, where wasps use a practical method—turn back and look—to orient themselves as they emerge from their nest. By flying to and fro, they look at the wasp nest from different angles and perspectives so they can recognise it again,” he explained. The robot’s panoramic camera logs the surrounding area and its key landmarks, which are then stored in its computer according to how reliable they are as navigational aids. The landmarks are then scaled, from small to large, so that the robot can recognise whether it is getting closer to or further away from them. Their location is built into a map in its mind, which operates at different scales and instructs the robot whether to turn left or right at a particular mark. The technology provides a general way for a machine to navigate an unknown landscape.

Alex Zelinsky 博士提出，黄蜂利用地标寻找归巢路线的方法，将来或许会成为一种汽车导航系统的一部分，让汽车知道该往哪里走。Zelinsky 博士领导的研究团队已经证明，一个机器人可以用全景相机逐一识别50个不同地标，并在其间导航。他解释说：“灵感来自生物界。黄蜂离巢时会采用一种实用方法：转身回望，以确定方向。它们来回飞行，从不同角度和视点观察蜂巢，以便再次认出它。”机器人的全景相机会记录周围区域及其主要地标，再按照这些地标作为导航参照的可靠程度，把它们存入计算机。随后，系统会把地标按从小到大的比例呈现，让机器人判断自己是在接近还是远离地标。各地标的位置被纳入机器人脑中的地图；这张地图会以不同尺度运作，并在特定标记处指示机器人左转还是右转。这项技术为机器在陌生地形中导航提供了一种通用方法。

CFor three decades, Professor Ruediger Wehner has journeyed from Switzerland to the Sahara Desert where Cataglyphis, a tiny ant with a brain weighing just 0.1 mg, performs acts of navigational genius when it leaves its nest, forages for food and returns successfully. Cataglyphis uses polarised light, caused when air molecules scatter light, to orient and steer itself. Wehner’s team found that the ant has a set of specialised photoreceptors along the upper rim of its eyes that detect polarised light, while other receptors perform different navigational tasks. As the sun moves, the ant notes its direction each time it leaves the nest and updates its internal compass. Using other eye receptors it stores a snapshot image of landmarks close to the nest entrance in its eyes and compares this with what it sees as it returns. The ant also has a way of measuring distance travelled, while a path integrator periodically informs the ant of its current position relative to its point of departure. Rather than integrate all the information it receives in its brain, the ant actually performs a number of complex calculations in different organs. Like a super-computer, the ant has many separate sub-routines going on simultaneously. Using the ant’s ability to steer by polarised light and to store and reuse landscape images, Wehner and colleagues have built “Sahabot,” a small vehicle that uses polarisers and a CCD camera to store 360° images of its surroundings. It navigates by using polarised sunlight and comparing the current images of landmarks to the ones in its memory.

三十年来，Ruediger Wehner 教授一次次从瑞士前往撒哈拉沙漠（Sahara Desert）。在那里，一种名为 Cataglyphis、脑重仅0.1毫克的小蚂蚁离巢觅食后能顺利返回，展现出非凡的导航本领。空气分子散射光线会产生偏振光（polarised light），Cataglyphis 便利用这种光来辨别方向并控制行进路线。Wehner 的团队发现，这种蚂蚁眼睛上缘有一组专门探测偏振光的感光细胞，其他感受器则负责不同的导航任务。随着太阳移动，蚂蚁每次离巢都会记下太阳的方向，并更新体内的指南针。它还会用其他眼部感受器，把巢穴入口附近地标的快照储存在眼中，返回时再与眼前所见进行比较。蚂蚁也能测量已经走过的距离，而路径积分器（path integrator）会定期告知它当前相对于出发点的位置。蚂蚁并非在大脑中整合收到的全部信息，而是在不同器官中分别完成多项复杂计算。它就像一台超级计算机，同时运行着许多彼此独立的子程序。Wehner 及其同事利用蚂蚁凭偏振光确定方向、储存并重复利用地貌图像的能力，造出了小型车辆“Sahabot”。它使用偏振片和 CCD 相机储存周围环境的360度图像，并利用偏振阳光导航，同时把当前地标图像与记忆中的图像进行比较。

DProfessor Robert Michelson had a different desert challenge – to design a flying robot that can not only navigate but also stay aloft and hover in the thin atmosphere of Mars. Drawing inspiration from insect flight, he has gone beyond nature to devise a completely new concept for a flying machine. The “Entomopter” is a sort of double-ended dragonfly whose wings beat reciprocally. Michelson says that the flapping-wing design gives the craft unusually high lift compared with a fixed-wing flyer, enabling it to fly slowly or hover in the thin Martian air – whereas a fixed-wing craft would have to move at more than 400 km/h and could not stop to explore.

Robert Michelson 教授面对的是另一项沙漠挑战：设计一种飞行机器人，使其不仅能够导航，还能在火星稀薄的大气中保持飞行并悬停。他从昆虫飞行中汲取灵感，又超越自然界，构想出一种全新的飞行器。“Entomopter”像一只双头蜻蜓，翅膀交替拍动。Michelson 称，与固定翼飞行器相比，这种扑翼设计能产生异常大的升力，使飞行器可在稀薄的火星大气中缓慢飞行或悬停；固定翼飞行器却必须以每小时400公里以上的速度飞行，无法停下来探索。

EEngineer Roger Quinn and entomologist Professor Roy Ritzmann are taking their inspiration from cockroaches. According to Quinn and Ritzmann, the ability of cockroaches to run very fast over rough terrain may one day give rise to a completely new all-terrain vehicle with six legs, or maybe even wheel-like legs called “whegs.” The key to the cockroach’s remarkable cross-country performance lies partly in the fact that its legs do a lot of the thinking without having to consult the brain. Quinn and Ritzmann are drawing on cockroach skills to create robotic walkers and control strategies that capture the remarkable capacity of these insects to traverse complex terrain and navigate safely toward goals while avoiding obstacles. The team has already designed a series of robots that run on six legs or on whegs, enabling them to handle surprisingly rugged terrain.

工程师 Roger Quinn 和昆虫学家 Roy Ritzmann 教授从蟑螂身上获取灵感。据 Quinn 和 Ritzmann 所说，蟑螂能在崎岖地面上飞快奔跑，这种能力将来或许会催生一种全新的全地形车辆，它可能有六条腿，甚至采用名为“轮足”（whegs）的轮状腿。蟑螂出色的越野能力，部分关键在于它的腿不必请示大脑就能自行完成大量“思考”。Quinn 和 Ritzmann 正借鉴蟑螂的本领，开发步行机器人和控制策略，以再现这种昆虫穿越复杂地形、避开障碍并安全驶向目标的非凡能力。团队已经设计出一系列靠六条腿或轮足行进的机器人，使它们能够应付异常崎岖的地形。

FInternational experts believe there are tremendous opportunities in biorobotics. However, delegates at the conference had differing visions for the future of the science. While some were concerned that the initial applications of biorobotics may be military, others, such as Dr Barbara Webb, predicted swarms of tiny, cheap, insect-like robots as society’s cleaners and collectors. Sonja Kleinlogel hoped the study of the hyperspectral eyes of the mantis shrimp might yield remote sensors that can watch over the environmental health of our oceans. Several delegates were concerned about the ethical implications of biorobotics and urged that close attention be paid to this as the science and technologies develop.

国际专家认为，仿生机器人技术蕴藏着巨大的机遇。不过，与会者对这门科学的未来抱有不同设想。一些人担心仿生机器人技术最初可能用于军事领域；另一些人则作出不同预测，例如 Barbara Webb 博士设想，会有成群结队、价格低廉的微型仿昆虫机器人充当社会中的清洁工和收集员。Sonja Kleinlogel 希望，对螳螂虾高光谱眼睛的研究能够催生远程传感器，用来监测海洋的环境健康状况。几位与会者对仿生机器人技术涉及的伦理问题表示担忧，并敦促人们在这门科学及其技术发展的过程中密切关注这些问题。
