The first plastics were developed as a substitute for natural rubber. Chemically, rubber is a polymer—a compound containing large molecules that are formed by the bonding of many smaller, simpler units, repeated over and over again. The same bonding principle—polymerization—is the basis of the creation of a huge range of plastics by the chemical industry.

最早的塑料是作为天然橡胶的替代品开发出来的。从化学角度看，橡胶是一种聚合物，也就是由许多较小、较简单的单元反复连接，形成大分子的化合物。同样的连接原理——聚合反应——也是化学工业制造种类繁多的塑料的基础。

The first plastic was developed as a result of a competition in the USA. In the 1860s, $10,000 was offered to anybody who could replace ivory—supplies of which were declining—with something equally good as a material for making billiard balls. The prize was won by John Wesley Hyatt, with a material called celluloid. Celluloid was made by dissolving cellulose, a carbohydrate obtained from plants, in a solution of camphor dissolved in ethanol. This new material rapidly found other applications in the manufacture of everyday products such as knife handles and detachable collars and cuffs. But perhaps the best-known celluloid product was photographic film, without which the film industry could never have taken off at the end of the 19th century.

第一种塑料源自美国的一场竞赛。19世纪60年代，由于象牙供应日益减少，有人悬赏$10,000，希望找到一种性能同样出色的材料来替代象牙制造台球。John Wesley Hyatt 凭借一种名为赛璐珞（celluloid）的材料赢得了奖金。赛璐珞的制作方法是把从植物中取得的碳水化合物——纤维素——溶解在由樟脑和乙醇配成的溶液中。这种新材料很快被用于制造其他日常用品，例如刀柄以及可拆卸的衣领和袖口。不过，最广为人知的赛璐珞制品或许是摄影胶片；若没有它，电影业不可能在19世纪末兴起。

Celluloid can be repeatedly softened and reshaped by heat, and is known as a thermoplastic. In 1907, Leo Baekeland (1863–1944), a Belgian chemist working in the USA, invented a different kind of plastic by causing phenol and formaldehyde to react together. Baekeland called it Bakelite, and it was the first of the thermosets—plastics that can be cast and moulded while hot, but cannot be softened by heat and reshaped once they have set. Bakelite was a good insulator, and was resistant to water and acid. With these properties it was soon being used in the manufacture of electrical switches as well as a variety of domestic items.

赛璐珞可以通过加热反复软化并重新塑形，因此属于热塑性塑料。1907年，在美国工作的比利时化学家 Leo Baekeland（1863—1944）让苯酚与甲醛发生反应，发明了另一种塑料。Baekeland 把这种材料命名为胶木（Bakelite），也就是第一种热固性塑料。热固性塑料受热时可以浇铸成形，但一旦固化，就无法再靠加热软化并重新塑形。胶木绝缘性能良好，而且耐水、耐酸。凭借这些特性，它很快被用于制造电气开关和各种家居用品。

As the century went on, the range of newly developed plastics increased. Chemists began looking for other small molecules that could be strung together to make polymers. In the 1930s, chemists in Britain discovered that the gas ethylene would polymerize under heat and pressure to form a thermoplastic they called polythene. Polypropylene followed in the 1950s. Both are used to make bottles, pipes and plastic bags. A small change in the starting material—replacing a hydrogen atom in ethylene with a chlorine atom—produced rigid PVC (polyvinyl chloride), a fireproof plastic suitable for drains and gutters. By adding certain chemicals, a soft form of PVC can be produced, suitable as a substitute for rubber in items such as waterproof clothing. A closely related plastic is Teflon or PTFE (polytetrafluoroethylene). It produces very little friction, making it ideal for products such as non-stick frying pans.

随着20世纪向前发展，新开发的塑料种类不断增加。化学家开始寻找其他可以串联起来形成聚合物的小分子。20世纪30年代，英国化学家发现，乙烯气体在加热和加压条件下会发生聚合，形成一种他们称为聚乙烯（polythene）的热塑性塑料。20世纪50年代，聚丙烯（polypropylene）也随之问世。这两种材料都用于制造瓶子、管道和塑料袋。只要略微改变起始材料——把乙烯中的一个氢原子换成氯原子——就能制成硬质PVC（聚氯乙烯）；这种塑料防火，适合制作排水管和檐沟。加入某些化学物质后，还可以制成软质PVC，用于替代防水服等物品中的橡胶。与之关系密切的另一种塑料是 Teflon，也称 PTFE（聚四氟乙烯）。它产生的摩擦很小，非常适合用于不粘煎锅等产品。

Polystyrene, a hard, clear material like glass, was developed during the 1930s in Germany, and its applications included food containers and toys. Expanded polystyrene is rigid and is widely used in packaging and insulation. Polyurethane, developed in the same country, was commonly produced as a foam, which was very useful in the production of insulating materials.

20世纪30年代，德国开发出聚苯乙烯（polystyrene）。这是一种像玻璃一样坚硬、透明的材料，可用于制作食品容器和玩具。发泡聚苯乙烯质地坚硬，广泛用于包装和隔热。在同一个国家开发出的聚氨酯（polyurethane）通常制成泡沫，对生产隔热材料非常有用。

In the 1930s, the first of the man-made fibres was created—nylon. Its inventor was a chemist called Wallace Carothers (1896–1937), who worked for the Du Pont company in the USA. He found that under the right conditions two particular chemicals would form a polymer that could be pumped out through holes and then stretched to form long glossy threads that could be woven like silk. Its first use was to make parachutes for the US armed forces in World War II. In the postwar years, it completely replaced silk in the manufacture of stockings.

20世纪30年代，第一种人造纤维——尼龙——诞生了。它的发明者是美国杜邦公司（Du Pont）的一名化学家 Wallace Carothers（1896—1937）。他发现，在适当条件下，两种特定化学物质会形成一种聚合物。这种聚合物可以从小孔中挤出，再拉伸成长而有光泽的细丝，像丝绸一样织成布料。尼龙最初用于制作第二次世界大战期间美国军队使用的降落伞。战后，它在长袜制造中完全取代了丝绸。

Many other synthetic fibres joined nylon, including Orlon, Acrilan, and Terylene. Today most garments are made of a blend of natural fibres, such as cotton and wool, and man-made fibres that make fabrics easier to look after.

继尼龙之后，又出现了许多其他合成纤维，包括 Orlon、Acrilan 和 Terylene。如今，大多数服装都由天然纤维与人造纤维混纺而成；天然纤维包括棉和羊毛，而人造纤维能让织物更容易打理。

Despite its enormous usefulness, plastic has its drawbacks. In fact, one of its great strengths—its indestructibility—is its greatest disadvantage. Beaches all over the world, even on the remotest island, are littered with plastic bottles that nothing can destroy. Nor is it very easy to recycle plastics, as different types of plastic are often found in the same items and call for different treatments.

塑料虽然用途极广，却也有缺点。事实上，它的一大优势——不易毁坏——恰恰也是它最大的弊端。世界各地的海滩上，甚至最偏远岛屿的海滩上，都散落着任何东西都无法毁坏的塑料瓶。塑料也并不容易回收，因为同一件物品中往往含有不同种类的塑料，而它们需要采用不同的处理方法。

Plastics can be made biodegradable by incorporating into their structure a material such as starch, which is attacked by bacteria and causes the plastic to fall apart. Other materials can be incorporated that gradually decay in sunlight—although bottles made of such materials have to be stored in the dark, to ensure they do not disintegrate before they have been used.

在塑料结构中加入淀粉等材料，可以使塑料具有生物降解性。细菌会侵蚀淀粉，从而使塑料解体。还可以加入会在阳光下逐渐分解的其他材料，不过，用这类材料制成的瓶子必须存放在黑暗处，以免还没使用就已经分解。
