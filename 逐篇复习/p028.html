The Blockbuster Phenomenon

“重磅展览”现象

Museums in Australia, like other pleasure-giving public organizations, are adapting their activities so that they more closely reflect the marketplace.

澳大利亚的博物馆和其他给公众带来乐趣的机构一样，正在调整自己的活动，使其更贴近市场。

ASince the 1980s, the term ‘blockbuster’ has become the fashionable word for spectacular, high-profile museum exhibitions that have the ability to attract large crowds. A blockbuster is a ‘large-scale loan exhibition that people who normally don’t go to museums will stand in line for hours to see’ (Eisen 1984). Once the museum that created the exhibition has shown it to its local market, it can be offered to other organizations for a fee. This means that you can boost your own door takings and make money from boosting someone else’s door takings.

自20世纪80年代以来，“blockbuster”一词开始流行起来，用来指场面壮观、备受瞩目、能够吸引大批观众的博物馆展览。重磅展览是“一种大型借展，连平时不去博物馆的人也愿意排上几小时队观看”（Eisen，1984）。策展博物馆先向本地市场展出，之后便可以收费提供给其他机构。这意味着，博物馆既能通过增加自己的门票收入获益，也能通过帮助其他机构增加门票收入来赚钱。

BWhile partaking of the excitement of the blockbuster, visitors thus lured are likely to stay longer at the museum. Betty Churcher, when Director of the Australian National Gallery, summed up the new blockbuster creed as follows: ‘The bonus of the blockbuster exhibitions is that people come to see the blockbuster and they stay to look at the permanent collection, so you are getting broader exposure for your collection.’

被重磅展览的热闹气氛吸引而来的观众，很可能会在博物馆停留更长时间。Betty Churcher 担任澳大利亚国家美术馆（Australian National Gallery）馆长时，曾这样概括重磅展览的新信条：“重磅展览的额外好处是，人们为了重磅展览而来，却会留下来观看常设藏品，因此能让更多人看到你的藏品。”

CMuseums across the UK, USA, Canada and Australia currently operate under a system of plural funding: revenue raised through contributions by federal, state and/or local governments, combined with revenue raised through admission charges and other activities. Maintaining and increasing visitor levels is thus paramount and involves not only creating or hiring blockbuster exhibitions, but providing regular exhibition changes and innovations. In addition, the visiting public have become known as customers rather than visitors, and the skills that are valued in museums to keep the new customers coming through the door have changed. Curators are now administrators, and being a museum director no longer requires an Arts degree—but public relations skills are essential if the museum is going to compete with other museums to stage travelling exhibitions which draw huge crowds.

英国、美国、加拿大和澳大利亚各地的博物馆目前采用多元筹资体系：联邦、州和地方政府中一个或多个层级提供的资金，与门票及其他活动带来的收入结合起来。因此，维持并增加参观人数至关重要；这不仅需要创办或租入重磅展览，还需要定期更换展览并不断创新。此外，前来参观的公众开始被称为“顾客”，而不再是“参观者”；为了让这些新顾客不断走进博物馆，馆方所看重的技能也发生了变化。策展人如今成了管理人员，担任博物馆馆长也不再要求具备人文学科学位；不过，如果博物馆要与其他博物馆竞争举办吸引大批人群的巡回展览，公共关系技能必不可少。

DThe convergence of museums, the heritage industry, tourism, profit-making and pleasure-giving has resulted in the new ‘museology’. This has given rise to much debate about whether it is appropriate to see museums primarily as tourist attractions. In literature from both the UK and USA, the words that are starting to appear in some descriptions of blockbusters are ‘less scholarly’, ‘non-elitist’ and ‘popularist’, while others extol the virtues of encouraging scholars to co-operate on projects, and to provide exhibitions that cater for a broad selection of the community rather than an elite sector. Whatever commentators may think, managers of museums worldwide are looking for artful ways to blend culture and commerce, and blockbuster exhibitions are at the top of the list.

博物馆、文化遗产产业、旅游业、营利活动和娱乐活动逐渐融合，催生了新的“博物馆学”。这也引发了大量争论：把博物馆主要视为旅游景点是否合适？英国和美国的相关文献中，有些描述重磅展览时开始使用“学术性较弱”“非精英化”和“通俗化”等词；另一些文献则赞扬鼓励学者开展项目合作、让展览服务于广泛社会群体而非精英阶层的好处。不论评论者怎么想，世界各地的博物馆管理者都在寻找巧妙融合文化与商业的方法，而重磅展览位居首选。

EBut do blockbusters held in public institutions really create a surplus to fund other activities? If the bottom line is profit, then according to the records of many major museums blockbusters do make money. For museums in some countries, it may be the money that they require to replace parts of their collections or to fix buildings that are in need of attention. For some museums in Australia, it may be the opportunity to illustrate that they are attempting to pay their way by recovering part of their operating costs. Also, creating or hiring a blockbuster has many positive spin-offs: blockbusters mean crowds, and crowds are good for the local economy, providing increased trade for shops, hotels, restaurants, the transport industry and retailers. The argument that the arts provide sustained economic benefits has been well illustrated in impact studies in the USA and UK.

然而，在公共机构举办的重磅展览，真的能产生盈余来资助其他活动吗？如果最终标准是利润，那么根据许多大型博物馆的记录，重磅展览确实能赚钱。对一些国家的博物馆来说，这笔钱可能是更新部分藏品或修缮需要维护的建筑所必需的。对澳大利亚的一些博物馆而言，这可能也是一个机会，用来表明它们正试图收回部分运营成本，靠自身努力承担开支。此外，创办或租入重磅展览会产生许多积极的连带效应：重磅展览会带来人群，而人群有利于当地经济，可以增加商店、酒店、餐馆、交通业和零售商的生意。美国和英国的影响研究已经充分说明，艺术能够带来持续的经济效益。

FHowever, blockbusters require large capital expenditure and draw on resources across all branches of an organization, and the costs don’t end there. There is a Human Resource Management cost in addition to a measurable ‘real’ dollar cost. Receiving a touring exhibition draws resources from across functional management structures in a project-management style. Everyone—from general labourers to building services, front-of-house, technical, promotional, educational and administrative staff—is required to perform additional tasks. Furthermore, as an increasing number of institutions try their hand at increasing visitor numbers and memberships (and therefore revenue) by staging blockbuster exhibitions, it may be less likely that blockbusters will continue to provide a surplus to subsidize other activities due to the competitive nature of the market.

不过，重磅展览需要投入大量资金，也会占用机构所有部门的资源，而且成本还不止这些。除了可以用实际金额衡量的成本，还存在人力资源管理成本。接待巡回展览需要采用项目管理方式，从各个职能管理部门调动资源。从普通工人，到楼宇服务、前台接待、技术、宣传、教育和行政人员，所有人都必须承担额外任务。此外，越来越多的机构开始尝试举办重磅展览，以增加参观人数和会员数量，从而提高收入；由于市场竞争激烈，重磅展览今后可能越来越难以继续产生盈余，补贴其他活动。

GIt has been illustrated in both the UK and USA that the blockbuster ideology has resulted in the false expectation that the momentum required to stage blockbusters can be maintained continually. Creating, mounting or hiring blockbusters is exhausting, with the real costs throughout an institution difficult to calculate. Secondly, as some analysts have argued, the ‘shopkeeping’ mentality and cost-benefit analysis, and a pure concentration on the bottom line, can squeeze substance out of an exhibition. Taking out substance can be a recipe for blockbuster failure and therefore financial failure.

英国和美国的情况都说明，重磅展览理念制造了一种错误预期：人们以为举办重磅展览所需的势头可以一直保持下去。创办、布置或租入重磅展览令人精疲力竭，而且整个机构承担的实际成本很难计算。其次，正如一些分析人士所说，“开店做生意”的心态、成本效益分析，以及一味关注最终利润，都可能使展览的实质内容减少。失去实质内容可能导致重磅展览失败，进而在经济上遭受损失。

HPerhaps the best pathway to take is one that balances both blockbusters and regular exhibitions. However, this easy middle ground may only work if you have enough space, and have alternative sources of funding to continue to support the regular, less exciting fare. Perhaps the advice should be to make sure that you find out what your local community wants from you and make sure that your regular activities and exhibitions are more enduring.

或许，最佳道路是在重磅展览和常规展览之间取得平衡。不过，这条看似轻松的中间路线，可能只有在场地足够、同时还有其他资金来源可以持续支持较普通、吸引力较小的常规内容时才行得通。也许更合适的建议是：务必弄清当地社区希望从博物馆得到什么，并确保日常活动和展览能够更加持久。
