Professor of anthropology Pat Shipman looks at the ways in which our relationships with animals have shaped our own human evolution.

人类学教授 Pat Shipman 探讨了我们与动物的关系如何塑造人类自身的进化。

Travel anywhere and you see something so common it may not even catch your attention. Wherever there are people, animals are being walked, herded and fed. Many, such as dogs, cats and sheep, are domesticated, but you will also find people living alongside wild creatures like monkeys and wolves. Close contact with animals is not confined to one particular culture, region or ethnic group but is a universal human trait.

无论到哪里旅行，你都会看到一种司空见惯、甚至可能不会引起注意的现象。只要有人居住，就有人遛动物、放牧动物、喂养动物。其中许多动物，如狗、猫和绵羊，已经被驯化；但你也会发现，有些人与猴子、狼等野生动物共同生活。与动物密切接触并不局限于某一种文化、地区或族群，而是人类共有的特征。

On the face of it this makes little sense. No other mammal adopts individuals from another species, with good reason. Since the ultimate prize in evolution is perpetuating your genes, providing food for another species is counterproductive to success. Even if pets offer friendship, that cannot explain why our bond with other species arose in the first place. Who would bring a ferocious wolf into their home in the hope that thousands of years later it would become a loving pet?

乍看之下，这似乎毫无道理。其他哺乳动物都不会收养异种个体，而且不这样做是有充分理由的。由于进化中的终极目标是延续自身基因，为其他物种提供食物反而与这一目标背道而驰。即便宠物能带来陪伴，这也无法解释我们与其他物种的纽带最初为何会出现。谁会把一头凶猛的狼带回家，只因希望几千年后它会变成亲人的宠物呢？

As an anthropologist I have tried to understand this puzzle by looking to the past for the origins of our link with animals. What I found was a long and unbroken evolutionary trail of evidence I call the ‘animal connection’. This trail links to three important developments in human evolution: tool making, language and domestication. If I am correct, and I believe that further research will conclusively show that I am, the animal connection is a hugely significant force instrumental in our global success.

身为人类学家，我一直试图通过回望过去、追寻人类与动物联系的起源来理解这个谜题。我发现的是一条漫长而从未中断的进化证据链，我称之为“动物联系”。这条线索将人类进化中的三项重要发展联系起来：工具制造、语言和驯化。如果我的观点正确——而我相信进一步研究会最终确证这一点——那么动物联系便是一股极其重要的力量，对人类在全球范围内取得成功起到了关键作用。

The trail begins at least 2.6 million years ago. That is when the first tools appear in the archaeological record. This development required the intellectual breakthrough of realizing that the properties of an object can be altered. Man learnt how to turn stone into small, sharp items that could cut up animal carcasses, providing them with an evolutionary shortcut to becoming predators.

这条线索至少始于260万年前，考古记录中最早的工具正是在那时出现。这一发展需要一次认知上的突破，即意识到物体的性质可以改变。人类学会把石头制成小巧锋利、能够切割动物尸体的工具，从而得到一条进化上的捷径，成为捕食者。

There were many consequences of the new hunting lifestyle. On the plus side, eating more meat and fat was a requirement for the increase in relative brain size. And since meat tends to come in bigger packages than leaves and roots, meat eaters could spend less time finding food and more on activities like learning and social interaction. On the minus side, preying on animals put our ancestors into direct competition with the other predators that shared their ecosystem, animals such as lions and hyenas. To get the upper hand, they needed more than just tools, and that is where the animal connection comes in. What made it possible for our ancestors to compete successfully with true carnivores was the ability to pay very close attention to the habits of both potential prey and competitors. Knowledge was power, so we acquired a deep understanding of the minds of other animals. Another significant consequence of becoming more predatory was the need to live at lower densities, as successful predators soon exhaust food supplies. The record of the geographic distribution of our ancestors provides further support for my idea that the animal connection has shaped our evolution. Early man lived in Africa and nowhere else, but then underwent a dramatic expansion in territory, forced by the search for new hunting grounds.

新的狩猎生活方式带来了许多后果。好的一面是，要使大脑相对体积增大，就必须摄入更多肉类和脂肪。而且，肉类通常能一次提供比树叶和根茎更多的食物，因此食肉者可以少花时间寻找食物，把更多时间用于学习和社交等活动。坏的一面是，捕食动物使我们的祖先与生活在同一生态系统中的其他捕食者直接竞争，例如狮子和鬣狗。要占据上风，他们需要的不只是工具，而这正是动物联系发挥作用之处。祖先之所以能够成功与真正的食肉动物竞争，是因为他们能极其仔细地观察潜在猎物和竞争者双方的习性。知识就是力量，因此我们深入理解了其他动物的思维。变得更具捕食性的另一个重大后果，是人类需要以更低的人口密度生活，因为成功的捕食者很快就会耗尽食物供应。祖先地理分布的记录进一步支持了我的观点，即动物联系塑造了我们的进化。早期人类只生活在非洲，后来却因寻找新猎场而被迫大幅扩张领地。

Throughout the period of these enormous changes in the lifestyle of our ancestors, gathering, sharing and recording knowledge became increasingly advantageous, and examples of prehistoric art that appear in Europe, Asia, Africa and Australia allow us to eavesdrop on their conversations. Through the art, we realize the main topic was animals: their colours, shapes, postures and habits. This focus is even more striking considering that scenes of human life are rarely depicted. Pictures of people, social interaction and ceremonies are rare.

在祖先生活方式发生这些巨大变化的整个时期，收集、分享和记录知识变得越来越有利。欧洲、亚洲、非洲和澳大利亚出现的史前艺术，使我们得以像旁听者一样了解他们的交流。通过这些艺术，我们认识到其主要主题是动物：动物的颜色、形态、姿态和习性。考虑到人类生活场景很少被描绘，这种关注就更为引人注目。人物、社会交往和仪式的图像都很少见。

This leads me on to the development of language. Nobody doubts this proved a major adaptive advantage to our ancestors in developing complex behaviours. How it arose, however, remains a mystery. I believe I am the first to propose a continuity between the strong human-animal link that appeared 2.6 million years ago and the origin of language. The complexity and importance of animal-related information, indicated in the predominance of animal images, spurred early humans to move beyond what other species could achieve. Vervet monkeys, for example, have different cries for eagles and snakes, but they cannot discuss what threats might appear tomorrow. The property of full language is that it is comprised of vocabulary and grammatical rules that can be combined in an infinite number of ways to convey fine shades of meaning.

这就把我引向了语言的发展。没有人怀疑，语言在祖先发展复杂行为的过程中是一项重大的适应优势。然而，它如何产生仍是个谜。我相信，我是第一个提出这种延续关系的人：一端是260万年前出现的牢固人类—动物联系，另一端则是语言的起源。动物图像占主导地位，显示出动物相关信息既复杂又重要，这推动早期人类突破其他物种所能达到的水平。例如，黑长尾猴会用不同叫声分别警示鹰和蛇，却无法讨论明天可能出现哪些威胁。完整语言的特性在于，它由词汇和语法规则组成，而二者能以无穷多种方式组合，传达细微的意义差别。

As our ancestors became more involved with animals, the third product of the animal connection appeared: domestication, which has long been linked with the keeping of animals like sheep. Domestic animals are usually considered as ‘walking larders’, or easy food sources, reflecting the idea that the basis of this change was a drive for greater food security. Looking at the origins of domestication for clues to its underlying reasons, I found fundamental flaws in this idea. Instead, my analysis suggests domestication emerged as a natural progression of our close association with, and understanding of, other species. If domestication was about knowing where your next meal was coming from, the first domesticate ought to have been a food source, yet analysis of fossils shows that the domestication of dogs pre-dated that of any other species, even plants, from wolves. Furthermore, if the objective was to have meat to eat, you wouldn’t select an animal that eats two kilograms of meat a day. It was familiarity with this species that led to eventual domestication.

随着祖先与动物的联系日益深入，动物联系的第三项产物——驯化——随之出现；长期以来，人们一直把驯化与饲养绵羊等动物联系在一起。家养动物通常被视为“会走路的食品储藏室”，也就是容易获得的食物来源；这反映出一种观点：促成驯化的动力，是希望食物来源更有保障。我考察了驯化的起源，希望从中找出其深层原因的线索，却发现这种观点存在根本缺陷。相反，我的分析提示，驯化可能是我们与其他物种密切交往并了解它们之后自然发展的结果。如果驯化只是为了知道下一顿饭从哪里来，那么最先被驯化的理应是食物来源；然而化石分析表明，由狼驯化而来的狗早于所有其他物种、甚至植物被驯化。此外，如果目标是获得肉食，人们不会选择一种每天要吃两千克肉的动物。正是人类对这个物种的熟悉最终促成了驯化。
