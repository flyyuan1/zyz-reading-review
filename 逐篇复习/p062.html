早在祖先拥有任何文明的外在标志之前，他们就已对物质商品产生兴趣。Kate Douglas 探究这种古老的痴迷最终把我们带到了何处。

A natural human talent

人类与生俱来的天赋

AHumans are born to trade and we don’t need shops or money to do it—the heart of commerce is an instinctive ability for what anthropologists call “reciprocity”. This is an ability to exchange goods of equal worth, and it evolved as the brains of our ancient ancestors and societies became more complex, allowing individuals to keep a running tally of their interactions with others. Evidence from modern hunter-gatherers leaves little doubt that the exchange of food and favours is innate, as is the ability to keep track of the credits and debits that accrue as a result. Combine this skill for mental book-keeping with even the most basic material culture, and trade inevitably follows.

人类天生会交易，而且不需要商店或货币也能做到。商业的核心，是一种人类学家称为“互惠”（reciprocity）的本能能力。这是一种交换等值物品的能力，随着远古祖先的大脑和社会变得更加复杂而进化形成，使个人能够持续记录自己与他人的往来。现代狩猎采集者提供的证据几乎毫无疑问地表明，交换食物和互相帮忙是与生俱来的，记住由此累积的付出与获得也是如此。把这种心算记账的能力与哪怕最基本的物质文化结合起来，贸易就必然随之出现。

BOnce trade gets off the ground, the economic benefits quickly make it irresistible. But exchanging tools, food and other essentials of life in a barter economy is a far cry from the shopping mall. Modern consumerism extends beyond subsistence and utilitarianism to encompass everything from Gucci handbags and BMW convertibles to valuable paintings. Clearly, the worth of such goods is not inherent but resides in certain intangible qualities that we invest in them. When did humans start holding these goods in such high esteem?

贸易一旦起步，其经济利益很快就会让人难以抗拒。然而，在以物易物的经济中交换工具、食物和其他生活必需品，与购物中心相去甚远。现代消费主义超越了生存需要和实用价值，涵盖从 Gucci 手袋、BMW 敞篷车到名贵画作的一切物品。显然，这些商品的价值并非与生俱来，而是存在于我们赋予它们的某些无形特质之中。人类是从什么时候开始如此看重这些商品的？

Q16 · 正确标题未作答

Recent finds alter theories about when consumerism started

新近发现改变了消费主义起源时间的理论

你的标题

未作答

CAll the archaeological evidence for the flowering of consumer culture has up to now pointed to a date of around 40,000 years ago. That’s when early modern humans started making increasingly intricate bone and stone tools, carving patterns into rocks and creating representational art such as carved figurines, jewellery and cave paintings. However, discoveries in Africa are pushing the origins of consumerism much further back into human prehistory. A few years ago, reports began to emerge of discoveries made at the Blombos Cave, a site of ancient human habitation in South Africa. Christopher Henshilwood and colleagues from the University of Bergen in Norway dated thousands of pieces of ochre from the cave, and many of them proved to be more than 100,000 years old — before the time that early humans moved out of Africa. Ochre, a coloured clay that comes in various shades from red to black, does not occur naturally around Blombos and must have been imported from quarries at least 30 kilometres away, either directly by Blombos residents or through trade. Although ochre can be used to dry and preserve (cure) animal hides, the researchers are convinced the Blombos ochre had a symbolic purpose. For a start, it is predominantly red—any of the other colours available would have done for curing—and the surfaces of the clay had been scraped in a way that indicates they were used to yield pigment for dyes.

迄今为止，有关消费文化兴盛的所有考古证据都指向大约4万年前。正是在那时，早期现代人开始制作日益精巧的骨器和石器，在岩石上雕刻图案，并创作雕像、首饰和洞穴绘画等具象艺术。然而，非洲的发现正把消费主义的起源大幅推向人类史前时期更久远的年代。 几年前，关于南非古人类居住遗址布隆博斯洞穴（Blombos Cave）的发现开始见诸报道。挪威卑尔根大学（University of Bergen）的 Christopher Henshilwood 及其同事测定了洞中数千块赭土的年代，结果表明其中许多已有超过10万年历史，早于早期人类走出非洲的时期。赭土是一种颜色从红到黑、深浅不一的彩色黏土，在布隆博斯附近并非天然存在，必定是从至少30公里外的采石场运来，可能由当地居民直接带回，也可能通过贸易获得。赭土可以用来使动物皮干燥并保存，但研究人员确信，布隆博斯的赭土具有象征用途。首先，这些赭土以红色为主，而用于处理兽皮时，任何一种现有颜色都可以；其次，黏土表面经过刮削，表明人们曾从中取得颜料用于染色。

Q17 · 正确标题未作答

The clues that indicate ancient items were decorative

表明古代物品具有装饰用途的线索

你的标题

未作答

DAn even more intriguing discovery from Blombos was of 41 beads dating from 76,000 years ago, found in clusters and made from the shells of a tiny mollusc. These cannot be natural deposits, argue the researchers, as each cluster contains shells of a similar size and colour with consistently placed holes. What’s more, all the beads display a pattern of wear suggesting friction from rubbing against thread, clothes or other beads. The previous oldest find of beads in Africa dates back to just 45,000 years ago. And it seems the Blombos people’s taste for beautiful items was not an isolated phenomenon. Jessica Thompson of Arizona State University in Tempe has described finding shell fragments from a site in Tanzania that she believes may represent debris from bead manufacture. They are at least 45,000—and possibly 280,000—years old. Although there is no evidence of how the ancient beads were used, their modern counterparts are often traded. So it looks as though our taste for jewellery and art is much older than we thought.

布隆博斯还有一项更耐人寻味的发现：41颗距今7.6万年的珠子。这些珠子成簇出土，由一种微小软体动物的贝壳制成。研究人员认为，它们不可能是自然沉积物，因为每一簇所含贝壳的大小和颜色相近，孔洞位置也始终一致。此外，所有珠子都呈现出一种磨损痕迹，说明它们曾与线、衣物或其他珠子相互摩擦。 此前，非洲发现的最古老珠子只能追溯到4.5万年前。而且，布隆博斯人对美丽物品的喜爱似乎并非孤立现象。位于坦佩（Tempe）的亚利桑那州立大学（Arizona State University）研究人员 Jessica Thompson 曾描述，在坦桑尼亚（Tanzania）一处遗址发现了一些贝壳碎片；她认为这些碎片可能是制作珠子留下的残屑。它们至少有4.5万年历史，甚至可能已有28万年。虽然没有证据说明古代珠子如何使用，但现代同类珠子经常用于交易。因此看来，我们对珠宝和艺术的喜爱远比原先认为的更加古老。

Q18 · 正确标题未作答

How status can be gained from ownership of goods

如何通过拥有物品获得地位

你的标题

未作答

EBut why did we come to value these objects in the first place? In many animal species, individuals signal their genetic fitness by showing off with attention-grabbing adornment. Some researchers think that in humans, consumer products play a similar role. Archaeologist Aimee Plourde, from the University of California, also argues that even in egalitarian societies, some people are more successful than others. Among our ancestors, superior skills in areas such as hunting, crafts, environmental knowledge and contact with neighbouring groups would have brought respect—in other words, prestige. And because prestige brings social benefits, people would want to show off their talents. The best way to do this would be through demonstrating possession of material items that are hard to fake. ‘A good hunter, for instance, could advertise his skills by wearing the tooth of an animal that is elusive or dangerous,’ says Plourde. The benefits of prestige would also lead to competition to acquire it. As a result, the value and variety of prestige goods would spiral and there would be a parallel increase in the ranking of social systems. If Plourde is correct, prestige goods form a direct link between our innate drive for trade and the development of structured, hierarchical societies. They are arguably the first step on the road to modern civilisation, paving the way for agriculture and urbanisation. We may not be impressed by beads any more, but their modern equivalents have the same fascination. Nobody believes the guy who spends €670,000 on a Bugatti Veyron car does so because he needs to travel at 250 mph. We all know in today’s consumer society he’s buying an exclusive status symbol.

然而，我们最初为什么会开始重视这些物品？在许多动物物种中，个体会用引人注目的装饰炫耀自己，以显示遗传适应性。一些研究人员认为，消费品在人类中发挥着类似作用。加州大学（University of California）的考古学家 Aimee Plourde 还指出，即使在人人平等的社会里，有些人也会比其他人更成功。在我们的祖先中，狩猎、手工技艺、环境知识以及与邻近群体联系等方面更胜一筹的能力，会带来尊重，换言之，就是声望。 由于声望能带来社会利益，人们会想要展示自己的才能。最好的办法，是展示自己拥有难以仿造的实物。Plourde 说：“例如，一名优秀猎人可以佩戴某种难以捕获或危险动物的牙齿，以展示自己的本领。”声望带来的好处还会引发争夺声望的竞争。结果是，声望商品的价值和种类不断攀升，社会体系的等级化程度也同步提高。 如果 Plourde 的观点正确，声望商品就在我们与生俱来的贸易冲动与结构化、等级化社会的发展之间形成了直接联系。可以说，它们是通往现代文明的第一步，为农业和城市化铺平了道路。珠子或许已无法打动我们，但它们的现代对应物仍具有同样的吸引力。没有人会相信，花67万欧元购买 Bugatti Veyron 汽车的人，只是因为需要以每小时250英里的速度出行。我们都知道，在今天的消费社会中，他购买的是一种专属地位象征。
